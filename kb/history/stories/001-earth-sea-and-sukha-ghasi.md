# Earth, sea and Sukha Ghasi

*The origin of Utkal Project’s colours, captured from the founding conversation. This is an editorial account of the documented exchange, not a reconstructed personal memoir.*

Utkal Project’s colours emerged through a conversation about what the project should feel like. The early concept was a warm, literary identity: deep red, ivory, charcoal, teal and ochre, with an open book becoming a boat. It offered a useful visual beginning for a living encyclopedia. It was still a proposal, carrying the earlier name “Utkala”. [POD-HIS-0010]

As the initiative took shape, the website adopted **Utkal Project** and the Founder chose **“Rediscover Utkal. Reimagine Odisha.”** The words connected attention to heritage with the possibility of a different future. They also widened the design question: the identity needed to hold knowledge, people, belonging and opportunity together. [POD-HIS-0007–0009]

The first two revised logo concepts were not the end of the search. Ahimanikya Satapathy asked for more concepts, more colour experiments and, specifically, an **“earthy - heritage feel.”** He also asked to try Odia lettering. The exploration expanded to palm-leaf, woven and threshold ideas, alongside actual Odia typefaces. None of those symbols was treated as a recovered historical emblem, and no final logo had been selected. [POD-HIS-0011–0012]

Then the Founder supplied the names that gave the palette a more personal foundation:

> Kankada Pathara ranga - Nila Sagara and Poda Ghasi

The assistant translated those references into proposed digital colours: an earthy laterite tone, a deep sea blue, and a grass-derived family. When asked to clarify the grass colour, the Founder chose **“Dry straw and muted olive.”** The material names came from him; the digital interpretation and sample layouts came from the assistant. [POD-HIS-0013–0014]

The next decision was to use all three as brand colours. The Founder renamed the grass family **Sukha Ghasi**, connecting it with cow dung and paddy straw used as cooking fuel in Odisha. That association is recorded as he supplied it. The conversation did not describe a particular childhood kitchen, village or incident, and this account does not invent one. [POD-HIS-0015]

The resulting palette has three named families: **Kankada Pathara**, **Nila Sagara** and **Sukha Ghasi**. Sukha Ghasi carries two working tones—muted olive and dry straw—supported by a warm light background and dark reading ink. The colour names retain their origin even if exact digital or print values are adjusted later.

What makes this an important project memory is the movement from a proposed visual style to references supplied by the Founder. The words behind the colours became part of the identity’s meaning. Preserving that exchange gives future contributors the reason for the palette, not just a list of hex codes.

After the palette was agreed, the Founder asked for a continuing ledger, observing that these records would be valuable ten years from now. This story is the first result of that instruction. [POD-HIS-0017]

## Subsequent decisions · captured 28 September 2026 UTC

The Founder made the earthy stone colour dominant, then confirmed its name as **Mankada Pathara — ମାଙ୍କଡ଼ା ପଥର**. The earlier Kankada wording is part of the record, not the current approved name. He returned to the original illustrated book-and-boat board because its elements matched his imagination, and asked to bring Odia lettering into the refinement. [POD-HIS-0018–0020]

He also asked for a material image reference and an internal story for future nostalgia. That story should preserve the actual conversation rather than invent childhood scenes or personal memories. The visual refinement and longer illustrated story remain work in progress under POD-WORK-002. [POD-HIS-0021]

## A correction that brought the material back

When the first Instagram card pictured loose straw, Ahimanikya clarified: “Ghasi is a round dosa size thing they make in the village - cow dunk and peddy straw”. The intended reference is the dried, flattened cooking-fuel cake made from cow dung mixed with paddy straw. The assistant had reduced the material to one ingredient; the revised card restores the whole object. The palette remains muted olive and straw, while the story connects it to the hearth. No particular childhood kitchen or incident is implied. [POD-HIS-0069]
