# ଆଦିମ ବିଚାର (Aadim Vichar): whose understanding of justice?

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

At an IFFI press conference in November 2014, **Atal Bihari Panda** spoke about acting as a search for truth through illusion. The official account introduced him as an octogenarian with nearly seven decades in theatre. Beside the film’s director, Sabyasachi Mohapatra, was a performer carrying a long life on the stage into the conversation about cinema.[^conference]

*ଆଦିମ ବିଚାର (Aadim Vichar)* brings those forms of storytelling together. Its protagonist faces a court, but the film asks viewers to consider a question that extends beyond a verdict: whose understanding of justice is being heard?

## An older man before the court

The official festival account describes a righteous tribal man implicated in a case, whose candid defence surprises the judge. Mohapatra presented the film as an argument for the value of traditional justice.[^conference] This is the director’s artistic argument within a fictional story, rather than a description of all Kandha communities.

The character’s voice, and the authority listening to him, invite viewers into the question. How is a person heard when the language of an institution meets the values by which he has lived?

## From a written story to a shared performance

The Indian Panorama catalogue credits **Kapileswar Prasad Mohapatra** with the story and Sabyasachi with screenplay and direction. Its cast includes **Atal Bihari Panda, Tapaswini Guru and Sushree Smita Panda**.[^catalogue] These names connect the film with the labour of writing and performance, rather than leaving it as a director’s achievement alone.

For Project Odisha’s wider story, this is a concrete link between literature and cinema. The story credit gives readers a writer to follow; the actor’s theatre experience gives another route back towards the stage. Adaptation and performance give this encounter a chosen viewpoint and a particular human presence.

## Selection and award

The film appeared in **Indian Panorama at IFFI 2014**. It subsequently received **Best Odiya Film for 2014** at the 62nd National Film Awards, announced on 24 March 2015.[^catalogue][^award]

The recognition offers another invitation to meet the work: to follow Panda’s journey from theatre and consider the story he helped bring before an audience.
