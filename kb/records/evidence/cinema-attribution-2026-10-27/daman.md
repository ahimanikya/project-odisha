# ଦମନ (DAMaN): the road from Malkangiri to national recognition

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

The story began with another film that needed making. In 2020, Vishal Mourya and Debi Prasad Lenka travelled to Malkangiri for a documentary assignment about malaria prevention. What stayed with them was the work: doctors, ASHA and anganwadi workers, and staff from other departments reaching distant villages. Mourya later recalled that encounter as the inspiration for *ଦମନ (DAMaN)*.[^making]

On **16 August 2024**, *DAMaN* was announced as **Best Odia Film at the 70th National Film Awards**, which honoured films of **2022**. The official record names producer JP Motion Pictures and directors Vishal Mourya and Debi Prasad Lenka.[^award]

For Odia cinema, this was another chapter in a long history of national recognition. For this particular film, it marked the distance travelled by a story rooted in the difficult business of getting care to people.

## The people who brought it together

Producer [Deependra Samal](../../people/deependra-samal.md), co-director [Vishal Mourya](../../people/vishal-mourya.md) and co-director and editor [Debi Prasad Lenka](../../people/debi-prasad-lenka.md) brought different responsibilities to the film. The certification register identifies Samal with JP Motion Pictures; the award catalogue records the directors and Lenka’s editing credit.[^producer-credit][^catalogue]

Their individual stories let us follow the work behind the finished film: backing an idea, developing it together and shaping it for the screen. They sit alongside the actors, technicians, local performers and healthcare workers whose contributions are also part of this account.

## A doctor who has to decide whether to stay

The National Film Awards catalogue places the story in 2015. Its young doctor, Siddharth, arrives in Malkangiri and struggles with the conditions of his posting before committing himself to tackling malaria. Babushaan Mohanty plays the doctor; Dipanwit Dashmohapatra plays the pharmacist who works alongside him.[^catalogue][^trade]

The premise raises a question: what happens when an assignment becomes a responsibility a person can no longer walk away from? The film gives that question a particular location and profession.

Babushaan told *Indulge* that he spoke with doctors while preparing for the role. He also described the Hindi release as fulfilling his wish to take his industry to a national audience. The ambition he expressed was to carry work from Odisha further while continuing to belong to its film industry.[^actor]

## Making the story travel

Mourya said the production drew performers from the Koraput and Jeypore film societies, alongside the lead actors and other local talent. The award catalogue credits Pratap Rout for cinematography and Debi Prasad Lenka for editing.[^making][^catalogue]

*DAMaN* opened in Odia on **4 November 2022**. Its Hindi version reached cinemas on **3 February 2023**. The second release brought the Odia production to viewers in another language.[^actor][^trade]

Contemporary reporting described packed shows during the first week. The New Indian Express recorded an opening in **51 theatres in Odisha and 15 across nine other cities**, including Bengaluru, Mumbai and Delhi.[^reach] The release gave the team’s ambition to reach audiences beyond the state a concrete form.

The reception was not uniform. *Film Information*, reviewing the Hindi version, praised the lead performances but found the screenplay slow and its dramatic appeal limited. That contemporary dissent belongs beside the later national honour. The film was finding attention and different responses at the same time.[^trade]

## The people beyond the central character

A feature film can organise a complex effort around one protagonist. The history behind it needs a wider frame.

That choice also drew criticism. Atri Prasad Rout, writing in *OrissaPOST*, admired the filmmaking but warned that building the story around a single doctor risked making an outsider into a saviour.[^rout] In *Countercurrents*, Pradyumna Behera argued that the film gave too little space to Adivasi agency and the history behind unequal access to healthcare.[^behera] These readings ask whose experience a story brings forward when it follows the person arriving to help.

The real programme, **Durgama Anchalare Malaria Nirakarana**, began implementation in 2017, after the film’s 2015 setting. Its work in remote and high-burden villages depends on repeated camps and screening.[^health][^evaluation] A published camp evaluation found reduced infection odds in its early-camp group, but its non-randomised design could not establish causation; it measured the programme, not the film.[^evaluation]

Health workers interviewed in a 2025 study described infrastructure, staffing and resource constraints, and proposed flexible camp schedules and more support for volunteers. Their accounts, from districts outside Malkangiri, show the organisation of care around the central encounter between a patient and a clinician.[^workers]

A resident’s account brings those access barriers closer. In a 2023 *Down To Earth* report, a Bonda resident of Sanuguda in Malkangiri, identified under the changed name Raja Kirsani, described a walk of more than three hours to reach care because ambulances could not reach his village. His testimony concerns healthcare access, not a response to *DAMaN*.[^resident]

A cinema story and a programme history can illuminate each other while answering different questions. The fictional doctor gives the film a central journey; the wider record makes room for the repeated work of teams and volunteers.

## Returning to the film

The production brought performers and film-society networks together; its release found audiences within and beyond Odisha. Watching it also leaves room to ask how the doctor’s journey makes the people around him visible. Recognition and thoughtful disagreement can both keep a film in conversation. The accounts of workers and residents help that conversation attend to lives beyond the screen.
