# Debi Prasad Lenka: shaping the story, from script to edit

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Debi Prasad Lenka appears in *ଦମନ (DAMaN)*’s credits in two closely connected roles: co-director and editor.[^catalogue] One involves helping decide what the camera will record; the other involves shaping those recordings into the film an audience sees. Following both roles gives us a fuller way of looking at his work.

Lenka is from Cuttack. His public professional biography describes a three-year filmmaking course followed by work as an assistant director and editor in Odisha and Mumbai.[^background] A 2022 *Sunday Post* profile identifies him as an alumnus of the Biju Patnaik Film and Television Institute of Odisha.[^training] His route into feature films therefore connects local training with the wider working world of television and production.

## A career beyond the feature-film poster

The National Film Awards catalogue describes Lenka’s work across corporate films, documentaries and government productions, including a television commercial for the 2018 Men’s Hockey World Cup.[^catalogue] These credits matter because a filmmaker’s working life often includes projects that viewers may never associate with the person behind them.

Mentis Films identifies him as a filmmaker, director, editor and founder. Its profile connects him with *B for Bundelkhand*, *ବିଜୁ ବାବୁ (Biju Babu)* and *DAMaN*.[^mentis] The range suggests a useful way to approach his public career: follow the work across formats, rather than begin and end with one award.

## A partnership formed at work

Vishal Mourya recalls meeting Lenka, then an editor, on a Zoom television project in 2010.[^partnership] They later made films together. NFDC’s 2017 Film Bazaar catalogue lists both as writers and directors of *B for Bundelkhand*, a Hindi film about a farming family under pressure.[^bundelkhand]

The partnership carried into Odia cinema. *Biju Babu* preceded *DAMaN*, and the latter brought Lenka’s editing work into the same project as his shared direction.[^mentis][^catalogue] The two directors’ careers overlap, but an individual profile makes room to notice the skills each had developed before they met.

## Preparing the story before the shoot

In the directors’ interview with *The Federal*, Lenka described detailed scripting and a fifteen-day actors’ workshop. The workshop also helped them revise dialogue and translate the passages spoken by tribal characters.[^workshop] It is an account of preparation in which language and performance were part of the filmmaking work, not details left until the end.

That account invites readers to look beyond a film’s headline recognition. How does a line sound when spoken aloud? What changes when an actor tries it? How does an editor hold the resulting scenes together? These are questions through which a viewer can return to *DAMaN* with closer attention.

## Recognition, and work still to explore

*DAMaN* won Best Odia Film at the 70th National Film Awards, announced in 2024 for films of 2022. The official result credits directors Lenka and Mourya alongside producer JP Motion Pictures.[^award]

Lenka’s public career offers a connection between training, paid production work, collaboration and a nationally recognised feature. There is room to explore more of his shorter films and his approach to editing as sources become available. For now, his work gives readers a reason to stay with the credits: the story continues there, in the names of the people who shaped what we watched.
