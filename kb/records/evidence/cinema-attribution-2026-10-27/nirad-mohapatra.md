# Nirad Mohapatra: making a film, making room for others

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

A filmmaker can leave behind more than the films he directs. There are also the screenings someone organised, the discussions that followed, and the opportunities to learn how an image works. **Nirad Mohapatra’s** career gives those activities a place beside *ମାୟା ମିରିଗ (Maya Miriga)*.

His memorial biography records a move from postgraduate political-science study to film training at FTII, followed by teaching there in 1972–74. It also records **Cinexstasy**, the Bhubaneswar film society he ran between 1974 and 1983, where world-cinema screenings were accompanied by professional analysis.[^life]

## A place to encounter cinema

The same account connects his film-appreciation courses at Utkal University with his work on the film section of the literary journal *Mana Phasal*.[^life] A journal page, a university course and a film-society screening offered different ways to encounter cinema. Editing brought it into a literary publication; teaching made time for sustained attention; a screening allowed viewers to meet a work together.

There is a practical idea here for readers considering how a film culture grows. Watching together and discussing a film can be organised work. It takes people, time and a place to meet. Mohapatra’s work gives these commitments names and places.

## A household at the centre

The extended family at the centre of *ମାୟା ମିରିଗ (Maya Miriga)* provides the subject of our [companion film article](../culture/cinema-series/maya-miriga.md), which follows its collaborators and later restoration. Here, the film sits alongside the director’s work in teaching and organising screenings.

## Recognition, and the work after recognition

The film received **Second Best Feature Film** at the 31st National Film Awards, covering films of 1983 and presented in 1984. Cannes Critics’ Week records its **1984 selection**. The award and the festival selection mark distinct forms of recognition.[^national][^cannes]

## What can be carried forward?

Mohapatra’s story belongs in a discussion of industry initiative because teaching, organising and preserving are activities through which people can make room for others. His career lets us recognise this work alongside the more visible act of directing.

His example leaves a useful question for the present: alongside making a film, what can a team help make possible for the next filmmaker or viewer? A screening, a careful discussion, a training opportunity or a usable preservation copy may each form part of an answer.
