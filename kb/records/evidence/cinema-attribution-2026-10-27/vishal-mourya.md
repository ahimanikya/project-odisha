# Vishal Mourya: finding a filmmaking language together

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Vishal Mourya’s route into Odia cinema began outside Odisha. In an interview with Anish Mohanty, he said he was from Gwalior and had studied engineering before working as a software developer in Pune. He left that work, took a filmmaking course and moved to Mumbai. Television brought him to a collaborator: while working on a Zoom programme in 2010, he met editor Debi Prasad Lenka.[^journey]

Their partnership gives this story its human connection. Different professional beginnings could meet in a shared practice, and an Odia film could become the place where that practice found a wider audience.

## Learning to work as two

Mourya recalled a period of financial difficulty and a YouTube channel whose earnings helped them make *B for Bundelkhand*. By 2017, the Hindi film appeared in NFDC’s Film Bazaar Viewing Room catalogue, with both men credited as writers and directors, and as producers alongside Vivek Maurya.[^journey][^bundelkhand]

The catalogue describes a farmer and his son facing drought and a strained relationship. It records a film seeking distribution, rather than an award or a confirmed theatrical release. That distinction matters when tracing a career: making a film, finding a market and reaching a paying audience are different stages.

Their later Odia collaboration, *ବିଜୁ ବାବୁ (Biju Babu)*, was released in 2019. Mentis Films’ own profile identifies Mourya as a director, writer and founder, and places this film alongside *DAMaN* and *B for Bundelkhand*.[^mentis]

## Returning to something they had witnessed

In 2020, Mourya and Lenka travelled to Malkangiri for a documentary about malaria prevention. Mourya later described how the work of health staff and other public-service workers suggested a feature film. Producer Deependra Samal’s encouragement helped them pursue that possibility.[^making]

The resulting *ଦମନ (DAMaN)* was rooted in a specific setting, but its making involved many people. Mourya’s account names performers drawn from the Koraput and Jeypore film societies as well as local talent. The production therefore connects a director’s career with the less visible networks that help a regional film take shape.[^making]

## Discussion before the camera turns

Mourya described a partnership in which either director could challenge an idea. They discussed shots together and worked through roughly ten drafts of *DAMaN* before filming.[^journey] It is a useful detail to place beside the finished film: the public result followed repeated decisions about what the story should become.

*DAMaN* received Best Odia Film at the 70th National Film Awards, announced in 2024 for films of 2022. The official entry names both directors and producer JP Motion Pictures.[^award] That recognition offers a reason to look closely at the work that preceded it, and at how filmmaking skills can travel between professions, places and languages.

Mourya’s story leaves a practical question for the next generation: where can people with different training meet, learn from each other and find the support to make an original film? The collaboration behind *DAMaN* is one place to begin that discussion.
