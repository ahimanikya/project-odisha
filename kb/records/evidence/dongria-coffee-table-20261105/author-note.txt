A shawl, and the questions it opened

This book began at a dinner table.

At Sabyasachi Supper Club, I noticed the cloth used for the table mats and hand towels. My host also had shawls for sale, and I bought one. I fell in love with it. Perhaps I had seen something like it before, but I had never paused to understand where it came from or whose work I was looking at.

I was already working on Project Odisha, exploring the places, culture and literature of Odisha. This encounter gave that work a personal direction. I wanted to learn about the people behind the cloth, the place they called home and the lives around its making.

The reading took me far beyond the shawl. Information was scattered across reports, studies and individual accounts. Over the following weeks, I read and brought these stories together, trying to keep people at the centre: those who grow food, keep seeds, embroider, teach, learn and sing.

My delight in the designs found other forms, too. I made a bag inspired by them and had ten T-shirts printed. Those were my own adaptations. As my curiosity grew, I wanted to understand more of the lives and work from which that inspiration came.

I felt embarrassed by how little I had known about this part of Odisha. That feeling soon became a reason to keep learning. What had first caught my eye as a beautiful garment had opened questions about skill, livelihood, land, memory and belonging.

I have not yet visited Niyamgiri. This first book grows from weeks of reading and research; the visit, and the listening I hope to do there, still lie ahead. There is much I do not yet know.

I hope these pages bring you insight and some of the joy this discovery has brought me. I hope, too, that when you look at a cloth, you pause a little longer with the people whose work gives it life.

For me, this is a beginning.

Ahimanikya Satapathy
