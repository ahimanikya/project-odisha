# Niyamgiri: mining decisions, community rights and a sacred landscape

**Twelve village assemblies, a documented place in Odisha’s mining decisions.** A May 2015 parliamentary answer records that Gram Sabhas in 12 villages—seven in Rayagada and five in Kalahandi—decided against mining at Niyamgiri. This is a count of the village decisions reported by the minister, not a count of voters or every community with rights in the hills. [Rajya Sabha, Question 1978(b), printed p.237](https://cms.rajyasabha.nic.in/UploadedFiles/Debates/OfficialDebatesDatewise/Floor/235/F13.05.2015.pdf).

## What the court asked the Gram Sabhas to decide

The Supreme Court’s 18 April 2013 judgment directed consideration of individual, community, cultural and religious rights, including worship associated with Niyam-Raja. It required a district-judge-rank observer and a later Ministry decision on Stage II forest clearance informed by the Gram Sabha decisions. These were distinct steps. The judgment was not itself a record of the later village meetings. [Paragraphs 55–63, printed pp.946–948](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/documents/supremecourtreport/2013_v6_piv.pdf).

| Evidence stage | What is documented | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed forest diversion | 660.749 hectares in the challenged proposal | Actual clearing, restored area or the whole Niyamgiri landscape |
| Court directions, 18 April 2013 | Rights determination and subsequent clearance decision | A universal current approval checklist |
| Parliamentary answer, 13 May 2015 | 12 village decisions against mining; 7 Rayagada, 5 Kalahandi | Voter totals, individual meeting dates or all affected villages |

The case record preserves opposing positions. OMC, the state and Sterlite challenged the refusal; the Union defended its review of clearance conditions and forest rights. Those submissions are attributed positions, not interchangeable with the court’s findings. [Paragraphs 16–22](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s3ec0490f1f4972d133619a60c30f3559e/documents/supremecourtreport/2013_v6_piv.pdf).

## Connect the landscape without merging different projects

The record connects mining with community rights and spirituality. Forest-rights determination does not itself measure household income, forest-product sales or ecological recovery. The proposed Niyamgiri mine and Lanjigarh refinery must remain distinct; neither is Baphlimali or Utkal Alumina. The rights inquiry also should not be collapsed into the separate question of ownership of minerals, which the judgment addresses in paragraph 50.
