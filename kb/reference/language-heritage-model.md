# Languages and Living Voices

Direction and concept: **Ahimanikya Satapathy**, Founder & Editor-in-Chief. Prepared with AI assistance. Founder requested language coverage with Odia primary and other languages explored through their cultural heritage and history. The structure below is a candidate implementation of that direction, not a set of completed language histories.

## Purpose and audience

Make language a core subject of the encyclopedia: a way into people's memory, literature, humour, work and everyday life. Odia — **ଓଡ଼ିଆ** — is the primary editorial focus. Other languages and regional varieties receive their own identities and substantive coverage. This editorial priority does not rank communities or establish a new website interface language setting.

Serve residents discovering neighbouring traditions, diaspora families reconnecting with speech and memory, students researching language history, and visitors learning to listen and participate respectfully. A successful profile helps someone hear a voice, understand its context and follow a connection to a person, place or work.

## Information and page hierarchy

Proposed website path: **Explore → Languages and Living Voices → language or variety → related places, people and works**. Each language is a first-class entity in the [Knowledge & Story Model](knowledge-story-model.md), rather than a paragraph repeated across destination pages.

| Profile section | What we collect and narrate |
| --- | --- |
| Meet the language | Community-preferred names, self-names, pronunciation, alternate names and the scope of this profile. |
| Where it lives | Places, communities, multilingual households and diaspora connections, each with a source and period. Maps show overlap; districts are not exclusive language boundaries. |
| Its journey | Dated evidence of speech and writing, manuscripts, inscriptions, language contact, education, publishing and change. Origin stories are identified as stories; first surviving evidence is not a language's birth date. |
| How it sounds and is written | Speaker recordings, scripts and their histories, regional forms, transliteration and accessible transcripts. Language and script have separate identities. |
| Stories carried by voice | Folktales, proverbs, riddles, lullabies, work songs, performance and oral histories, including teller, locality and version. Written literature is not a prerequisite for a rich profile. |
| Literature, song and screen | Poets, writers, books, lyrics, theatre and films; original title, creator, date, language and precise place connection. Verified excerpts and separate translation credits. |
| Words woven into life | Food, farming, fishing, crafts, seasons, festivals, ecological knowledge and expressions of affection or humour, grounded in particular speakers and contexts. |
| People keeping it alive | Contemporary writers, teachers, performers, translators and community initiatives, alongside historical figures. |
| Learn and connect | A few reviewed greetings or everyday phrases, listening resources, learning materials and public invitations to participate. State local usage and politeness context. |
| Life today and tomorrow | Use across generations, education, media and digital life. Any speaker count or vitality assessment includes source, year, geography and method. |

Suggested reading order: a short original opening → hear a consenting speaker → see where the language lives → discover its history → meet a poem, song or story → learn a phrase → explore connected places. Use real, relevant images of manuscripts, performances and everyday settings with rights and contextual captions. No autoplay audio. Full references and credits belong in an expandable end section; essential context remains beside the material.

## First research queue

This is an editorial queue, **not an exhaustive language census, settled classification or ready-to-publish list**. Source labels and self-identification may differ.

| Stage | Research subjects | Initial evidence and next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First full profile | Odia | CIIL's catalogue records a 2019 introduction to Odia language, literature and culture. Inspect the book and other primary works; build a sourced chronology and select reviewed literature/audio. |
| First comparative profiles | Santali; Kui; Kuvi; Saora | SCSTRTI lists separate learning booklets for each. Read their contents, resolve preferred names and scripts with speakers, and develop separate histories. Do not merge Kui and Kuvi. |
| Regional speech and identity | Sambalpuri/Kosali; Desia | Research naming and classification as attributed positions. SCSTRTI uses both Desia and Bhatri-Desia labels on its catalogue page; their relationship needs investigation. Sambalpuri/Kosali naming and equivalence remain an explicit research question here. |
| Extend coverage | Ho, Juang, Koya, Gondi, Kisan, Sadri and further entries in SCSTRTI's catalogue | A resource label is a discovery lead, not proof of a one-to-one language/community identity. Resolve labels such as Munda, Oraon, Gadaba and Bonda before creating canonical aliases or merging records. |
| Wider linguistic life | Research Bengali, Telugu, Urdu, Hindi and other languages in Odisha's towns, border areas and communities | Use dated census tables plus local scholarship and speaker accounts. Explore multilingualism and migration without assuming a person's origin from the language they speak. |

Odia's pilot should cover early evidence and script development, major literary periods, print and public life, regional expression, songs and cinema, everyday speech and contemporary creators. These are research headings; specific dates, quotations, works and associations need evidence before publication.

## Editorial and data rules

- Preserve a community's self-name alongside names used by cited sources. Record disputed classifications with their author and date. Do not automatically classify every regional voice as a dialect of Odia or treat every community label as a language.
- Keep editorial priority, official recognition, linguistic classification, speaker identity and script distinct. Language is not proof of ethnicity, religion, ancestry or historical influence.
- Record mother-tongue counts separately from all-speaker estimates. State the census year and geography; never present 2011 counts as today's population. Do not infer endangerment from a small count alone.
- Keep original text, transliteration and translation separate. Record translator and native-language review. Do not invent proverbs, reconstruct quotations from memory or present synthetic speech as a community recording.
- For audio, record speaker's chosen public credit, variety/locality, recording date, transcript, translation, permission scope and reuse rights. Keep private contact details and consent documents outside the public KB; store only a safe reference. Community restrictions on sacred or private knowledge must be respected.
- Link poems and songs to their creators, language and places using supported relationships. A song's language does not establish where it was composed, filmed or recorded.
- Source pages are research material; availability online does not grant permission to reproduce complete books, poems, songs or recordings.

## Delivery and acceptance

1. Fill the [language template](../models/knowledge-story-v0.1/language.template.json) for Odia alongside an initial set of other language profiles; completion of Odia is not a prerequisite for their research. Preserve gaps instead of filling every section with generic prose.
2. Obtain native-speaker review of names, original text, pronunciation and translations; verify historical and cultural claims against suitable sources. No reviewer has been appointed by this document.
3. Build the language hub and illustrated/audio-enabled profiles. A place page can show local voices and courtesies; a work page can link its language; a future tour book can include relevant reviewed phrases.
4. Publish only the reviewed candidate approved by the Founder. Add more languages progressively. Interface translation and a multilingual AI assistant remain separate implementation work.

Minimum publication readiness: supported identity and scope, inspectable history and cultural evidence, precise place/work links, an honest statement of gaps, checked rights for every displayed asset, appropriate language review and a recorded human publication decision. An absent audio recording does not block an otherwise useful profile; show no pretend recording or empty player.

## Founder clarification: languages and literary lives

The public hub is proposed as **Languages of Odisha**, with each language receiving substantive coverage. **Odia Literature & Literary Lives** is a dedicated linked collection of writers, works and their cultural influence. See the [literature collection plan and initial personality queue](../research/culture/odia-literature-and-literary-lives.md). This extends the existing domain rather than treating other languages as a translation feature or collapsing their literature into Odia.
