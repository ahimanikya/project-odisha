## India and international framing

For Indian audiences, test weekend discovery, regional food and meaningful purchases as entry points. For global audiences, test making processes, named landscapes and explained cultural context. Avoid treating either audience as uniform: language, motivation and familiarity should guide later testing.

## Copy discipline

Use a strong supported detail instead of an unverified superlative. “More to discover” is an invitation; “never discovered” erases the people who know a place. A growth story needs a period and measure. A maker story needs a name and credit. A travel story needs current practical information before it becomes a recommendation.

## Presenting numbers

Follow the [statistics presentation standard](statistics-storytelling.md): one clear takeaway, one main number, a useful comparison and a simple visual. Explain what the measure means. Keep periods, units and material qualifications visible, with deeper evidence on demand.
