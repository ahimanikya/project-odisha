# Odisha community history: working coverage index

Paika begins this series with a question about service, land and community identity. The second published study is Khandayat: how the name appears in historical records, how its meanings vary by place and period, and how community histories relate to military service without making Paika and Khandayat interchangeable.

This is a research map, not a complete list of Odisha castes. Its [machine-readable inventory](coverage-index.json) separates source observations from editorial topics. An administrative row can contain several names, regional restrictions, substitutions or deleted entries. It cannot by itself establish one community, shared ancestry, an alias, or present legal eligibility.

The combined inventory currently has 226 observations and six source records, including the department landing page. These are provenance counts, not population or community counts. All 216 SEBC serials are represented, including deleted entries at 68 and 75. Row 138 spans two pages and includes a deletion affecting a name within the row; its label must always be read with the preserved qualifiers. Pages 13 and 14 were visually inspected. Other rows retain extracted text and page associations but have not all been manually proofread.

## How coverage grows

For each article, identify the community's own names and regional branches using specific evidence. Preserve original source labels alongside any proposed editorial name. Treat occupational titles, caste identities, tribal identities and administrative categories separately; neither punctuation nor spelling similarity proves equivalence. Investigate dated evidence, origin traditions, inherited work, social change and local variation without assuming a community was deliberately created for one purpose.

Odia heritage is the central concern. Customs should be described through evidence of place, community, period and practice. Research should distinguish documented local traditions, shared regional customs and recent adoption, including concerns about displacement of Odia practices. An administrative list cannot settle cultural authenticity.

New evidence can add observations without forcing a new canonical topic. Alias or equivalence proposals need an explicit source-supported decision and separate review. The validator checks references and declared evidence flags; it cannot decide whether an equivalence is historically true. Community self-identification and field questions remain open where evidence is missing.

Coverage planning and index maintenance receive zero substantive credit toward the 500-item programme. This internal index does not change website publication, search eligibility or legal guidance.
