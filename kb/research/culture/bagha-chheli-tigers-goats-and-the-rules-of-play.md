Bagha Chheli gives its two players different jobs. One controls tigers that capture; the other controls goats that try to leave the tigers unable to move.[^1]

## A gap can matter

The Ancient Indian Boardgames archive records a version with four tigers and twenty goats. Four goats begin on the board; the rest are added individually before goats may move. A tiger captures by jumping over a goat into an empty point beyond. For the goats, numbers alone do not settle the game: the task is to close the tigers’ routes.[^1]

The pieces need not resemble animals. In her August 2020 account for *Mycitylinks*, Sameedha Mohanty describes larger stones for the tigers and smaller ones for the goats.[^2] Size makes the two sides distinguishable. A stone becomes a tiger through its place in the game and the moves allowed to it.

## Agreeing on the finish

The archive’s modern Rules field gives the tigers victory after capturing every goat. Mohanty’s account instead ends their task at five captures.[^1][^2] These are different winning conditions, and players should agree which one they are using before they begin. Knowing the name of a game does not by itself settle every rule.

Photographer Subhashish Panigrahi’s record places a game of Bagha Chheli in a Bengaluru home on 29 July 2014. His description identifies two players taking part.[^3]

For anyone returning to the board, a useful opening question is simple: how did the people you played with decide when the game was won?
