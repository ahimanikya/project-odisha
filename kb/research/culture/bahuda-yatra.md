title: "Bahuda Yatra: What the Deities Eat on the Way Home"
description: "From sal-leaf parcels in Bargarh to a stop for pitha on Puri’s Grand Road, Odisha’s return chariot journeys keep time with the people who cook for them."
---

In Gopalpur in Bargarh district, Kabi Satpathy’s family begins work on Navami, while Dadhibaman is still away from his temple in Bhatli. The next day is Bahuda, the return journey, and the family has a cake to deliver. In 2025, sixteen cooks prepared 600 kilograms of *poda pitha* for the occasion. They wrapped the mixture of wheat flour, jaggery, ghee and spices in sal leaves and baked it under embers. Satpathy told *The New Indian Express* that his family had made the offering for eight generations. For the people tending the fire, bringing the deity home is already a day’s work before anyone touches a rope. <sup id="ref-1-1"><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Footnote 1">1</a></sup>

At [Ratha Yatra](/stories/ratha-yatra/), devotees draw the deities away from their own shrine for a visit. Bahuda Yatra (ବାହୁଡ଼ା ଯାତ୍ରା) is the journey back. In Puri it falls on Dashami, the tenth day of the bright fortnight of Ashadha. The siblings leave Gundicha temple and travel south along the Grand Road towards the Lion Gate of the Srimandir. Balabhadra goes first, then Subhadra, then Jagannath. A person who wants to feed them has to know where the chariots will pause. <sup id="ref-2-1"><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Footnote 2">2</a></sup> <sup id="ref-3-1"><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Footnote 3">3</a></sup>

## The aunt’s kitchen on the Grand Road

The Mausi Maa temple is a stop along that road. Gundicha is where the siblings have been staying. Mausi Maa, honoured as their aunt, has something ready for them on the way back. Bishwanath Mishra, a priest of her temple, explained to *OrissaPOST* how the shrine makes its *poda pitha*. The cooks mix flour, fresh cheese, sugar, cloves and cardamom. They steam the dough for hours, cut it into pieces and fry the slices in ghee. In Puri and Bhatli, both preparations carry the same name. In one kitchen there is a pan of ghee for the last stage. In the other, a sal leaf has been holding the cake together under the embers. <sup id="ref-4-1"><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Footnote 4">4</a></sup> <sup id="ref-1-2"><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Footnote 1">1</a></sup>

Mishra described the pitha being offered to the siblings on their respective chariots and then shared with devotees. There are other gifts along the Grand Road. In his account of the temple’s rites, Bhaskar Mishra records temples, institutions and servitors bringing dry foods as *pankti bhoga*. After the Mausi Maa stop, the chariots of Balabhadra and Subhadra go ahead to the Lion Gate. Jagannath’s Nandighosa pauses at the royal palace. Lakshmi comes in a palanquin to meet him, and the Gajapati brings the two deities together. She receives Jagannath’s garland before his chariot travels the last stretch. <sup id="ref-4-2"><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Footnote 4">4</a></sup> <sup id="ref-3-2"><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Footnote 3">3</a></sup>

## A meal before leaving, a halt halfway home

At Subarnapur in western Odisha, the first question on Bahuda is what the visitors will eat **before they leave**. In a 2005 account of the town’s chariot festival, Pabitra Mohan Nayak names the Keots of Lankabahal as the people who offered *upan bhog* to the deities on Dashami before their departure from the Gundicha Mandap. He preserves the local name of the offering and the community that brought it. <sup id="ref-5-1"><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Footnote 5">5</a></sup>

What counts as the last stretch of road changes from town to town. In Baripada, Jagannath’s chariot leads on the way home. Subhadra follows, then Balabhadra. The return pull takes two days. In July 2022 Subhadra’s chariot stood overnight in front of the town police station. Women picked up the ropes there on Monday and drew her the rest of the way to Haribaldev Jew temple. Readers who met these women on the outward journey will recognise the landmark. This time the destination was home. <sup id="ref-6-1"><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Footnote 6">6</a></sup> <sup id="ref-7-1"><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Footnote 7">7</a></sup>

In Buguda in Ganjam district, the Baladev Jew temple’s festival brings two neighbouring shrines into the story. While the deities are staying at Gundicha, the Biranchi Narayan Sun temple sends a sweet offering called *mistanna bhoga*. Devotees there receive a share. The local account also names the *poda pitha* at Mausi Maa’s shrine, made with rice and gram flour and sweetened with molasses. Remembering who sends which food lets a family tell the visit and the journey home apart. <sup id="ref-8-1"><a href="#source-8" aria-label="Footnote 8">8</a></sup>

## When the wheels stop

In Puri, reaching the Lion Gate still leaves several rites to come. The deities appear on the chariots in gold ornaments for *Suna Besha*. The drink called *Adhara Pana* belongs to the days after the return, while the chariots stand outside. Then comes *Niladri Bije*, the entry into the temple, when rasagola figures in Jagannath’s reconciliation with Lakshmi. The aunt’s pitha on the road and the sweet for Lakshmi at home answer to different occasions. <sup id="ref-3-3"><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Footnote 3">3</a></sup>

Jarada in Ganjam has its own ending. Prafulla Chandra Mohanty describes Dadhibaman dressed as Rama and Sarathigosain as Lakshmana for Bahuda. They regain their temple throne that same day. After the ritual exchange of words called *Bachanika*, Mahalaxmi receives rasagola. A family in Jarada can see the return and the welcome indoors in a single day. <sup id="ref-9-1"><a href="#source-9" aria-label="Footnote 9">9</a></sup>

In [The Year That Feeds Us](/stories/the-year-that-feeds-us/), the people at the ropes are easy to see. Bahuda also gives us the people who measure the journey from a kitchen. In Gopalpur, the day to start cooking is written into the festival calendar. If the family waited to see Dadhibaman coming home, it would already be too late to light the fire. <sup id="ref-1-3"><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Footnote 1">1</a></sup>
