title: "Chandan Yatra: When the Deities Take to the Water"
description: "Three neighbourhoods share a boat festival in Ganjam. In Puri, servitors prepare a bath in the middle of a tank. Across Odisha, summer changes how communities care for their deities."
---

In Ganjam town, three neighbourhoods shared the work of taking Madan Mohan Gopala Swami out on the water. The opening procession in April 2026 set off from Samantara Sahi. Karana Sahi and Panda Sahi joined it in organising the festival. Reporting for *Dharitri*, Bidyadhar Sahu described what happened at night. The deity was anointed with sandalwood and seated in a decorated boat. In those early evenings of the festival, conches and *kahali* horns sounded as the boat made three circuits. A bath in the *chandan kunda*, the basin set aside for it, followed the ride. <sup id="ref-1-1"><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Footnote 1">1</a></sup>

Chandan Yatra (ଚନ୍ଦନ ଯାତ୍ରା) takes its name from that fragrant sandalwood. Its place in [The Year That Feeds Us](/stories/the-year-that-feeds-us/) comes into focus beside [Baisakha Brata](/stories/baisakha-brata/). Above a courtyard tulasi, a family hangs a clay pitcher and keeps it filled through the hot days. At a temple, the same season can call for a palanquin, a boat crew and a bath prepared for a deity. Baisakha Brata follows the lunar month. In Puri, Chandan Yatra opens on one particular day of it, Akshaya Tritiya, the third day of the bright fortnight. <sup id="ref-2-1"><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Footnote 2">2</a></sup> <sup id="ref-3-1"><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Footnote 3">3</a></sup>

## A bath in the middle of Narendra tank

A visitor waiting on the bank at Puri sees deities who can travel. Madan Mohan represents Jagannath, and Rama Krishna represents Balabhadra. Processional images from five Shiva temples join them. Together those five are known as the *Pancha Pandava*, after the five brothers of the Mahabharata. Servitors bear the images in palanquins to Narendra tank. On this same day, work begins on the chariots for Ratha Yatra. A farming family marking [Akhi Muthi Anukula](/stories/akhi-muthi-anukula/) has a different task for the occasion, carrying the first handful of paddy seed to the field. <sup id="ref-3-2"><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Footnote 3">3</a></sup> <sup id="ref-4-1"><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Footnote 4">4</a></sup>

The boats need attention long before the procession reaches the bank. On the full moon of Chaitra, Bhoi servitors lift the five hulls that have been kept submerged in Narendra tank. Four are joined in pairs to form the two ceremonial craft, Nanda and Bhadra. By the time the images arrive for *chapa khela*, their outing on the water, the boats are decorated and ready to receive them. <sup id="ref-5-1"><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Footnote 5">5</a></sup>

After a circuit of the tank, the images are carried to the *Chandana Ghara*, the small shrine in the water. There the bath has been prepared in stone tubs with water, sandalwood paste, scents and flowers. The deities are dressed again and offered food. There is a second outing by boat before the return to the temple. This outdoor part of Puri’s festival, *Bahara Chandana*, lasts twenty-one days. Another twenty-one, *Bhitara Chandana*, belong to the rites inside the temple. There the water for play is held in vessels on a special platform. <sup id="ref-3-3"><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Footnote 3">3</a></sup> <sup id="ref-4-2"><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Footnote 4">4</a></sup>

## Other waters, other arrangements

In Bhubaneswar, Lingaraj’s procession goes to Bindusagar. The temple’s arrangements for 2023 show how the outing fitted around the deity’s day. The palanquins were to be ready before the afternoon offering, *Dwipahara Dhoopa*. Once it was over, the deities could leave for the tank. At Kendrapara, Baladevjew has a Chandan pond and a twenty-one-day observance of his own. The district also records Chandan Yatra for Baraha Jew at Aul and Dadhibaman Jew at Demal. A family in one of those places can mark the festival at its own deity’s shrine. <sup id="ref-6-1"><a href="#source-6" aria-label="Footnote 6">6</a></sup> <sup id="ref-7-1"><a href="#source-7" aria-label="Footnote 7">7</a></sup>

Near the Mahanadi in Sambalpur, people once made a floating stage by joining country boats on Chandan Bandha, the pond at the Somnath temple in Balibandha. In 2015, *The New Indian Express* traced the interruption of that custom to the Mahanadi flood of 1982. The pond became swampy, and the yatra there stopped in 1983. The report also remembered a different site in the same city, the tank outside the Radha Madhab temple in Sakhipada, where decorated boats had carried representative images of Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra. <sup id="ref-8-1"><a href="#source-8" aria-label="Footnote 8">8</a></sup>

In Ganjam, the April account tells us what waited on shore after the boat came in. Once Gopala Swami had bathed, there was a change of dress, a place for him on his throne, lamplight and a food offering. For the people of those three neighbourhoods, getting the deity afloat was only half the evening’s work. They also had to receive him home. <sup id="ref-1-2"><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Footnote 1">1</a></sup>
