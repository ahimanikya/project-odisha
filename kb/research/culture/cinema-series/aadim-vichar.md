# ଆଦିମ ବିଚାର (Aadim Vichar): whose understanding of justice?

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

At an IFFI press conference in November 2014, **Atal Bihari Panda** spoke about acting as a search for truth through illusion. The official account introduced him as an octogenarian with nearly seven decades in theatre. Beside the film’s director, Sabyasachi Mohapatra, was a performer carrying a long life on the stage into the conversation about cinema.[^conference]

In a 2013 interview, Panda recalled theatre around trees, without a raised platform. Spectators preferred to sit in a circle rather than have a separate stage.[^atal-theatre]

*ଆଦିମ ବିଚାର (Aadim Vichar)* brings those forms of storytelling together. Its protagonist faces a court, but the film asks viewers to consider a question that extends beyond a verdict: whose understanding of justice is being heard?

## An older man before the court

The official festival account describes a righteous tribal man implicated in a case, whose candid defence surprises the judge. Mohapatra presented the film as an argument for the value of traditional justice.[^conference] This is the director’s artistic argument within a fictional story, rather than a description of all Kandha communities.

The character’s voice, and the authority listening to him, invite viewers into the question. How is a person heard when the language of an institution meets the values by which he has lived?

## From a written story to a shared performance

The Indian Panorama catalogue credits **Kapileswar Prasad Mohapatra** with the story and Sabyasachi with screenplay and direction. Its cast includes **Atal Bihari Panda, Tapaswini Guru and Sushree Smita Panda**.[^catalogue]

Speaking to Ashok Palit about the planned sequel to *Sala Budha*, Sabyasachi identified its source as his father’s short story *Adim Bichar*.[^source-story]

Panda recalled Kapileswar as his classmate, and Sabyasachi as a young man who had played a small part in his play *Phata Mardala*. Before *Aadim Vichar*, Sabyasachi asked him to help adapt his father’s stories for *ଶଳାବୁଢ଼ା (Sala Budha)*, then invited him to play its lead. For *Aadim Vichar*, Panda remembered winter filming in Kandhamal and the research and practice he undertook for the demanding role.[^panda-recollection]

In a 2019 interview, Sabyasachi described his usual way of working with family collaborators: he would tell the story to his brothers, nephews and nieces, then hear their individual views. Debate could bring useful suggestions. He welcomed those contributions while keeping the final decision with himself.[^family-practice]

## Selection and award

The film appeared in **Indian Panorama at IFFI 2014**. It subsequently received **Best Odiya Film for 2014** at the 62nd National Film Awards, announced on 24 March 2015.[^catalogue][^award]

Return from the award to the older performer at the press conference: Panda brought decades of theatre to the role. The court in the story gives that voice an authority to confront; the performance gives us a person to listen to.
