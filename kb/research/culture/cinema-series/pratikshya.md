# ପ୍ରତୀକ୍ଷା (Pratikshya): a family waiting for security

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

A secure job can become more than employment. It can stand for a family’s hopes, the ability to repay a loan and relief from being dependent on someone else. **ପ୍ରତୀକ୍ଷା (Pratikshya)** places that desire beside a father’s illness, until the son’s hope for security becomes entangled with the possibility of loss.

The Film Bazaar viewing-room synopsis locates the story in a middle-class Bhubaneswar household. Sanjay wants a government job; his father Bipin approaches retirement while the family carries wedding debt. Illness changes the meaning of their waiting.[^bazaar]

## A writer, a director and a leading actor

The official IFFI account identifies a short story by **Gourahari Das** as the film’s inspiration. **Anupam Patnaik** directed it; **Dipanwit Dashmohapatra**, playing Sanjay, described this as his first lead role.[^iffi] These connections make the film a useful meeting point between contemporary Odia writing and performance.

Our [Gourahari Das profile](../../people/gourahari-das.md) follows his fiction and translations beyond this adaptation. Keeping that wider work in view matters: a film may introduce a reader to a writer, but it does not replace the writer’s books.

## The pressure inside a household

The Film Bazaar synopsis describes Sanjay learning of compassionate appointment after an employee’s death. The prospect distorts his hopes for his father.[^bazaar] This is the dramatic situation presented by the film, not guidance on employment entitlements.

The discomfort of that premise gives viewers room to think about the relationship between affection and economic pressure. A household can contain care, resentment and dependence at the same time. The question is not whether every family behaves like this one, but what this particular conflict lets us examine.

## A film’s development and its recognition

The viewing-room entry credits **Roshan Bisoi** as writer and gives April 2021 as a tentative completion date.[^bazaar] That is a development-stage record, not proof of a theatrical release on that date. It also reminds us that the credited source author and the person writing for the screen have different roles.

*Pratikshya* later received **Best Odia Film at the 69th National Film Awards**, announced in August 2023 for the **2021 award year**. The result names **Amiya Patnaik Productions** and **Anupam Patnaik** as producer and director recipients.[^award] Award eligibility, festival appearances and theatrical release dates should not be collapsed into one year.

## Watch an official introduction

[Amiya Patnaik Productions’ official trailer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_p4kHzZBTM) offers an introduction to the film. It is a trailer, not the complete film; current playback and territorial availability have not been tested here.

The film also belongs beside the series’ [economics chapter](cinema-economics.md). On screen, a family searches for financial stability. Behind the screen, another set of decisions determines how a story gets made and finds its audience. These are connected questions without being the same story.
