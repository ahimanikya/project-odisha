Two feet outside a hiding place. A rice-pounder in heaven. A heart burning unseen. Odia Dhaga give thought a home in pictures, sounds and voices.

These twenty-five are an editorial starting point drawn from [*Words We Carry*](https://projectodisha.org/dhaga/), not a ranking of the most popular expressions. The five sections form a reading journey through proverbs, idiomatic actions and verse. Read aloud, follow the picture, and consider the occasion.

Odia meanings and English literal renderings reuse the collection. Every “Imagined occasion” is a new example, not a collected conversation or origin story. A saying can illuminate a situation without becoming a rule for everyone.


## Words that make a scene


### 1. Advice that stops at the doorstep

> କହି ଦେଉଥାଇ ପରକୁ, ବୁଦ୍ଧି ନ ଦିଶଇ ଘରକୁ

One keeps advising others, but wisdom is not seen at home.[^dhaga-006]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଅନ୍ୟକୁ ଉପଦେଶ ଦେଇ ନିଜେ ତାହା ନ ମାନିବା।

The rhyme carries advice out towards others and brings it back home. The joke becomes more useful when we turn it on ourselves: a good principle deserves a place in our own habits, too.

**Imagined occasion:** Someone urges everyone to keep a reading diary, then laughs on discovering their own notebook untouched.


### 2. A hiding place that gives you away

> ଲୁଚିଛି ନା ଗୋଡ ଦିଇଟା ଦିଶୁଛି!

Are you hiding? Your two feet are showing![^dhaga-037]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଲୁଚାଇବାକୁ ଚେଷ୍ଟା କଲେ ମଧ୍ୟ କୌଣସି ଚିହ୍ନରୁ କଥାଟି ସ୍ପଷ୍ଟ ଜଣାପଡ଼ିଯିବା।

We see the hiding place and the feet at once. That small contradiction makes a whole comic scene without explaining how anyone got there. It is a gentle way to puncture pretence, though an apparent clue is not proof of guilt.

**Imagined occasion:** A birthday parcel is tucked behind a curtain, with its bright wrapping plainly visible underneath.


### 3. An answer that reveals too much

> ଠାକୁର ଘରେ କିଏ ରେ? ନାଁ ମୁଁ କଦଳୀ ଖାଇନାହିଁ

Who is in the shrine room? Well, I have not eaten the bananas.[^dhaga-043]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** କିଏ ଅଛି ବୋଲି ପଚାରିବାରେ କଦଳୀ ଖାଇନାହିଁ ବୋଲି ଉତ୍ତର ଦେବା—ଅନାବଶ୍ୟକ ସଫେଇ ଦେଇ ନିଜ ଉପରକୁ ସନ୍ଦେହ ଆଣିବା।

The question asks who; the answer supplies bananas. The listener has to complete the little play. Its pleasure lies in the excess information, not in a dependable method for detecting dishonesty.

**Imagined occasion:** Asked who moved a notebook, a friend immediately explains that they never read its last page.


### 4. The art of saying it well

> କହି ଜାଣିଲେ କଥା ସୁନ୍ଦର ବାନ୍ଧି ଜାଣିଲେ ମଥା ସୁନ୍ଦର

Know how to speak, and words are beautiful; know how to arrange hair, and the head is beautiful.[^dhaga-083]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** କଥା କହିବାର ଢଙ୍ଗ ଜାଣିଲେ କଥା ଭଲ ଲାଗେ; କେଶ ସଜାଇବା ଜାଣିଲେ ମଥା ସୁନ୍ଦର ଦିଶେ। ଉଭୟରେ କୌଶଳର ମୂଲ୍ୟ ଅଛି।

The paired phrases suggest that expression is a craft: choosing an order, finding a fitting tone, knowing when to stop. Beauty here comes through care rather than grand vocabulary.

**Imagined occasion:** Before a welcome speech, two friends shorten an awkward sentence until it sounds like something one person could say to another.


### 5. Words travel farther than drums

> ଚମବାଇଦ କୋଶେ, ତୁଣ୍ଡ ବାଇଦ ଶହସ୍ର କୋଶ

A hide-drum carries one kos; the mouth’s drum a thousand kos.[^dhaga-098]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଢୋଲର ଶବ୍ଦ ଯେତେ ଦୂର ଯାଏ, ଲୋକମୁଖରେ ଚାଲୁଥିବା କଥା ତାଠାରୁ ବହୁ ଦୂର ପହଞ୍ଚେ। ହଜାର କୋଶ କଥାଟି ଏଠାରେ ଅତିଶୟୋକ୍ତି।

The exaggeration catches how a story gains reach as people repeat it. It asks us to notice the responsibility that comes with passing words on.

**Imagined occasion:** A remark made to one neighbour has reached the whole lane by evening; the speaker resolves to check the next story.


## Pausing before we act


### 6. When too much turns bitter

> ଅତି ଲେମ୍ବୁ ଚିପୁଡ଼ିଲେ ପିତା ।

If you squeeze a lemon too much, it turns bitter.[^dhaga-001]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଅତି କଲେ ଭଲ କାମର ଫଳ ମଧ୍ୟ ଖରାପ ହୋଇପାରେ।

That reversal makes proportion easy to feel. More insistence is not always more care, and knowing when to stop can be part of doing something well.

**Imagined occasion:** After a friend agrees to one small favour, someone adds a fourth request.


### 7. Planting when the meal is due

> ବାହାଘର ବେଳେ ବାଇଗଣ ରୂଆ

Planting eggplant at wedding time.[^dhaga-008]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଦରକାର ବେଳ ଆସିଗଲା ପରେ ପ୍ରସ୍ତୁତି ଆରମ୍ଭ କରିବା।

The two clocks make the joke. Good intentions do not shorten every preparation, so the saying invites us to begin the slow work before urgency arrives.

**Imagined occasion:** A group starts rehearsing its performance on the morning of the event.


### 8. Anger aimed at the bowl

> ଓଷଦ ରାଗରେ ତାଟିଆ କାମୁଡିବା

Biting the bowl in anger at the medicine.[^dhaga-009]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଯାହା ପାଇଁ ରାଗ, ତାହା ବଦଳରେ ଅନ୍ୟ ଉପରେ ରାଗ ସୁଝାଇବା।

This absurd action gives displaced anger a visible shape. Recognising the picture may help us spare the person who merely brought unwelcome news.

**Imagined occasion:** A delayed delivery makes someone snap at the family member who answered the door; they pause and apologise.


### 9. Measure farther than you step

> ହାତେ ମାପି ଚାଖଣ୍ଡେ ଚାଲିବା

Measure a cubit, then move a span.[^dhaga-099]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଆଗକୁ ଭଲଭାବେ ବୁଝି ବିଚାରି ଛୋଟ ପଦକ୍ଷେପ ନେବା। ଯେତିକି କାମ କରିବା, ତାଠାରୁ ଅଧିକ ଦୂରର ଫଳ ଭାବିବା।

Caution becomes a bodily action rather than a lecture. We can take a modest step while thinking beyond its immediate result; preparation reduces risk without abolishing it.

**Imagined occasion:** Before promising to organise a weekend gathering, a volunteer checks the room, cost and travel arrangements.


### 10. Coincidence is not cause

> କାଉ ରାବିବାକୁ ତାଳ ପଡିବା

The palm fruit falls as the crow calls.[^dhaga-122]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଦୁଇଟି ଘଟଣା ଏକା ସମୟରେ ଘଟିଲେ ଗୋଟିଏ ଅନ୍ୟଟିର କାରଣ ବୋଲି ଧରିବା ଠିକ୍ ନୁହେଁ।

The saying interrupts that easy story. It is a compact invitation to ask what connects two events before deciding that one produced the other.

**Imagined occasion:** A device stops working just after a visitor arrives; someone checks the loose cable before blaming the visit.


## Work, skill and what sustains us


### 11. Learning when responsibility arrives

> କାନ୍ଧରେ ପଡିଲେ ବଜାଇ ଶିଖନ୍ତି

When it falls on the shoulder, one learns to play.[^dhaga-004]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଦାୟିତ୍ୱ ପଡ଼ିଲେ କାମ କରିବାର ଉପାୟ ଶିଖିହୁଏ।

A responsibility lands on the shoulder before the skill feels secure. The image gives courage to a beginner who must learn by doing. It does not make preparation needless, and the wording leaves the instrument unnamed.

**Imagined occasion:** The usual organiser is absent, so a new volunteer learns to run the meeting with help from the others.


### 12. Value put to the test

> କାଚ, କାଚ, ମଣି, ମଣି, କିଣା ବିକା ବେଳେ ଜଣା ଜଣି

Glass is glass, a gem is a gem; buying and selling reveal which is which.[^dhaga-020]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ପରୀକ୍ଷା ବେଳେ ଅସଲ ମୂଲ୍ୟ ଜଣାପଡ଼େ।

Then comes the moment when the difference matters. The saying moves us from an impressive claim to a practical test, without promising that every buyer will judge fairly.

**Imagined occasion:** Two tools look equally handsome in a catalogue; a craftsperson asks to try them on the actual task.


### 13. A noise that does not impress

> କଂସାରୀ ଘର ପାରାଙ୍କୁ କୁଲା ଢାଉଁ ଢାଉଁ

To the bell-metal worker’s household pigeons: bang, bang with a winnowing tray![^dhaga-046]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଯିଏ ପୂର୍ବରୁ ସେହିପରି ପରିସ୍ଥିତିରେ ଅଭ୍ୟସ୍ତ, ତାକୁ ସାମାନ୍ୟ ଧମକ ବା ଆଡ଼ମ୍ବର ଦେଖାଇ ପ୍ରଭାବିତ କରିବାର ଚେଷ୍ଟା।

They have heard more. The repeated sound supplies the comedy: a performance meant to intimidate meets an experienced audience.

**Imagined occasion:** A newcomer makes a theatrical threat to abandon a game; the older players smile and carry on.


### 14. The same work, even in heaven

> ଢିଙ୍କି ସ୍ୱର୍ଗକୁ ଗଲେ ବି ଧାନ କୁଟିବ

Even if the dhinki goes to heaven, it will still pound paddy.[^dhaga-048]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଢିଙ୍କି ସ୍ୱର୍ଗକୁ ଗଲେ ମଧ୍ୟ ତାହାର କାମ ଧାନ କୁଟିବା। ସ୍ଥାନ ବଦଳିଲେ ବି ପୁରୁଣା ଅଭ୍ୟାସ ବା କାମରେ ପରିବର୍ତ୍ତନ ନ ଆସିବାକୁ ଏହି କଥାରେ ଥଟ୍ଟା କରାଯାଏ।

Heaven is a magnificent destination for a humble rice-pounding implement. Yet the work follows it there. The impossible journey lets us smile at habits that survive a new address, while leaving open the possibility of deliberate change.

**Imagined occasion:** On holiday, the family organiser is still arranging everyone’s timetable and laughs at the reminder.


### 15. Even river sand runs out

> ବସିଖାଇଲେ ନଈବାଲି ସରେ

If one sits and consumes, even the river’s sand runs out.[^dhaga-097]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଭଣ୍ଡାର ଯେତେ ବଡ଼ ହେଲେ ବି କେବଳ ଖର୍ଚ୍ଚ କରି ଚାଲିଲେ ତାହା ସରିଯାଏ। ଭରଣା ନହେଲେ ନଈର ବାଲି ପରି ପ୍ରଚୁର ଜିନିଷ ବି ଶେଷ ହେବ ବୋଲି କୁହାଯାଇଛି।

River sand looks beyond counting, but the saying imagines even that reserve running out. The scale makes its point: having plenty and renewing what we use are different things. It need not become a rebuke to someone unable to work.

**Imagined occasion:** A club keeps drawing from its savings for celebrations; a member proposes replenishing it.


## Making room for other people


### 16. Some help rather than none

> ନାହିଁ ମାମୁଁ ଠଉଁ କଣା ମାମୁ ଭଲ

A one-eyed maternal uncle is better than no maternal uncle.[^dhaga-036]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** କିଛି ସହାୟତା ନ ମିଳିବାଠାରୁ ସୀମିତ ସହାୟତା ମିଳିବା ଭଲ। ଏଥିରେ ଥିବା ଦୃଷ୍ଟିଦୋଷର ତୁଳନାକୁ ମଣିଷର ମୂଲ୍ୟ ମାପିବାର ମାନଦଣ୍ଡ କରିବା ଉଚିତ ନୁହେଁ।

The family word brings assistance close to home. The practical question is whether limited help can let us begin when the ideal is unavailable. The bodily comparison also carries a prejudice: disability is not a measure of anyone’s worth. We can understand the thought without directing the comparison at a person.

**Imagined occasion:** A reading group accepts a small borrowed room rather than postponing every meeting until a perfect hall is found.


### 17. The fire nobody sees

> ବଣ ପୋଡ଼ିଲେ ଦୁନିଆ ଜାଣେ, ମନ ପୋଡ଼ିଲେ କେହୀ ନ ଜାଣେ

When a forest burns, the world knows; when the heart burns, no one knows.[^dhaga-073]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ବାହାରର ବିପଦ ସମସ୍ତଙ୍କୁ ଦେଖାଯାଏ, କିନ୍ତୁ ମନର ଦୁଃଖ ଅନ୍ୟଙ୍କ ଅଗୋଚରରେ ରହିପାରେ।

Forest and heart meet through sound and burning. One fire becomes public knowledge; the other may leave no visible trace. The line makes room for sorrow that has not announced itself, rather than declaring that understanding is impossible.

**Imagined occasion:** A usually cheerful friend has grown quiet; instead of assuming all is well, someone makes time to listen.


### 18. A meal ends; its reputation remains

> ଦିନକର ଭାତ ଯୁଗକର କଥା

One day’s rice; ages of reputation.[^dhaga-085]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଭୋଜନ ଦିନକର, କିନ୍ତୁ ସେହି ଅବସରର ଭଲମନ୍ଦ ନାମ ବହୁଦିନ ରହିପାରେ।

The leap from one day to ages gives a brief act of hospitality a long afterlife. The memorable part may be how someone was welcomed, not how elaborate the food was.

**Imagined occasion:** A guest recalls a simple meal years later because the hosts made room for them without fuss.


### 19. The crow eats; someone else is blamed

> କଥାର ମଞ୍ଜି, କୁଆ ଧାନ ଖାଏ ବୋହୁକୁ ଗଞ୍ଜି

The kernel of the matter: the crow eats the paddy, but the daughter-in-law is scolded.[^dhaga-086]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଦୋଷ କରୁଛି ଜଣେ, ଗାଳି ଶୁଣୁଛି ଆଉ ଜଣେ। ପ୍ରକୃତ କାରଣ ନଦେଖି ଅନ୍ୟ ଉପରେ ଦୋଷ ଲଦିବାର ଅନ୍ୟାୟ ଏଥିରେ ଧରାପଡ଼େ।

The crow eats while the daughter-in-law receives the scolding. We can see the injustice before anyone explains it. The saying turns attention from the easiest person to blame towards what actually happened.

**Imagined occasion:** A shared document goes missing, and the newest volunteer is blamed before anyone checks the folder history.


### 20. One hand cannot clap

> ଏକା ହାତରେ ତାଳି ବାଜେ ନାହିଁ

A clap cannot sound with one hand alone.[^dhaga-104]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** କିଛି କାମ ପାଇଁ ଉଭୟ ପକ୍ଷର ସହଯୋଗ ଦରକାର; ଗୋଟିଏ ପକ୍ଷର ଚେଷ୍ଟାରେ ସବୁବେଳେ କାମ ହୁଏ ନାହିଁ। ତେବେ କ୍ଷତି ହେଲେ ଉଭୟ ପକ୍ଷ ସମାନ ଦୋଷୀ ବୋଲି ଧରିନେବା ଠିକ୍ ନୁହେଁ।

Two palms make the sound together. It is a useful picture of cooperation when a task needs a response from someone else. It is not a rule that both people are equally responsible for harm, especially where one faces coercion or abuse.

**Imagined occasion:** One person keeps proposing dates for a shared project; the other finally helps settle a workable plan.


## Time, place and the person we become


### 21. Refusing to be polished into something else

> ମୋତେ ଯେତେ ମାଠିବୁ ମାଠ ମୁହିଁ ସେଇ ଦରପଡ଼ା କାଠ

Polish me as much as you like; I remain that half-burnt wood.[^dhaga-031]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଯେତେ ବୁଝାଇଲେ ବା ବଦଳାଇବାକୁ ଚେଷ୍ଟା କଲେ ମଧ୍ୟ ନିଜେ ସେପରି ରହିବି ବୋଲି କହିବା।

The wood answers back in the first person. Repeated polishing meets repeated refusal, and ମାଠ against କାଠ makes the answer snap shut. It can be comic self-recognition or stubborn defiance; it is a voice in a conversation, not a verdict that people cannot learn.

**Imagined occasion:** A friend who always arrives with too many books jokes about remaining unchanged after another plea to pack lightly.


### 22. There will be another winter

> ଏକା ମାଘକେ ଶୀତ ଯାଏନାହିଁ।

Cold does not finish with a single Magha.[^dhaga-003]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଆଜିର ଦରକାର ସରିଗଲେ ମଧ୍ୟ ଆଗକୁ ପୁଣି ଦରକାର ପଡ଼ିପାରେ।

What sounds like a remark about weather can become a reminder that we may need each other again. Spoken warmly, it encourages continuing care; spoken sharply, it can sound like a warning.

**Imagined occasion:** After receiving help with a difficult task, someone remembers to be available when the helper needs a hand months later.


### 23. Each place has its own ways

> ଦେଶକେ ଫାଙ୍କ୍, ନଈକେ ବାଙ୍କ୍

Each place has its particular ways; each river has its bends.[^dhaga-103]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ସବୁ ଅଞ୍ଚଳର ରୀତି ଏକା ପରି ନୁହେଁ; ନଈର ବାଙ୍କ ପରି ପ୍ରତ୍ୟେକ ସ୍ଥାନର ନିଜସ୍ୱ ଢଙ୍ଗ ଅଛି। ଏହା ସ୍ଥାନୀୟ ଚଳଣି ବୁଝିବାକୁ କହେ, ସବୁ ରୀତିକୁ ନିର୍ବିଚାରରେ ମାନିନେବାକୁ ନୁହେଁ।

The bends belong to the river; local ways belong to a place. The compact pairing gives difference a shape we can recognise. Learning how things are done elsewhere begins with attention, without requiring us to accept every practice uncritically.

**Imagined occasion:** At a gathering in another town, a visitor asks how guests usually take part before assuming they match home.


### 24. Distance makes things look better

> ଦୂର ପର୍ବତ ସୁନ୍ଦର; ଦୂର ବନ୍ଧୁ ସୁନ୍ଦର

A distant mountain is beautiful; a distant friend is beautiful.[^dhaga-109]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଦୂରରୁ ଦେଖିଲେ ପାହାଡ଼ ଓ ବନ୍ଧୁ—ଉଭୟଙ୍କ ଦୁର୍ବଳତା ଲୁଚିଯାଇପାରେ। ନିକଟରୁ ଜାଣିଲେ ଚିତ୍ରଟି ବଦଳିପାରେ।

Both may look simpler before we encounter their rough edges. The parallel invites affection with clearer sight, not the conclusion that friendship is better kept remote.

**Imagined occasion:** Someone envies a friend’s apparently effortless life until they hear about the work behind it.


### 25. Do not laugh at another’s turn

> ଉପର ବରଡ଼ା ଖସୁଛି,
> ମଝି ବରଡ଼ା ହସୁଛି 
> ତଳ ବରଡ଼ା କହୁଛି,
> ମୋ ଦିନ କାଳ ଯାଉଛିରେ ଭାଇ ତୋ ଦିନକାଳ ଆସୁଛି ॥

The upper leaf falls; the middle leaf laughs; the lower leaf says, “My time is passing, brother; your time is coming.”[^dhaga-153]

**ଅର୍ଥ:** ଅନ୍ୟର ଅବସ୍ଥା ବଦଳିବା ଦେଖି ହସିବା ପୂର୍ବରୁ ନିଜ ପାଳି କଥା ଭାବିବାକୁ ଏହା ମନେ ପକାଏ। ସମୟ କାହା ପାଇଁ ଅଟକି ରହେନାହିଁ।

The leaves fall, laugh and speak. This editorial reading hears a warning in their exchange: another person’s turn is not separate from our own passage through time. The changing voices make the reflection memorable without settling a botanical explanation or an original occasion.

**Imagined occasion:** A young colleague laughs at an older person’s difficulty with a new tool; another invites them to remember being a beginner.

Choose one that stays with you. Ask someone when they would use it—and when they would not. Keep differing versions beside it. A proverb can open a conversation rather than win one.
