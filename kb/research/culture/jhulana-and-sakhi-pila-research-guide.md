# Jhulana and Sakhi Pila: a research guide

This guide connects separate local articles on Jhulana and Sakhi Nata with their remaining research questions. Original source posts are credited with each topic; the earlier survey inspected captions, not all audiovisual material.

## Jhulana: song, season and painting

Read [Jhulana in Odisha: the swing, the song and the season](jhulana-in-odisha.md), an illustrated article distinguishing Puri and Paralakhemundi observances, a named repertoire entry and a lyric. See [the original post](https://www.instagram.com/odissiandbeyond/reel/Dciw7uUzIFR/).

**Remaining evidence:** Recover full festival PDFs for direct page inspection, audition the catalogue recording, and establish any local ritual history of the Baikunthanath lyric. The Pahari painting is comparative imagery, not Odisha field evidence.

## Sakhi Pila

Read [Sakhi Nata and Sakhi Pila: song, dancer and companion](sakhi-nata-and-sakhi-pila.md). It follows Pathy’s description of responsive performance, Balaram Gouda’s documented repertoire and the relationship to Odissi theatre history. [Soumya’s post](https://www.instagram.com/odissiandbeyond/p/DcfmhbDCPGB/) remains credited as the lead.

**Documentary candidate found:** the [JD Centre of Art programme](https://www.jdcentreofart.org/program), 8 February 2020, lists *Sakhi Pila (Boy Odissi Dancer)*, Santosh Gour, India, 1999, 18 minutes, with a Gotipua synopsis. The post caption names Santosh Kumar; the catalogue does not prove that this is the excerpted film.

**Next evidence needed:** inspect the film and credits, match the excerpt, and identify “Kelu sir.” Neither the full film nor this identity has been verified. Read [Champu and the literary sakhi](champu-and-sakhi.md) and [Gotipua](gotipua.md) with attention to distinct uses of the names; avoid both universal equivalence and absolute separation.
