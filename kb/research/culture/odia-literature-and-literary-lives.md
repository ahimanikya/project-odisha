# Odia Literature & Literary Lives

Concept and direction: **Ahimanikya Satapathy**, Founder & Editor-in-Chief. Research planning and documentation prepared with AI assistance. The Founder asked for substantive coverage of Odisha's languages beyond Odia and of Odia literature and its prominent personalities. This is a collection plan and research queue, not completed biographies or publication approval.

## Two connected doors into the encyclopedia

**Languages of Odisha** gives each language or variety its own history, people, literature, oral traditions and present-day life. Odia is the primary focus, while research on other languages begins alongside it. Santali, Kui, Kuvi, Saora, Ho, Juang, Koya and other entries have resource leads in [SCSTRTI's learning catalogue](https://scstrti.in/index.php/resources/mle-initiative/trilingual-booklets). Regional naming and classification questions, including Sambalpuri/Kosali and Desia, remain attributed research questions. The [language model](../../reference/language-heritage-model.md) holds the fuller queue and evidence rules.

**Odia Literature & Literary Lives** is a dedicated collection, reached from the Odia profile and the main culture collection. It connects an author's life to their works, literary context, influence and places. Literature in other languages belongs to those languages' collections and can connect through translation or exchange; it is not automatically Odia literature because its creator lives in Odisha.

Proposed navigation: **Explore → Languages of Odisha** and **Explore → Literature → Odia Literature → Writers / Works / Literary Journeys**. These routes are proposals, not deployed website paths.

## What the literature collection covers

- **History and movements:** early textual evidence, epic retellings, devotional traditions, poetic forms, print culture, modern fiction, literary movements and contemporary writing. Period boundaries and claims of a “first” require attributed scholarship.
- **People:** poets, novelists, short-story writers, playwrights, essayists, translators, editors and collectors of oral traditions. Include women and less represented voices from the beginning; awards help discovery but do not determine inclusion.
- **Works:** an approachable introduction to a poem, story, novel, play or essay; what it explores, its original language and edition, why it matters, and where someone can legally read or hear it.
- **Literature in life:** performance, recitation, songs, classrooms, magazines, household storytelling and public debate. Separate original writing from later adaptation and performance.
- **Places in literature:** precise links to a writer's life, a work's setting or its subject. A literary place can later become part of a visitor's tour book, with practical access checked separately.

## Reusable profile structure

**Writer page:** name in Odia and English → a short account of their contribution → sourced life timeline → selected works → themes and literary context → connected people and places → verified short excerpt or licensed recording → further reading and compact source notes. Distinguish documented influence from editorial interpretation. Use authenticated portraits, manuscripts or relevant locations with checked rights.

**Work page:** original title and transliteration → author and other credited roles → original language, genre and edition → accessible synopsis → historical context and themes → supported place connections → limited verified excerpt and separately credited translation → reading/listening options → adaptation and rights history.

Use the existing model's `person`, `cultural_work`, `language` and `place` entities. Connect them with `wrote`, `written_in_language`, `about`, `set_in` and specific biographical relations. Translation records retain the translator, source work and edition. Do not duplicate a full biography on every destination page.

## First delivery sequence

1. Research an Odia overview alongside an initial set of non-Odia language profiles; other languages are not conditional on completing the Odia collection.
2. Prepare a small literary pilot across periods and forms: Sarala Das, Fakir Mohan Senapati, Bhima Bhoi, Gopinath Mohanty, Pratibha Ray and Pratibha Satpathy. Choose one verified work for each before drafting its narrative. This selection is an editorial proposal.
3. Review original-language names, excerpts and translations with a competent human reader; verify claims and image/audio rights. Publish the Founder-approved candidate, then expand the collection.

Each published writer must connect to at least one researched work. Each published work must identify its author, language and edition/source. No invented quotation, unsourced superlative or decorative AI portrait presented as a real author. Missing material becomes tracked research work, not filler.
