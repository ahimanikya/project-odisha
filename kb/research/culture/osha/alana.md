# Alana Osha

Alana makes restraint part of food culture. Its saved descriptions discuss salt, while a modern story begins with daughters excluded from a pitha meal. These are different kinds of evidence: an account of observance and a narrated explanation. Their calendars also disagree.

**Research note: original history and local practice remain incomplete.** No annual visit date confirmed.

## What does without salt mean?

Dash says that no salt is eaten during Alana; Mahanti says no salt is added to the bhoga, generally fruits and sweets. The first wording concerns consumption, the second preparation of offerings. They do not establish identical restrictions, their duration or a household menu. Dash’s adjacent general rice-and-biri cakes and khiri discussion is not an Alana recipe.

## Keep the deities and calendars attributed

Dash lists Ashwina and Lakshmi. Mahanti describes Ashwina, Surya and married women. Shrutilipi’s collected account instead gives sunrise worship on Dhanu Sundays ending at Makar Sankranti. Solar Dhanu is not silently converted into lunar Pausa. The differences remain unresolved; one season or deity cannot yet be presented as universal.

## Two daughters and a pitha meal

In Shrutilipi’s retelling, a Chakulia Panda abandons his daughters after their mother shares rice-and-biri pitha with them. A protector shelters them. They learn Alana using materials left by celestial women, including banana peel and coconut. Prosperity follows; after marriages, the elder daughter loses wealth when she neglects the observance. These are narrative events and beliefs, not verified history.
