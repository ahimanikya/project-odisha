# Ashoka Astami Brata

Ashoka Astami opens three related but different lines of enquiry: a household vow for children, a prayer involving spring flower buds, and Bhubaneswar’s Rukuna Ratha festival. Their shared name invites comparison. It does not yet establish one story, one deity or one meal across all three.

## A household vow to Surya

Mahanti describes married women observing a Chaitra bright-eighth vow to Surya for their children’s well-being. His brief entry names no food or household katha. He also says the vow is no longer observed, but gives no survey or locality-level evidence for that conclusion; its present prevalence remains unknown.

## Rama and the festival story

In Mahanti’s separate festival account, Ravana is protected by Durga. Following Vibhishana’s advice, Rama worships her; she withdraws her support on the eighth day. Rama defeats Ravana and celebrates with a chariot procession of Shiva and Durga. This is the author’s attributed legend, not a dated founding event or a recovered story of the household Surya vow.

## Eight buds in a textual prescription

The opening of chapter CXXXIII in the Garuda Purana translation attributed to Manmatha Nath Dutt prescribes eating eight ashoka flower buds on Chaitra’s bright eighth when joined with Punarvasu. Its prayer addresses ashoka as dear to Hara and seeks release from grief. This is a ritual prescription and promise within the text, not evidence of a health effect.

Sources: [Garuda Purana, Dutt translation: Asokastami Vratas etc., chapter CXXXIII](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-garuda-purana-dutt/d/doc122613.html). Chapter CXXXIII opening paragraph, before Mahanavami.
