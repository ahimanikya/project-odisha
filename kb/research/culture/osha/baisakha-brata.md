# Baisakha Brata

Baisakha Brata joins a month of devotion to a repeated evening meal. The food matters through its place in a daily sequence of worship, reading and restraint. Broader Vaishakha stories also give food another role: hospitality to people travelling in the heat.

**Research note: published Odisha practice and a comparative devotional story; local text, origin and recipe remain unresolved.**

## A month of Vishnu devotion

Mahanti describes Baisakha Brata throughout the lunar month, observed by married women and widows. Worship of Vishnu or a temple visit, together with reading the Vishnu Purana, precedes the meal. He says no formal brata is worn. Dash also lists Baisakha, but supplies no further description. These accounts do not establish a universal household rule or current participation rate.

## The meal: havishanna or kechudi

Mahanti names havishanna or kechudi as the single meal taken towards evening. He supplies no proportions, grain or pulse variety, vegetables, seasoning or cooking method. The two names are retained without turning them into a fixed recipe or assuming they denote exactly the same preparation. A local account should explain how the meal is made and who shares it.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed 45 / PDF61.

## A comparative story of hospitality

In V. D. N. Rao’s condensed Skanda Purana telling, King Kirtiman encounters Vasishtha’s disciples offering shade, fruit and drinks to travellers. After learning about Vaishakha, he extends such service across his kingdom. Vishnu protects his devotion when Yama objects to its extraordinary results. This is a religious narrative from a broader tradition, not a documented Odisha king or an identified local Baisakha katha.

Sources: [Essence of Skanda Purana: Vaisakha Month’s Significance](https://www.kamakoti.org/kamakoti/details/skandapurana28.html). Web chapter 28, King Kirtiman paragraphs.

## Food given and food eaten

Rao’s account commends supplying water, meals and shelter in Vaishakha. These gifts belong to its account of charity. They do not establish the ingredients of an Odisha observer’s evening meal. Mahanti’s reference to reading the Vishnu Purana likewise does not identify this Skanda Purana story as the text read locally.
