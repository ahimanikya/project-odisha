# Bali Trutiya

Bali Trutiya brings together a vow, a story of choosing a husband, and food withheld, offered and eventually eaten.

**Narrative checkpoint; origin date, local variation and public availability remain open.**

## Sand gives the observance a tangible form

Mahanti describes married women worshipping Shiva and Parvati through sand images on Bhadraba’s bright-fortnight third day. He explains the name through bali, meaning sand. Dash lists Bali Trutiya or Tija Brata Katha among bratas. These descriptions establish a named tradition in modern accounts, not its starting date.

## The story in the recovered Odia text

Parvati asks Shiva how she came to marry him. He recalls her father’s promise to Vishnu, her distress, and a friend who takes her into the forest. Beside a river she fasts and worships Shiva; he grants her wish. Her father later accepts her chosen marriage. This is the booklet’s ritual narrative, not a historical event.

Sources: [Bali Trutiya ba Tija Brata Katha — scanned Odia text](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/33/Bali_Trutiya_Brata.pdf). PDF pp.3–10; narrative decision pp.6–7, river and vow pp.8–9, resolution p.10.

## Fruit offered, fruit eaten

In the story, Parvati breaks her fast with forest fruit after the immersion. Later instructions separately list fruit among offerings and prohibit eating on Tritiya. These are different food roles within one text. The passage supplies neither fruit species nor a cooked-dish recipe.

Sources: [Bali Trutiya ba Tija Brata Katha — scanned Odia text](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/33/Bali_Trutiya_Brata.pdf). PDF p.9, banajata phala and parana; p.10, phala nibedana; p.11, fasting injunction.

## Reading its significance

The friend’s intervention makes this story useful for discussing companionship and a woman’s expressed choice, alongside family authority. That is an editorial reading, not evidence of how every practitioner understands the vow. The text’s threats of misfortune for women who eat are devotional rhetoric; the encyclopedia does not attribute bereavement or suffering to someone’s food choices.

## A season, not a confirmed travel date

The booklet names Bhadraba shukla Tritiya with Hasta nakshatra. This is a traditional calendar rule; converting it into a date for a particular year requires a separately checked local calendar.

Sources: [Bali Trutiya ba Tija Brata Katha — scanned Odia text](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/3/33/Bali_Trutiya_Brata.pdf). PDF p.4 and pp.8–9.

## What can be said about its history?

The archive’s approximate eighteenth-century attribution, its metadata date of 8 February 1934, and the newsletter’s 2005 label do not agree. The supplied scan contains an excerpt and a digital cover, with no verified publication imprint. Edition and composition dates therefore remain unknown. Mahanti also relates a Rama-before-Lanka legend and attributes a Parvati account to the Padma Purana; neither an original Purana chapter nor the relationship between these versions has been established here.

## A maritime interpretation remains unresolved

Biswal and Dash’s jhoti article associates a celebration named Bali Trutiya with boat drawings and trade with Bali. That account has not been reconciled with the sand-image Shiva–Parvati descriptions. It is retained as a source conflict requiring locality and textual verification, not adopted as maritime history or an explanation of this name.
