# Bata Osha

A village path becomes a place of prayer. Accounts of Bata Osha bring sweeping, family welfare and food offerings into the same ritual space, while disagreeing on its calendar and related names.

## A story of protection

Sarangi relates a tradition that Kunti performed this worship during the Pandavas’ exile and entrusted her sons’ protection to Yama. This is her retelling; an exact epic passage or dated katha witness has not been inspected. It cannot establish when the observance began.

## The work of tending a path

Sarangi describes girls sweeping together with bajramuli brooms during Margasira, accompanied by a song. Her account culminates at the full moon with a decorated panthei pit and images of the Pandavas and Yama. She interprets cleared paths as helping families carrying harvested rice. That practical explanation is an author’s interpretation, not demonstrated proof of the ritual’s origin.

## Worship outside the house

## Names that should not yet be merged

Dash lists Bata with Yama and separately lists Danda Panhara with Shiva in Pausa. Mahanti calls the observance Bata or Danda pahara and describes Yama worship. We preserve both records without declaring the names universally interchangeable or claiming two independently established traditions. Local naming and textual comparison remain necessary.
