# Dutiya Osha

Dutiya’s food story begins before the curry is cooked. Produce is gathered for an offering, then becomes ghanta for a meal and exchange. Reading the occasion through that movement reveals women’s ritual work and relationships between households. Its stories and names need equally careful handling: a published Jimutavahana retelling, a coastal description and western Pua Jiuntia accounts are related reading, not one interchangeable history.

**Research note: full local katha, history and situated practice remain incomplete.** Ashwina association; no annual visit date confirmed.

## Produce changes its role

Pratap Kumar Dash’s2006 essay describes fruits and vegetables arranged as bhoga and used for ghanta the next day. Mahanti’s coastal account names twenty-one seasonal vegetables under an aparamanga leaf, later cooked and exchanged as ghanta tarkari. Dash names an Apamaranga branch elsewhere. A branch and a covering leaf are different descriptions; neither source here establishes the plant’s botanical identity. These accounts support a sequence of offering, cooking and sharing, rather than a compulsory ingredient list.

## A maternal vow and its figures

Mahanti presents Dwitibabana as Surya’s son through a Brahmin widow and describes women praying for children’s long lives or hoping for a child. This is a belief account, not biological or historical evidence. He names sand figures of a fox and kite; Dash says fox and eagle. We retain those source words rather than assign one bird species. The complete local story explaining the figures has not been recovered.

## A published story of choosing to protect another

Rajalakshmi Tripathy’s2023 essay retells Jimutavahana discovering the remains of snakes sacrificed to Garuda. He offers himself in a snake’s place; the arriving snake intervenes, and Jimutavahana persuades Garuda to renounce violence. The retelling ends with his marriage to Malayavati and her introduction of the vow. Tripathy calls it an Odia Ramayana story without identifying an edition or passage. We present her retelling, not an established founding event or the recovered fox-and-bird katha.

## An offering list is not a recipe

Dash’s food paragraph prints jambila, naranga, kerandakoli and padma puskar among Dutiya’s required fruits/items. These source forms remain unresolved here; we do not identify species or treat them as the complete curry. The adjacent list of Dudha gainthala, mula kanji, muga manda and muga paiti belongs to his discussion of Danda Panhara and must not be copied into Dutiya’s menu. A named household preparation, quantities and substitutions are still needed.

Sources: [The Perishing Tradition of Osha, Brata and Mela in Orissa: An Overview](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/April2006/engpdf/The_perishing_tradition_of_osha.pdf). Printed17/PDF3, right column, Dutiya passage followed by Danda Panhara.

## Compare western Odisha without erasing it

The saved Pua Jiuntia study records Mahanti’s jugara and tikhiri offerings, followed by a vegetable curry, suji manda and kheeri. E Kiran Mohan’s Barpali account differs in vegetable count, wrapping leaf and thread placement. Those details remain on that regional page. Shared Dutibahana naming and a maternal vow invite comparison but do not establish that every coastal Dutiya household follows the same sequence, dishes or story.

## Where history still needs a text

Saved institutional catalogue records identify Duti Bahana Osha at ORPR-106 and Dwitia Osha at OL-2452, and museum palm-leaf entries OL/2673 and OL/2674. Their contents and copying dates remain uninspected. The existing portal lead advertises a22-chapter Bruhat Dwitibahan Osha credited to Purusottam Das but offers an app-only route. Neither listing verifies authorship, edition date or earliest observance. Tripathy’s early-panji and Dhauli claims stay quarantined because no identified original passage has been recovered.

## Reading significance through food and care

The offering-to-meal sequence gives this page its organising idea: ritual food becomes food exchanged between people. Mothers’ preparation and fasting make care visible in the published accounts; exchange makes the occasion larger than an individual plate. This is an editorial reading of documented actions, not a measured social, nutritional or economic effect. The wish for children’s protection stays within the observers’ belief.

## A seasonal enquiry, not a promised meal

Mahanti gives Ashwina’s dark eighth, while the saved register retains Tripathy’s seventh-day statement as a conflict. No annual date is generated. Odisha Tourism also describes ghanta in a general Jajpur food listing; that does not establish a Dutiya meal or an operating venue. A visitor enquiry should identify the preparation, confirmed date, provider and welcome separately. Private worship and shared household food do not themselves establish visitor access.
