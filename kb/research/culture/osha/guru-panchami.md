# Guru Panchami

Guru Panchami opens a question about whom a community honours as its guru. The accounts found here point to teachers, the seven sages and Brihaspati, the divine teacher. They cannot yet be joined into one calendar, story or menu.

**Research note: published descriptions and a devotional comparison; local identity, calendar, full katha and food remain unresolved.**

## Teachers and disciples in one Odisha account

Mahanti places the vow on Bhadrava’s bright fifth day, after Ganesh Chaturthi. He associates it with disciples honouring teachers in residential gurukulas. His next paragraph describes married women fasting and honouring the seven sages in relation to menstrual purity beliefs. These are the author’s descriptions; the beliefs are not adopted as judgments about women or their bodies.

## Three calendar statements, identities still open

Dash’s 2006 inventory assigns Guru Panchami to Ashwina and separately names Roosi Panchami. Mahanti gives Bhadrava. In her February2022 essay, Tulasi Ojha assigns a Guru Panchami dedicated to Brihaspati to Phalguna’s bright fifth day, and treats Rishi Panchami separately in Bhadrava. The discrepancy may involve different uses of the name, differing accounts or error. The evidence does not yet decide between these explanations.

## Brihaspati: an attributed devotional episode

Ojha presents Brihaspati as adviser to the gods. She recalls his sending his son Kacha to Shukracharya to learn the Sanjivani mantra despite the danger. This is an episode in her account of the divine teacher, not a recovered Odisha household brata-katha or a historical event.

## Food evidence: fasting, with the menu unknown

Mahanti reports upabasha in the women’s observance, but names no dish, ingredients, offering, water rule, duration or fast-breaking meal for Guru Panchami. ## What can be said about history

The sources establish published descriptions, not a founding date. Mahanti calls the practice extinct and links that to the disappearance of gurukulas, but supplies no statewide survey or dated institutional series in this passage. That statement is not sufficient to declare the observance absent today. A modern article using the name likewise does not establish living practice.

## Significance without flattening differences

An editorial reading can explore gratitude towards teachers, the transmission of knowledge and the authority attached to ritual instruction. It should also ask whose work and restrictions sustain an observance. The comparison is most useful when participants can explain their own names, stories and food rules. A complete cultural history needs those situated accounts alongside identifiable editions.
