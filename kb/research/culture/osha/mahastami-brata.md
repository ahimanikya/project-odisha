# Mahastami Brata

Mahastami brings the household vow into the wider season of Durga worship. Its short published account places fruit, fasting and a brata worn after worship at the centre. Understanding that small ritual scene also requires asking which story a household reads and how its food differs from the neighbouring traditions of Bhai Jiuntia and public Durga Puja.

## The household account

Mahanti places Mahastami Brata on Aswina’s bright eighth day. Married women fast, worship a brata before Durga with fruit, then wear it; he also mentions reading Durgastami brata-katha. He supplies neither named fruit varieties nor a complete story.

## Significance through household practice

The account invites a reading of devotion as preparation, restraint, storytelling and something carried on the body after worship. Food has several possible roles: an offering, something shared, and a meal that ends a fast. Keeping those roles separate makes space for households to explain their own practice. This is an editorial interpretation and a research agenda, not a claim that all participants give the vow one meaning.
