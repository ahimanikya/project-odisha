# Manabasa Gurubara

A measure of grain becomes a way to think about sustenance, dignity and who may share a meal.

**Narrative research checkpoint**. Traditional month: Margasira. No annual visit date is confirmed.

## Lakshmi and the rejected devotee

Balabhadra Ghadai retells the Lakshmi Purana associated with Balaram Das: Lakshmi honours Sriya, a devotee identified in the narrative as Chandala. Balabhadra objects to the visit, and Jagannath excludes Lakshmi. The conflict centres on caste and the goddess’s freedom to recognise devotion. This is the text’s story, not evidence that discrimination subsequently disappeared.

Sources: [Cult of Goddess Mahalakshmi](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/orissareview/2018/Jun-July/engpdf/june-july-or-2018.pdf). Cult of Goddess Mahalakshmi, printed p.102.

## Hunger changes the argument

The Mohantys’ 2021 essay describes the brothers’ hardship after Lakshmi leaves and reads the work as a challenge to caste barriers. Food becomes central to the dispute over authority. Our interpretation is that a household’s feeding labour is made visible through its withdrawal: those who dismiss its source discover their dependence on it.

Sources: [Laxmi: The Goddess of Prosperity and Salvation](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/2021/June-July/engpdf/page-21-25.pdf). Printed p.23, Lakshmi Purana discussion.

## The meal and the recognition

In Ghadai’s retelling, the hungry brothers reach Lakshmi’s new household. Told that its mistress is Chandala, they first attempt to cook for themselves and fail. They finally accept her cooked food, recognise its familiar preparation and reunite with her. The reversal makes exclusion answerable to the experience of hunger. Ghadai himself says the story’s time of formation is not definitely known.

Sources: [Cult of Goddess Mahalakshmi](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/orissareview/2018/Jun-July/engpdf/june-july-or-2018.pdf). Printed pp.103–104.

## The household observance

The same essay places the worship on Margasira Thursdays and identifies the mana or grain measure among its objects. Dash records khiri, khechudi, kakara and chakuli as associated foods. These are attributed examples, not a compulsory menu for every Thursday or home.

Sources: [Laxmi: The Goddess of Prosperity and Salvation](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/2021/June-July/engpdf/page-21-25.pdf), [The Perishing Tradition of Osha, Brata and Mela in Orissa: An Overview](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/April2006/engpdf/The_perishing_tradition_of_osha.pdf). Mohanty p.23; Dash p.17.

## The measure, grain and drawn threshold

The Odia transcription describes the first Margasira Thursday through a prepared grain measure, new paddy and rice-paste designs. Areca nuts, sugarcane, radish and banana belong to its ritual arrangement. This passage records objects and preparation, not a verified household menu.

## A separate Sudasa passage

The text then explicitly introduces Sudasa Brata: a Thursday coinciding with the bright tenth. Its ten-knotted thread and ten manda with banana, coconut and chhena follow that transition. They cannot automatically be assigned to every Manabasa Thursday; quantities and practice need edition and household checks.

Source: [Odia textual witness](https://or.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=ମାଣବସା_ଲକ୍ଷ୍ମୀ_ପୁରାଣ&oldid=57790). Revision57790, displayed57–69; opening ମହାଲକ୍ଷ୍ମୀଙ୍କର ଆଉ ଏକ ବ୍ରତ.


## Return on the condition of shared food

At the close, Lakshmi confronts the brothers for accepting food after condemning her as Chandala. Before returning, she demands mutual feeding across the caste hierarchy; Jagannath agrees. The passage makes shared food a condition of reconciliation within the story. It does not prove that historical discrimination ended.

Source: [Odia textual witness](https://or.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=ମାଣବସା_ଲକ୍ଷ୍ମୀ_ପୁରାଣ&oldid=57790). Revision57790, displayed463–480; especially477–479.
