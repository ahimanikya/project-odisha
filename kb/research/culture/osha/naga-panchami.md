# Naga Panchami

Naga Panchami brings the serpent into a season of devotion. To understand its history, we must read the calendar, the story, the image and the offering as different kinds of evidence.

**Research note: calendar identity, attributed offerings and a sculptural witness; local food preparation and earliest observance history remain open.**

## A Sravana observance with its own identity

Mahanti places Naga Panchami on Sravana shukla Panchami, the fifth day of the bright lunar fortnight. He places Nagala Chaturthi separately in Kartika. His joint description associates both with fasting and women’s prayers for children, and describes Manasa worship through a branch, earthen pot or clay serpent. These are attributed descriptions, not a survey of all Odisha households.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed55 / PDF71.

## Milk and rice: the role matters

The same joint account names milk and rice as offerings. It does not tell us whether that rice is raw, cooked or made into a sweet, or describe a post-fast meal. We therefore record the ingredients and their ritual role without inventing a pitha, payasa or standard festival menu. Nagala-specific dry-fruit wording from another source is not transferred to Naga Panchami.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed55 / PDF71; joint entry, not a separate Naga Panchami household record.

## A story in which a request ends revenge

The saved Ganguli translation tells how Astika asks King Janamejaya to stop the snake sacrifice. In the selected sections his mother is Jaratkaru, Vasuki’s sister. Mahanti retells the rescue with Manasa as mother and attaches its ending to Sravana Panchami. The inspected epic sections do not supply that calendar statement. The detailed comparison is retained in the Nagala study; this profile does not count it as another independent witness.

Sources: [Mahabharata, Volume I, Adi Parva — Astika Parva, sections LIV–LVIII](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15474), [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). ## The story also appears in Odisha sculpture

Sasanka Sekhar Panda documents an Astika–Jaratkaru image at Banesvara Shiva temple, Deogaon near Banei in Sundargarh. He describes a seated woman carrying Astika and a serpent emblem on the pedestal, and assigns the sculpture to the eighth–ninth century CE by iconography. His Plate 11 illustrates it. That is an attributed date for an image: it cannot establish the age of Naga Panchami, its calendar or its food. His note 46 refers back to his own 1995 report, which remains unread here.

Sources: [Nagas in the Sculptural Decorations of Early West Orissan Temples](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Journal/jounalvol1/pdf/orhj-4.pdf). Printed22 / PDF7; printed34 / PDF19 note46; printed36 / PDF21 Plate11.

## Serpent imagery has a wider setting

The Odisha State Museum describes Naga and Yaksha images collected around Bhubaneswar. This is evidence of objects in its collection account, not a claim that every serpent image represents Naga Panchami. An image, a temple association and an annual observance need separate evidence before they become one history.

Sources: [Archaeology — Odisha State Museum](https://odishamuseum.nic.in/?q=dept/archaelogy). Archaeology introduction.

## Care, restraint and practical knowledge

As an editorial reading, the family prayers and Astika’s interruption of revenge make care and restraint useful themes for telling this story. The material work deserves equal attention: shaping clay, making metal images and preparing offerings. Their local techniques, recipes and transmission still need documentation. Neither successful ritual performance nor an old sculpture establishes medical efficacy, snake ecology knowledge or measured conservation outcomes.
