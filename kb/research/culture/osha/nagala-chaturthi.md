# Nagala Chaturthi

A serpent image, an offering and a story about stopping revenge open this Kartika vow to study. Similar names and shared stories need careful separation.

**Research note: identity, literary comparison and attributed offerings; local katha and earliest history remain open.**

## Two observances, two calendar rules

Mahanti distinguishes Nagala Chaturthi on Kartika shukla Chaturthi from Naga Panchami on Sravana shukla Panchami. His joint entry describes serpent worship, fasting and women’s prayers for children. Dash places Nagala in Kartika, naming Shiva in the inventory and serpent worship later. Shared devotion does not make the two dates interchangeable.

## Offerings are not yet a household menu

Mahanti’s joint account names milk and rice as offerings. OdiaPortal’s Nagala landing page instead names milk and dry fruits. Neither establishes a named household’s preparation, what worshippers eat afterwards, or a cooked dish. Dash’s nearby bundi reference belongs to Sankata Chaturthi and must not be transferred here.

## Astika asks the king to stop

In Ganguli’s translation, Jaratkaru sends her son Astika to help her serpent kin. He wins an audience with Janamejaya and asks him to end the snake sacrifice, refusing wealth in its place. The king finally grants that request. The story makes an appeal for relatives more valuable than a material reward. This is literary narrative, not a verified historical event.

Sources: [Mahabharata, Volume I, Adi Parva — Astika Parva, sections LIV–LVIII](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15474). Adi Parva, Astika Parva, sections LIV, LVI and LVIII.

## The calendar ending changes between accounts

Mahanti attaches the rescue story to Sravana Panchami. Samal’s 2019 retelling attaches its ending to Kartika Chaturthi. Their overlapping wording cannot serve as independent confirmation of either association. The inspected Ganguli sections contain no corresponding month-and-tithi statement. They also name Astika’s mother Jaratkaru; the later accounts name Manasa. These differences require textual comparison, not a silently harmonised story.

## The local katha has identifiable leads

The National Mission for Manuscripts catalogue lists Nagala Chaturthi titles at Odisha State Museum under CY-666, ORPR-66, ORPR-209 and CY-2106, plus a composite record containing that title. These are five catalogue records, not five inspected or independently established versions. Copy dates, authorship and textual relationships remain unknown. OdiaPortal advertises a Raghunath Das book, but its full text is app-only.

## Significance and practical knowledge

Read together, the offerings, family prayers and interruption of violence suggest questions about care and coexistence. This is an editorial reading. To study practical knowledge, we need named local accounts of image-making, grain preparation, storage, fasting and sharing. The sources do not establish conservation outcomes, nutritional benefits or protection from snakebite. Reported live-snake customs are descriptions, not visitor activities recommended by this guide.

## History and a seasonal visit remain open

The complete Odia Nagala katha and its imprint have not yet been inspected. The epic episode cannot date the Odisha observance, and the modern publication dates establish only those publications. Kartika is a reading window here: no annual date, public host, serving place or food availability has been verified.
