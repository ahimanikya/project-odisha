# Prathamastami

Prathamastami brings the eldest child into the centre of family attention. New clothes arrive from the maternal household, an older woman offers blessings, and enduri parcels carry a recognisable food tradition into the celebration. Looking closely at those parcels also reveals variation: an offering menu, a published recipe and a gift between friends are different kinds of evidence.

**Narrative checkpoint; history and local review remain incomplete.** Traditional month: Margasira. No annual visit date is confirmed.

## The child, the mother and the maternal uncle

Mahanti describes the eldest child, a girl or boy, wearing new clothes and sitting on a wooden pidha before a kalasha. A mother or older woman performs bandapana and prays to Sasthi for the child’s long life. The maternal uncle sends clothing and worship materials. Enduri and kheeri are offered as bhoga. This account describes a family relationship through gifts, blessing and food; it is not a survey of every household.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed59 / PDF75, Prathamastami osha.

## An Odia account names a fuller offering

In her 8 January 2018 essay, Namita Sarangi describes bathing and dressing the eldest child in clothes from the maternal home, then offering turmeric-leaf enduri, radish dalma, kheeri and fish to Sasthi before bandapana. Her locality and household sample are not specified. The fish reference belongs to her Prathamastami description; dried fish in the following Kanji Anla passage has a separate role. Neither a universal vegetarian menu nor a requirement to include fish follows from this account.

## What happens inside the leaf

Lipi Das’s 2015 ICAR-CRRI compilation documents a rice–black-gram batter and a filling containing coconut and paneer, with jaggery or sugar, cardamom and pepper. The filled turmeric leaves are folded and steamed. The book describes both a steamer and parcels placed on cloth tied across a pot’s mouth under a lid. Odisha Tourism’s shorter description names coconut and jaggery. These are attributed descriptions, not one compulsory recipe; the dairy ingredient in the book’s version should remain visible. No recipe testing or health benefit is claimed here.

Sources: [Traditional Rice Foods: The Rich Heritage of India — Lipi Das](https://icar-crri.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/final_book_ldas.pdf), [The Taste of Odisha](https://odishatourism.gov.in/content/tourism/en/the-taste-of-odisha.html). ## Food also travels between people

Soumya’s Svanir blog recalls receiving enduri from Sunanda aunty, a friend of the writer’s mother-in-law, and learning about the occasion through family conversation. ## Kinship in a temple setting

IGNCA’s Bharati Matha inventory records Prathamastami among the site’s rituals and describes Lingaraja visiting his uncle at the matha.
