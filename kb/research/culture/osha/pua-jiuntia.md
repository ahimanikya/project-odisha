# Pua Jiuntia

A mother’s vow, a thread and a shared meal provide an entry into this western Odisha observance.

**Research note.** Practice and food accounts are documented; full ritual stories and earliest histories remain open.

## A different vow from Bhai Jiuntia

Mahanti places mothers’ worship of Dutibahana on Ashwina’s dark-fortnight eighth day. Bhai Jiuntia follows on the bright-fortnight eighth, with sisters praying to Durga for brothers. Similar names do not make the observances interchangeable.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed p.49 / PDF65.

## Offerings and the following meal

Mahanti names puffed-rice jugara and moong-dal tikhiri as offerings. His following-day account adds a twenty-one-vegetable curry, suji manda and kheeri, shared with relatives and friends. This records a sequence, not a verified standardized recipe.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed p.50 / PDF66.

## A dated Barpali account

E Kiran Mohan’s 2011 account describes mothers assembling with plates of thread, grass, juggar and twenty vegetables. Its opening caption identifies a gathering at Tehsil Chowk, Barpali, on 1 October 2010. This is a dated local witness; it does not date the beginning of the custom.

Sources: [PUA JIUNTIA [ପୁଅ ଜୀଉନ୍ତିଆ]](https://barpalidays.blogspot.com/2011/08/pua-juintia.html). Published13August2011; opening caption and ritual-plate paragraph.

## Keep the differences visible

Mohan describes Rengal-pattar wrapping and an eight-knotted thread worn at the child’s neck. Mahanti describes sal-leaf wrapping and wrist tying. The accounts also differ between twenty and twenty-one vegetables. Geography, household custom or description may explain these differences, but the evidence does not settle which.
