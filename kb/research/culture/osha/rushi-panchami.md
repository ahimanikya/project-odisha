# Rushi Panchami

Rushi Panchami brings gratitude to the sages into Odisha’s ritual year. Its Puri accounts connect listening to a sacred story with offerings at Markandeswar. That temple setting gives food a specific ritual role; household meals still need their own evidence.

**Research note: temple calendar, an attributed story and offering descriptions; full local katha, origin and household food remain open.**

## A place in the ritual calendar

Mahimohan Tripathy’s July2003 temple-festival survey places Rishi Panchami on the fifth day of Bhadraba’s bright fortnight. The2021 Lakshmi essay lists Rishipanchami among her temple festivals. These dated publications support the traditional calendar and association, not a date or programme for a particular year.

## The seven sages and a short sacred story

Tulasi Ojha’s2022 essay names Kashyapa, Atri, Bharadwaja, Vishwamitra, Gautama, Jamadagni and Vasishtha. She links their worship to respect for inherited sacred learning. Her brief story tells of a Brahmin couple reborn as a bull and a female dog, released through service to the seven sages. This is an attributed ritual narrative, not a historical event or a complete recovered brata-katha.

Sources: [Tithi Panchami: Eka Anudhyana — Guru Panchami Katha and Rishi Panchami sections](https://fliphtml5.com/aukd/fzar/February_2022/). Printed108 / viewer110.

## Mahalakshmi at Markandeswar

Ojha describes Sridevi travelling from the Ratnasimhasana to Markandeswar tank. After ceremonial bathing, Varuna and the seven sages are invoked; she hears the Rishi Panchami story read by the Pujapanda and returns after arati.

Sources: [Tithi Panchami: Eka Anudhyana — Guru Panchami Katha and Rishi Panchami sections](https://fliphtml5.com/aukd/fzar/February_2022/). Printed109 / viewer111.

## A story pictured in the temple

Acharya’s2013 article describes a painting inside the Jagamohan showing Lakshmi hearing the Rishipanchami vow from Markandeya. He also lists Rishipanchami among occasions linking Markandeswar’s rites with Jagannath temple. A depicted sage and a performing priest occupy different levels of this account: one belongs to the sacred scene, the other to the ritual description. The article does not date the painting.

Sources: [The Temple of Markandeswar of Puri](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/2013/jul/engpdf/105-109.pdf). Printed106–107 / PDF3–4.

## Food as an offering

Ojha names milk, banana and other fruit as naivedya. Her passage supplies neither a complete recipe nor a household fast-breaking menu. She also reports women observing the vow in relation to menstrual-purity beliefs; these are attributed beliefs, not judgments about women’s bodies. A temple offering cannot establish what every household eats.

Sources: [Tithi Panchami: Eka Anudhyana — Guru Panchami Katha and Rishi Panchami sections](https://fliphtml5.com/aukd/fzar/February_2022/). Printed108–109 / viewer110–111.

## What the published record can and cannot date

The2003 calendar is the earliest dated publication inspected in this bounded pass that explicitly lists Rishi Panchami. It is not the earliest evidence in existence or a founding date. Dash’s2006 inventory separately names Roosi Panchami and Guru Panchami. The conflicting Guru accounts remain in their own profile; similarity in sages or calendar does not settle local equivalence. Older temple records and identifiable Odia katha editions are still needed.

## Why listening matters

Read together, the descriptions invite a cultural question: how do a narrated story, a painted scene and a shared offering sustain respect for learning? This is an editorial interpretation. The next stage should ask willing participants how they understand the vow, who prepares the offerings, and how family practices differ. It should preserve disagreement instead of turning one temple account into a statewide rule.
