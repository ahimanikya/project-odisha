# Shani / Tota Osha

Shani or Tota Osha introduces a different rhythm to the food calendar: a Saturday vow rather than one annual festival season.

**Early research notes: complete katha, dated history and local variation remain open.** No annual visit date is confirmed.

## Why a mango grove?

Dash’s April 2006 inventory names Shani Osha and Tota Osha together and places Saturday worship in a mango grove. Mahanti explains the grove or temple setting through a belief that Shani should remain outside the home. This is an attributed explanation of the name and setting, not a demonstrated origin history.

## A promise with a beginning and an end

Mahanti describes fasting and Saturday worship for a personally vowed period, without a fixed month. An udyapana concludes the vow on its last Saturday. The account does not prescribe one duration for everyone.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed54 / PDF70, opening paragraph and note36.

## Food and ritual materials have different roles

The Tota Osha passage names black-gram khichidi as an offering, alongside black cloth and tila oil. Mahanti’s separate Saturday account describes eating black-gram kechidi with sesame seeds. These are two passages in one book, not independent confirmations of a recipe. Oil listed for worship is not automatically a cooking ingredient.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed54 / PDF70 and printed64 / PDF80.

## A Hanuman story in the Saturday account

Mahanti recounts a legend in which Hanuman rescues Shani from Ravana during the burning of Lanka. Shani then promises to spare Hanuman’s devotees. This is context supplied in the Saturday section; it is not an inspected complete Tota Osha katha or a verified passage from an identified epic edition.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed64 / PDF80, Sanibara paragraph.

## Shani Osha and Sanishchara Mela

Dash separately lists Shani/Tota among oshas and Sanishchara among melas. We retain those source categories. A shared deity and weekday do not settle whether all local performances, stories and offerings are interchangeable. Dash’s general discussion of rice and black-gram cakes is not a Shani-specific menu.
