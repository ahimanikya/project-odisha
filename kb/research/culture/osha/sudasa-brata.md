# Sudasa Brata

A knotted thread and an offering of manda connect a recurring vow with Lakshmi.

**Narrative research checkpoint**. The traditional rule is a Thursday coinciding with the bright fortnight’s tenth lunar day. No annual visitor date is confirmed.

## The vow and its objects

The Mohantys describe a thread with ten knots, each associated with a name of Lakshmi. Mahanti gives the calendrical condition as a Thursday coinciding with the bright fortnight’s tenth lunar day; it can recur within a year. Dash’s Kartika-only description is therefore retained as a conflict, not used as the general rule.

Sources: [Laxmi: The Goddess of Prosperity and Salvation](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/2021/June-July/engpdf/page-21-25.pdf), [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf), [The Perishing Tradition of Osha, Brata and Mela in Orissa: An Overview](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/April2006/engpdf/The_perishing_tradition_of_osha.pdf). Mohanty p.25; Mahanti p.43; Dash p.15.

## Manda in the offering

Dash names manda for Sudasa. The existing food record describes a steamed version, but that does not establish the recipe of every ritual offering. The useful questions concern dough, filling, cooking method, number and distribution. A dish name can remain stable while its preparation varies.

Sources: [The Perishing Tradition of Osha, Brata and Mela in Orissa: An Overview](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/April2006/engpdf/The_perishing_tradition_of_osha.pdf). Printed pp.17–18.

## Two stories around the vow

In the scanned verse booklet, a kite takes a Brahmin woman’s ritual thread and the king gives her the queen’s brata; Nala and Damayanti later endure hardship before renewal and restoration. In the typed prose, a cat steals a merchant wife’s manda and King Sudarshana obtains a replacement from Queen Sudasha’s offering. The merchant’s wife later helps the queen renew the vow. The thread and the cake carry different narrative roles. Neither story establishes a historical event.

Sources: [Damayantinkara Sudasha Brata](https://www.odiabooks.com/sudasha-brata-katha-pdf-ebook.html), [Shri Sudasha Brata Katha — anonymous typed prose copy](https://www.dropbox.com/s/c0u5sg3lcm9y8b5/sudashabrata_txt.pdf?dl=1). Verse printed pp.1–12; prose PDF pp.1–4.

## What history can and cannot say

The sources connect the brata to Lakshmi devotion, but no earliest Odisha performance is established here. Mahanti’s claim of revival by Balarama Das still needs a dated textual witness. The two recovered copies tell different stories, so neither is treated as a single authoritative original.

Sources: [Fading Festivals of Odisha: A peep into the religious practices in Odisha](https://www.esamskriti.com/essays/pdf/17%20FINAL%20Fading%20Festivals%20of%20Odisha%20%281%29.pdf). Printed p.43.

## Reading its significance

A useful editorial lens is the relationship between an enduring object and repeated action: a thread can remind its wearer of a vow between observances. This is a question to explore with practitioners, not an assertion that everyone gives the thread the same meaning.

## A printed offering passage

An undated verse booklet naming Damayanti in its title describes ten manda with chhena and coconut filling. Damayanti is a character in its story, not an established author credit. The passage supplies a textual food reference while leaving the cooking method unresolved; it does not prove the observance’s starting date or a recipe followed by every household.

Sources: [Damayantinkara Sudasha Brata](https://www.odiabooks.com/sudasha-brata-katha-pdf-ebook.html). Printed pp.2–3, especially verse17 on p.2; PDF p.3.

## Rice and filling in another textual witness

The saved Odia Lakshmi Purana transcription introduces Sudasa separately from Manabasa. It instructs soaking arua rice and pounding it into flour, then making ten manda with banana, coconut, chhena and other filling ingredients. The cooking method remains unspecified. This adds a textual preparation reference alongside the booklet’s chhena-and-coconut passage, without establishing which text is earlier.

Source: [Odia textual witness](https://or.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=ମାଣବସା_ଲକ୍ଷ୍ମୀ_ପୁରାଣ&oldid=57790). Revision57790, displayed57–68, especially66–67; scanned booklet printed p.2, verse17.


## Who receives this offering

The transcription tells the observer to eat the prasada and withhold it from others, explicitly including a married daughter. This restriction belongs to the Sudasa instructions in this witness. It is distinct from Lakshmi’s later demand for food sharing across caste boundaries in the story, and does not establish how households distribute offerings today.

Source: [Odia textual witness](https://or.wikisource.org/w/index.php?title=ମାଣବସା_ଲକ୍ଷ୍ମୀ_ପୁରାଣ&oldid=57790). Revision57790, displayed68–69; compare477–479.
