# Pala: poetry with people around it

![A standing performer at a microphone gestures towards a seated gathering beneath a decorated canopy.](media/pala-nalconagar.webp)

*A Pala performance at Nalconagar, Odisha, in 2013. The performer is not identified here. Photograph: Subhashish Panigrahi; source and licence below.*

Look at the photograph: the gathering sits close to the performer. A hand is raised, a microphone waits, and faces turn towards the same point. The image cannot tell us which verse was being performed or what anyone was thinking. But it can suggest a question worth taking into an evening of Pala: **what happens to a poem when it has people around it?**

## The reader behind the voice

Jagannath Behera's 2009 Sangeet Natak Akademi citation describes reading across Odia, Bengali, Hindi, Sanskrit and English and bringing literary quotations into performance. That is one artist's documented practice at the time of an award—not a requirement for every performer.[^pala-sna-behera2009]

It gives the listener something to follow. Behind a voice may be years of reading, remembering and finding a way to carry a passage to an audience. Ask which work is being presented and why the performer chose it. A title can become your next book; a performance can become your way into literature.

## An evening has more than one part

The Kalahandi district table describes Palla around Bhawanipatna, Dharamgarh and M. Rampur, naming Gayak, Bayak and Palias with Mrudanga, Gini and Chamar. It lists six participants and lyrical narration of Puranic and historical events. This is a local description, not a universal cast list.[^daskathia-kalahandi]

Try following someone other than the lead performer for a moment. When do they join in? What changes in the rhythm or the exchange? Ask how their contribution fits the story. The person at the microphone is not the whole performance.

Pala and [Daskathia](daskathia.md) share routes into telling aloud; their names and forms deserve to remain distinct.

## Literature in public life

A Chief Minister's office report dated 28 December 2025 credits Pala singers and Bayaks with bringing Odia poetry to the public at the 48th state-level Pala Sammilan in Kendujhar. This records a dated gathering and its stated cultural purpose. It does not tell us attendance, earnings or today's event schedule.[^pala-cm-sammilan2025]

For Utkal's reader, the invitation is smaller and more personal: find one work you would like to hear explained. Ask a host whether the story and language will be accessible to you. If you need an introduction, request it beforehand; nobody owes a visitor an explanation in the middle of a performance.

## Three things to carry into a listening evening

- **A question:** which poem, episode or text will we hear?
- **An introduction:** who performs each part, and how would they like to be credited?
- **A little patience:** let a phrase unfold before reaching for a translation or your phone.

Confirm the actual occasion, duration and access with the host. Ask before recording, and respect a refusal. No current booking or performance invitation has been verified for this article.

A rewarding evening might leave you with one unfamiliar title and one artist's name. That is enough to start another journey.

# Pala and its culture

Pala belongs at the meeting point of performed poetry, musical storytelling and community life. A 2012 festival account connects these ballad traditions with literature, rhythm, humour and epic narratives. [Pala and Daskathia Utsav](https://www.narthaki.com/info/rev12/rev1245.html).

## How to begin listening

Listen for the story, the explanation of a verse and the exchange between the lead voice and accompanying performers. The official 2025 Sammilan account explicitly credits singers and Bayaks for communicating Odia poetry. [48th annual state-level Pala Sammilan at Parklane, Kendujhar](https://cm.odisha.gov.in/en/latest-news/participated-48th-annual-state-level-pala-sammilan-organized-parklane-kendujhar). Detailed roles, instruments and local terminology need an attributed demonstration before becoming a performance manual.

## Forms and history: preserve the source’s vocabulary

Dash’s January 2011 article distinguishes **Pothi Pala** performed seated, **Thia Pala** performed standing, and **Badi Pala**, where singers take turns on a stage. These notes come from the indexed passage at printed page 73; direct PDF access failed. [The Socio-Cultural Value of Odia Folkdance: Pala](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/2011/Jan/engpdf/72-74.pdf).

A full history needs separately examined evidence for Satyanarayana/Satyapir associations, manuscript dates, teacher lineages and later stage practice. Those are research questions here. A devotional origin account cannot supply a verified founding date; older claims of decline do not measure the present condition.

## A living archive and a dated gathering

Its [Pala Kabita catalogue](https://sjprf.in/index.php/pala-kabita) records author attributions, including Bhagabata Bandana by Sarat Chandra Dash. These are useful discovery routes; recordings, editions, translations and rights need item-level checks.
