Sagada, <span lang="or">ଶଗଡ଼</span>, is the Odia word for a cart.[^1] A photograph in B. G. Yadav’s 1978 study of bullock-cart transport in Odisha shows a load above the frame and a log suspended beneath it.[^2]

## Above and below the frame

Yadav explains the compromise: a high platform takes more effort to load from above, but leaves room for a heavy load beneath. The photograph shows one use for that space.[^2]

He also relates wheel diameter and rim width to the ground beneath the cart: load and contact area affect how far a wheel sinks. His concluding discussion brings local materials, cost and available artisans into the design question.[^2]

## Keeping a cart working

A later discussion gives repair a place alongside design. In a contribution to West African animal-traction proceedings published in 1993, J. S. Adeoti considers the animal and the transport device together. He discusses bearings, harnesses and brakes, as well as facilities for manufacture and repair.[^3]

This is a broader technical context, separate from Yadav’s Odisha case. It helps frame a question about the cart’s working life: can its parts be maintained where it is used?
