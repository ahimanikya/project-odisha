# When Odisha Became a Homepage

I remember getting to know Debasmita Misra through ORNET, the mailing list that connected Odias across countries. I think we began talking soon after I joined IIT Bombay in September 1996. I was a Research Associate at Ada Labs under Prof. Deepak Phatak. Internet access was still unusual in India, and being there gave me a chance to explore it.

The Oriya Homepage appears to have begun in 1995. I knew it during my IIT Bombay days. Debasmita and I worked together on its redesign in 1998.

In an email dated 3 February 1998, I told him I had been “a silent observer of the page since sept 96.”

![Debasmita Misra in the good old days.](https://archive.projectodisha.org/article-media/when-odisha-became-a-homepage/image9.jpg)

Debasmita Misra in the good old days.

The surviving January 1998 exchange starts with something small. I was in Bangalore and asked Debasmita to update the link to my personal homepage. He looked at my Java applets and asked whether he could use some of them on the Oriya Homepage. He particularly liked the clock showing the time in Odisha.

I offered to help. We began discussing a redesign that would make the page load faster. On 28 January, his subject line was “Welcome Aboard.” He welcomed me as co-host and said it was a relief to have someone help with a site that had grown difficult to manage alone.

On 1 February I sent him a design plan. Before getting into the details, I asked if I could call him “Bhaina,” since he was older than me. Soon the emails were going back and forth as Bapu and Bhaina.

I worked on a copy on my Linux PC and put it online for him to review. We agreed to tell each other what we were changing and leave notes with the files. The plan included simpler navigation, smaller images, fewer broken links and better ways for visitors to send us material.

A surprising amount of our conversation was about how long a page took to appear. In one email I worked out how replacing a 56 KB animation with smaller pictures and an applet could save 35 KB. That was worth writing a long email about.

Debasmita made graphics and commented on the look of the pages. I worked on the applets, navigation and the way the files fitted together. We tested, sent suggestions and sometimes had to undo things. He wanted Jagannath’s eyes to look right. I wanted the images to load quickly. Both concerns kept coming back.

On 6 March I asked him for a new background so the redesign would look different. He sent one on 9 March. After I suggested trying a light background colour, he sent a revised version on 10 March. Both files were still attached to the emails when I opened them again.

There was room for teasing too. When he suggested putting my name before his, I asked whether he was trying to escape. He replied that we would be “partners in the same crime.”

The versions that survive show how much the homepage changed. The pale background from our March emails is visible in the December 1998 capture. By May 2001, the homepage displayed through odiya.org had changed again.

We planned the release for Saturday, 14 March 1998. That day I told a visitor who was testing the forum that I would make the new site public by the evening. In another message dated 14 March, I told Bhaina that the counter, chat, forum and applets were working. There were still broken links in the children’s section to sort out.

On 16 March I sent the announcement to ORNET: “We released a new version of the Oriya Home Page.” I listed thirteen sections, from songs and dance to poetry, children’s pages and people’s memories. We had a chat room and a Java forum. I also suggested using a text browser for anyone who needed information quickly on a slow connection.

It was still a work in progress. People told us what worked and what did not. Some liked the new look; others found it slow or had trouble with the applets. We kept making changes. On 18 March I told him I had changed the front-page layout and added a guestbook.

A few days earlier, when we were talking about criticism, I had written: “What I did is for a mass not for you, not for me.” We were making something for other Odias to use.

![Our names together on the homepage, 1998.](https://archive.projectodisha.org/article-media/when-odisha-became-a-homepage/image5.jpg)

Our names together on the homepage, 1998.

On 5 March, I thanked Bhaina for the opportunity to work on the homepage and told him how much it meant to me as an Odia.

The messages from readers help explain it. In April, Joyshree Mansinha wrote from New York that she had little contact with other Odias and enjoyed visiting the homepage. She wanted to contribute memories of growing up in Rambag. There were people on the other side of these pages, carrying their own memories of home.

There were unexpected requests too. In February, John H. Jenkins wrote from Apple, saying he was working on Unicode support for the Macintosh. He had found our site and asked how to write “Think different” in Odia. Around the same time I was asking about fonts and a free Odia editor.

Later in 1998, I moved to the United States. This picture was taken in Fort Lauderdale, after the move. Back in March I had told Bhaina that I did not have a scanned photograph of myself.

Ahimanikya Satapathy, Fort Lauderdale, Florida, 1998.

Bikash Panda called it the first website in his article, “Orissa on the Web,” and credited Debasmita and me. Debasmita had built and maintained the earlier site; I joined him as co-host, and we worked together on the 1998 redesign.

There were other people building an Odia presence online too. Priyadarsan Patra, Somdutt Behura and Chitta Baral appear in the early records. Ashutosh Dutta helped with hosting at Columbia. People shared material, offered space and wrote back with suggestions. Our work belonged to that wider effort.

The emails have brought back the ordinary parts of it: waiting for a reply, asking for a different colour, trying to arrange a chat across time zones. In one letter Bhaina sent his best wishes “as a brother and a good friend.”

## References and image notes

Website screenshots are modern captures of Wayback Machine replays. Their dates identify archive captures, not design launch dates; replay images may come from other capture dates. The two March 1998 background files are original email attachments. Photograph dates are distinguished from archive dates below.

[Bikash Panda, “Orissa on the Web”](https://web.archive.org/web/20010302181224/http://www.stpbh.soft.net:80/souvenir/orissa.htm). Attributes the first website in its account of Odisha’s early web presence to Debasmita and Ahimanikya. The emails distinguish Debasmita’s earlier site from their 1998 redesign. Article captured 2 March 2001; publication date unverified. This is not exhaustive proof of priority.
