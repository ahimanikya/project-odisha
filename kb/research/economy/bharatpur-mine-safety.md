# Bharatpur: working lives and mine-safety evidence

**Safety belongs in Odisha’s mining story alongside output and public revenue.** The Ministry of Coal’s 2020 committee agenda records four deaths at Bharatpur opencast mine in Angul on 23 July 2019. Its CIL account describes 13 people at the immediate worksite; nine escaped and were rescued. These are event counts, not a workforce total or annual accident rate. [Agenda V, p.51](https://www.coal.nic.in/sites/default/files/2020-09/Annexure11022020.pdf).

The account describes failure of a coal barrier retaining an overburden dump. It also says a specialist study was needed to establish the geotechnical contribution conclusively. The recommendations concern site-specific design, slope monitoring, drainage and hazard planning; their presence does not demonstrate implementation. [Agenda V, pp.52–53](https://www.coal.nic.in/sites/default/files/2020-09/Annexure11022020.pdf).

A later parliamentary answer lists the same date and four deaths. We count the event once and do not assume an independent investigation simply because two publications report it. [Rajya Sabha, Question 835, Annexure I, p.3](https://sansad.in/getFile/annex/262/AU835.pdf?source=pqars).

## What further evidence would change this account

The final DGMS inquiry, original restriction/reopening orders, corrective-action verification and comparable hours-worked data remain unverified. No current safety rating, legal liability, injury total or successful mine closure is inferred. “Recovery” in the rescue account means recovery of people; it is not restoration of mined land.
