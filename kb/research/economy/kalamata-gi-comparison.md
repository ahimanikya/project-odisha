## What the specification makes explicit

The [Greek specification](../sources/gi-kalamata-specification.md), selected sections 2–4 and 7–8, describes extra-virgin oil mainly from Koroneiki, with at most 5% Mastoeidis. It sets an acidity ceiling of 0.50 grams oleic acid per 100 grams oil and defines the production geography as Messinia’s administrative area.

Its origin trail connects producer and parcel declarations to daily mill receipts, harvested quantities, harvest dates, tanks and oil dispatches. Packing records connect incoming oil with its mill and outgoing sales; package identifiers provide another trace. The text names OPEGEP and Messinia’s agricultural/veterinary directorate as control bodies. These are documentary requirements and institutional names in that edition, not an audit of every operator or a current contact directory.

## The boundary was tested through opposition

The [2015 Commission decision](../sources/gi-kalamata-decision2015.md), adopted 13 August and published 14 August, approved the amendment following objections to extending the area from Kalamata province to Messinia. Recitals 11–18 explain the Commission’s assessment of geographic and product evidence. This is the decision-maker’s assessment; Utkal has not independently reanalysed the underlying samples.

**Production geography and bottling geography differ.** Recital 15 accepts bottling outside the delimited area in this case: the applicants had not proposed a mandatory local-bottling restriction. The earlier framework shorthand about an entirely local chain should therefore not be read as requiring every packing step to remain there.

Recital 19 also corrects the amendment application’s **average acidity for “rest of Messinia” from 0.49 to 0.37**. That correction concerns a comparison-table average; it does not revise the separate0.50grams-per 100 grams specification ceiling. Preserve the original value and the correction together. ## A reported enforcement action, with its outcome limits

On 19 October 2021, the [Greek ministry reported](../sources/gi-kalamata-enforcement2021.md) that inspections and cross-checks found 20,620 kg of uncertified extra-virgin oil marketed in Britain as Kalamata PDO. It reported withdrawal of the business’s certification for private-label marketing of that product, referral for sanctions, and a call for the labelled products to be recalled.

The report establishes the ministry’s account of a decision. It does **not** verify a completed recall, final penalty, present business status or the proportion of all operators inspected. The quantity belongs to this reported incident; it is not annual output or exports. The same release’s separate feta-fines paragraph must not be attributed to olive oil. No producer premium, income effect or scheme-wide success rate is established.
