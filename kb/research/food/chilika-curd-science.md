# Chilika curd: traditional practice and fermentation evidence

**Chilika curd connects practical food knowledge with laboratory fermentation research.** The evidence is strongest for particular microbial isolates and studied samples; it is not a blanket guarantee about every batch sold today.

This study review informs the existing Manikapatna Curd application dossier. [Application2117 review](manikapatna-curd-application.md). The two names have overlapping evidence, but their producer populations and geographic coverage have not been proved identical.

## The 2013 study: what was sampled and tested

[Nanda and colleagues](../sources/chilika-curd-nanda2013.md) studied ten milk and ten curd samples from Chilika Buffalo Society farmers around Satapada. They transported samples refrigerated, then followed curd at unspecified room temperature for14days. Collection dates, seasonal coverage, numbers of distinct farms and a numerical storage-temperature range were not reported. Its October2013 publication is not a current field survey.

The paper records preparation in woven bamboo bowls coated with previous curd, dried in the sun, filled with concentrated milk and covered with leaves (printed708–709). This documents a practice; no controlled experiment separately establishes the effects of bamboo, sun treatment or buffalo diet. Those mechanisms remain proposed explanations in the discussion. The household knowledge deserves credit without attributing modern molecular understanding to its practitioners.

The researchers used chemical measurements, culture-based microbiology and targeted PCR. They recovered64 microbial isolates across milk and curd. Of18 identified as Lactobacillus, six showed stronger and two weaker inhibition against one test yeast, *Candida parapsilosis* NCDC279. The remaining ten were not reported as positive. These are isolate counts, not species richness, producer counts or protection against all food hazards.

The authors reported sensory stability for seven days and a curd pH of3.87±0.07 (printed710). This confirms the number reproduced in the2026 application. It does not reconcile that result with the applicant's proposed pH4.2–4.8 range. The paper does not identify what the reported plus/minus represents; this review does not label it a confidence interval or standard deviation.

A14-day observation period is not a14-day shelf life. Small samples, unspecified room temperature and researcher sensory assessment also do not establish a universal seven-day storage instruction. The authors' broader cold-chain and health suggestions are not adopted as validated advice or commercial specifications here.

## What the later study adds

The [2024 follow-up](../sources/chilika-curd-kumar2024.md) examined six previously isolated cultures and investigated C-14 in more detail. It reported fungal inhibition and organic acids in laboratory culture liquid. This is additional experimental work on earlier isolates, not an independent survey of today's producers. The methods name C-8 where Table1 names C-10; neither identity is silently corrected. Culture-liquid measurements are not the nutrient composition of a bowl of curd.

|---|---|---|

| Producer and vessel-making connections | Measure costs, spoilage, maker payments and buyer demand with consent | Current revenue, profitable margins or a GI price premium |

These next steps are editorial research proposals. No cultures have been acquired, experiments commissioned, partners contacted or product rights granted. The evidence cannot establish national uniqueness by itself; comparison with other fermented foods requires comparable experiments and original sources.

The pH hold remains open, now with its original source confirmed. The separate2024 strain-identity hold prevents accidental merging of culture records. Current storage advice, safety claims and an agreed Manikapatna specification remain unestablished.
