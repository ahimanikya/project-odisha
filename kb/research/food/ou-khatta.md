## A cook’s two versions and a remembered meal · reviewed 4 November 2026

In her 2017 sweet elephant-apple relish, Sasmita cooks and drains the fruit before moving it to another pan. Cumin and mustard seeds temper the oil; ginger, jaggery or sugar, and coconut follow. She also allows the cooked pieces to be crushed. Even within this recipe, texture is a choice.[^food-ou-sasmita2017]

Her 2019 sweet-and-sour version uses a mustard–cumin paste alongside panch phoron in the oil. She blends the paste separately; the whole spices go into hot oil before the cooked fruit is added. The precooked, drained fruit then meets the paste and jaggery. She suggests rice and dalma alongside it. These are two recipes by the same cook, offering variation rather than evidence from two households.[^food-ou-sasmita2019]

Julie Acharya Ray’s 2014 memoir places ou khatta in her mother’s Kartik meal. On banana leaves, rice and dal occupy the centre, with vegetable dishes and ou khatta around them. This is one family’s remembered arrangement, not a menu for every household.[^food-ou-ray2014]
