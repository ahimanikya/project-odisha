# Next evidence needed for a global trading story

## Highest-value joins

1. **Odisha → country → product → year:** obtain official state-of-origin exports at HS product-code level, in both value and quantity, for China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam and Sri Lanka. Retain state-of-origin methodology, revisions, currencies and complete annual series. This will identify real product relationships rather than relying on country-level symbolism.
2. **Product → producer → buyer:** identify public, attributable commercial examples in metals, seafood, textiles and food. Confirm actual shipments or contracts before treating plans or trade-fair meetings as outcomes.
3. **Festival → seller → sales:** obtain ORMAS and district closing reports, definitions of sales, participating-stall counts and event dates. Separate visitor entries from unique people; avoid economic-impact multipliers without a study.
4. **Object → excavation → origin:** retrieve Manikapatna and Khalkatapatna catalogues, accession identifiers, dating methods and museum locations. Ask a qualified archaeologist to review the proposed public claims.
5. **Inscription → transcription → translation:** pursue the Kling/Cane and related Javanese leads through original editions and current scholarship. Historical ethnonyms need contextual interpretation.
6. **Cultural influence:** investigate one named practice or object at a time with Indonesian and other regional scholarship. Similarity alone does not establish source, direction or exclusive authorship.

## Measurement plan

For future campaigns, track qualified enquiries, maker referrals, completed purchases, export leads progressing to confirmed orders and visitor bookings with consented attribution. Set a baseline before launch. Historical content is an entry point; business outcomes need contemporary measurement.

This is a research queue, not evidence that ancient trade volumes, trader counts or market shares are known. The initial maritime module is not an exhaustive archive of all Odisha history.
