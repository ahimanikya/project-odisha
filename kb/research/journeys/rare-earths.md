# Odisha’s rare-earth journey

## The reader’s takeaway

**An existing processing base, with a new corridor ambition.**

IREL lists **11,200 tonnes per year of installed mixed rare-earth chloride capacity** at OSCOM in Odisha. That is plant capacity, not measured annual output or pure rare-earth oxide tonnage.[^journey-irel-reep]

## Milestones

- **1986:** IREL commissioned the OSCOM mining and mineral-separation unit at Chatrapur.[^journey-irel-history]
- **FY2015–16:** IREL’s plant profile dates commissioning of its rare-earth extraction plant to this financial year.[^journey-irel-reep]
- **Union Budget 2026–27:** Odisha was named alongside Kerala, Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu for proposed dedicated rare-earth corridors. An April 2026 parliamentary reply said a joint working group was being constituted to develop guidelines.[^journey-rare-corridor]

- **11 August 2026:** a ministerial meeting discussed an integrated science roadmap linking the proposed corridor with marine biotechnology and other research. This release does not establish corridor commissioning or new plant output.[^weekly-rare-aug2026]

## Meaning and presentation

Show two clearly labelled layers: **established facilities** and **proposed expansion**. A simple value-chain illustration can explain mineral extraction, chemical processing and downstream manufacturing, but each stage needs its own location and evidence. IREL’s company profile places high-purity oxide refining at Aluva in Kerala; do not label the entire company value chain as located in Odisha.[^journey-irel-history]

The story opportunity is Odisha’s role in higher-value materials. Do not equate all beach-sand minerals with rare earths, or connect rare-earth projects to Odisha semiconductor projects as a confirmed supply relationship without evidence.
