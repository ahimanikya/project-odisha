# Bibhuti Pattanaik: Books Within Reach

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Bibhuti Pattanaik’s answer to a question about readers began with libraries. In a 2016 interview, he proposed making good books available there as a way to encourage Odia reading. He also spoke as a reader himself, naming Manoj Das, Surendra Mohanty and Rabi Pattnaik, and praising [Basanta Kumari Pattanayak](basanta-kumari-patnaik.md)’s *Amada Bata*.[^reading]

That attention to other writers also took a public form. At Sahitya Akademi’s 2013 centenary seminar for Rajkishore Ray in Bhubaneswar, Pattanaik presided and discussed Ray’s works.[^seminar]

Writing about him in 2022, Kedar Mishra recalled the editor behind *Galpa* and his involvement in organising the Bhubaneswar Book Fair. Mishra also described his work teaching Odia language and literature.[^working-life] Books were part of that wider working life: writing them, discussing them and helping make occasions for readers to meet.

## A novel reaches the screen

ବଧୂ ନିରୁପମା (Badhu Nirupama) connects that literary work with cinema. Its publisher’s description dates the novel to 1965 and its Odia film adaptation to 1987.[^badhu] Between those dates lies the distinction at the heart of an adaptation: the book has a life of its own before filmmakers take it up.

The novel can still be approached through a particular edition. IIT Bhubaneswar’s library catalogue identifies a copy published by Odisha Book Store; a separate Black Eagle Books edition supplies another bibliographic route.[^edition][^badhu] For a reader arriving through the film, these records lead back to a book with its author’s name on it.

His joint story credit with Nirad Mohapatra in [ମାୟା ମିରିଗ (Maya Miriga)](../culture/cinema-series/maya-miriga.md) leads to another kind of collaboration.[^bologna]

## Return to the books

His short-story collection *Mahishasurara Muhan* received the **Sahitya Akademi Award for Odia in 2015**.[^award] The collection offers a further reading direction beyond the novels connected with films.

Begin with the form that draws you in: a novel, a short story, or a film that sends you looking for its writer. Then follow a name Pattanaik himself recommended. A writer’s reading can become part of our own.
