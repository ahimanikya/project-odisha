# Biyott Projna Tripathy: Finding a Story’s Form

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

An actor’s work can depend on knowing when to hold something back. Recalling the making of *ବିଦେହ (Bideha)*, Kajal Satpathy described how Biyott Projna Tripathy guided dialogue and scenes through small emotional choices: when to cry, when to smile and when to restrain a feeling.[^performance] Her account brings directing close to the person who must speak the words.

Speaking about *Bideha*, Tripathy emphasises the close relationship between literature and cinema, and the stories that literature can offer filmmakers.[^literature-outlook] Her own work brings that interest alongside directing actors and collaborating with poets and musicians.

## A writer on the page

Tripathy writes poems, stories and columns in Odia. A 2018 institutional biography also records her work teaching film and media.[^writing] These activities place writing beside conversations about how moving images are made.

Her “Palmful of Stars” appeared in *Indian Literature* in its January–February 2015 issue. In the opening of “Palmful of Stars”, her fictional narrator moves from memories of unwanted touch to a college relationship she initiates. Afternoon cinema visits become part of that remembered intimacy.[^palmful]

Tripathy identifies her own story “ବିଦେହ (Bideha)” as the source of the film. She recalls writing it in 2005 or 2006 and says it appears in her collection *କଡ଼ାପାନ ଓ କ୍ଲିଓପାଟ୍ରା (Kadapana o Cleopatra)*. An Odisha government catalogue lists the collection’s first edition, published by Pakshighara Prakashanee in 2010.[^bideha-story]

## Documentary work

Her English-language documentary *Ladakh, The Land of Mystery* received the National Film Award for **Best Promotional Film for 2003**. Tripathy was named as both its producer and director.

The award citation draws attention to Ladakh’s landscape and people, including their culture, beliefs and daily life. It recognises a film’s way of bringing a place and its inhabitants into view.[^ladakh]

## Words find music and performers

In *Yogini*, Haraprasad Das supplied the concept, lyrics and narration. Omprakash Mohanty composed the music, and Susmita Das sang. Tripathy wrote the screenplay and directed, while she and Susmita Das shared the producing credit. Cinematographer Dhrubanand Panda and choreographer Md. Shafi-Ul-Haque, who shares that credit with Tripathy, also helped give the work its screen form.[^yogini]

These names let us follow distinct kinds of making: a lyric, a musical setting, a voice, movement and the image. The [Literature and Screen](../culture/cinema-series/literature-and-screen.md) reading follows such connections, while [Cinema Music](../culture/cinema-series/cinema-music.md) stays with the writers, composers and singers behind a soundtrack.

## A film needs people to meet it

For the Odia film *Bideha*, Tripathy’s credits include story, screenplay and production. She shares the dialogue and lyrics credits with Devdas Chhotray.[^bideha] Kajal’s recollection adds the actor’s work to that chain: the written scene still needs someone to find its emotional measure.

Tripathy has also spoken directly to potential viewers. In a public appeal for *Bideha*, she described the difficulty of making an independent film and bringing it to theatres, asking people to support it through tickets and word of mouth.[^audience]

The invitation leaves a place for us in this story. We can seek out a writer’s work, notice the collaborators in a film’s credits and tell someone what held our attention. A film’s meeting with an audience begins with people choosing to make time for it.
