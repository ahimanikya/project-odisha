# Deepak Kumar Nayak and Kalinga heritage documentation

Deepak Kumar Nayak’s documented contribution to Odisha heritage research is clearest in field surveys and the publication of their results. His work provides a way to follow Kalinga-related places beyond today’s state boundary while asking how inscriptions are located, read and connected with earlier scholarship. The useful subject for this series is the work and its evidence, including the people who make it possible.

## A book built around North Andhra

INTACH’s April–July 2025 newsletter identifies Nayak as the author of *Relics of Kalinga in South India, Part-I*, jointly edited by Sanjib Chandra Hota and Asoka Kumar Rath. It records the first-edition launch in Cuttack on 27 April 2025 and names B.K. Publications. The volume covers Srikakulam, Vizianagaram, Parvatipuram Manyam, Visakhapatnam and Anakapalle. Further South India volumes were planned; their publication is not established here.[^nayak-intach2025]

The ebook listing gives 304 pages; the launch report describes 307 pages and 57 sites. Keep these source-specific figures separate until the print imprint and ebook pagination can be compared. The launch report credits Nayak as project coordinator, Bishnu Mohan Adhikari as epigrapher, and Bikram Kumar Nayak, Suman Prakash Swain, Bikash Prasad Das and Chiranjib Rout among the survey team. It also names contributor Satya Chandra Saikumar Kethineedi.[^nayak-book-listing][^nayak-launch2025]

## Fieldwork and the earlier record

A March 2024 report describes Nayak and Bikram Kumar Nayak’s work near Dharmapuri in Andhra Pradesh, assisted by local heritage enthusiasts D Santosh and Bijaya Kumar. The same report acknowledges that Satyanarayana Rajaguru had already published the inscription in *Inscriptions of Orissa*, volume 3. This is a valuable example of returning to a recorded object and its setting. The earlier edition has now been located: Rajaguru’s 1961 volume III, part II, entry 232, printed page 261, records the Chodagangesvara temple inscription and cites SII X, no. 702 and AR no. 366 of 1905. Its footnote assigns Saka 1072/1150 CE and treats construction by Chodaganga as probable. The modern object/location match still requires an image and site comparison. See the [history feature](../stories/reading-kalinga-beyond-odisha.md) for the source-linked account.[^nayak-dharmapuri2024]

The 1948 SII volume X scan has now been inspected at entry 702, printed page 368 (PDF page 382). Its English summary describes a land gift supporting a perpetual lamp at Cholagangisvara in Dharmavoramu, Talandi-Twelve, and locates the slab on a riverbank mound at Dvarapureddipalem. Its heading gives Saka 1071; retain this alongside Rajaguru’s later correction to 1072. This is evidence of an endowment, not a demonstrated temple construction date. The Telugu transcription has not been independently deciphered, and no modern stone match is claimed.[^nayak-sii1948]

The October 2024 Chitreswari report names five collaborators: Satyajit Swain, Bikram Kumar Nayak, Mihir Bahinipati, Bishnu Mohan Adhikari and Nayak. Adhikari’s reported reading identifies a sculptor, Sreepati. The report expressly says the inscription supplies no construction date; its early nineteenth-century attribution is based on script. The older sacred narrative and this proposed image date answer different questions. A facsimile and critical edition are needed before adopting the reading as settled history.[^nayak-chitreswari2024]

## From documentation to conservation

Nayak’s Facebook posts describe RLHT stepwell documentation in 2021–22 and later official visits to Ambilijhari, Chanditala and Mahavinayaka sites following his request. This is a lead for a conservation story. A site visit or proposed revival does not establish completed restoration. His Umga temple article in Sunday Sambad and Prachi-valley posts are additional publication leads, awaiting original issues and dated records.[^nayak-facebook]

## Imprint and selected entries verified · 8 November 2026

The publisher’s free sample identifies ISBN978-93-48078-94-0, first edition27April2025, BK Classics (an imprint of B.K. Publications Private Limited), and copyright2025 INTACH Odisha State Chapter. Its team page confirms the named surveyors and Bishnu Mohan Adhikari as epigraphist. Reader page labels differ from printed headers; the existing page-count differences remain unresolved.[^nayak-book-preview]

Selected reading covered the Baidyanath/Pidi Mandasa opening and references (printed3,6) and Siddheswar/Ronanki entry (printed7–9). Pidi Mandasa’s proposed construction chronology includes local tradition. At Ronanki, the author reports from local conversations that a slab was buried under a renovated floor. This is a documented author account, not independently verified destruction or proof of permanent loss. These entries connect survey observations with earlier bibliographies rather than establishing every historical assertion anew.[^nayak-book-preview]

The28March2024 newspaper’s page4 photograph shows one oblique face of an inscribed slab. SII702 provides a typeset two-face transcription, not an old photograph for matching shape. The available image cannot establish character-by-character identity, dimensions or continuity of findspot. Clear photographs of both faces and documented location remain needed.[^nayak-report2024-print]
