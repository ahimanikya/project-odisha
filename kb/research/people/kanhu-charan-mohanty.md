# Kanhu Charan Mohanty: The Household and the Wider World

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

An unpublished manuscript can hold the beginning of a writing life. Kanhu Charan Mohanty wrote *Utshabe Byasane* in 1923–24; it remained unpublished and was later untraced. The books that followed included *Ha Anna* in 1935, *Sasti* in 1946, *Abhinetri* in 1947 and କା (Kaa) in 1956.[^akademi-fellowship] Behind a familiar film title lies this longer companionship with writing.

## Making room for the page

Financial constraints interrupted Mohanty’s engineering studies. He graduated from Ravenshaw in 1929 and entered government service in 1931. Retirement came in 1964, followed by three years as secretary of the Odisha Sahitya Akademi.[^akademi-fellowship]

Writing continued through those working years. The dates place his novels alongside a life of employment, rather than apart from it. There is something worth pausing over here: a book may eventually stand alone on a shelf, but the time given to it belonged to a person carrying other responsibilities too.

*Kaa* received the **Sahitya Akademi Award in 1958**.[^akademi-fellowship] This was recognition of the literary work. The novel’s later life on screen would involve another set of makers and decisions.

## Home opens towards society

A critical discussion in *Orissa Review* connects *Ha Anna* and *Sasti* with famine, its aftermath and social inequality. It also considers widow remarriage in *Nishpatti*, placing rural relationships, poverty and social conventions within Mohanty’s fiction.[^orissa-review-2008]

These concerns suggest why the household can be such a demanding subject for a novelist. Decisions about marriage, food and belonging are intimate, but the freedom to make them is not shared equally. Reading Mohanty offers an opportunity to follow how such pressures enter relationships, rather than treating home as a world sealed off from public life.

His work belongs beside the other writers in this collection without becoming interchangeable with them. [Basanta Kumari Patnaik](basanta-kumari-patnaik.md) brings another attention to domestic life; [Kalindi Charan Panigrahi](kalindi-charan-panigrahi.md) offers another encounter with family and land. Their books leave room for readers to discover differences as well as connections.

## A novel finds other voices

The film *Kaa* credits Mohanty’s novel as its source, **Sarada Naik** for screenplay and dialogue, and **Siddharth** for direction: Ram Chandra Thakur, Gour Ghose and Parbati Ghose.[^gour-parbati-kaa] Following [Parbati Ghose’s work](../culture/cinema-series/parbati-ghose.md) brings that partnership into view.

*ଅଭିନେତ୍ରୀ (Abhinetri)* also reached the screen, in a 1965 film directed by Amar Ganguly under Chhayabani Pratisthan.[^abhinetri-database] These adaptations are part of Mohanty’s continuing presence in cinema. They also offer a reason to return to the novels, where a character’s life unfolds through the writer’s language and the reader’s time.

The [literature-and-cinema reading](../culture/cinema-series/literature-and-screen.md) follows that exchange more widely. [Gopal Chhotray](gopal-chhotray.md)’s career follows the work of finding words for performance. Between a source novel and a finished film, other people must decide what to carry forward and how to give it form.

Mohanty’s place in this story begins with the page. A film may lead us there, but the writing has its own company to offer: households, social questions and lives that ask for more attention than a remembered title alone can give.
