# Manglu Charan Biswal: Words for the Stage and the Page

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

A film can keep a play’s name in circulation. A translation can bring us back to its words. In 2023, Hachette India published *The Starved*, Nirjharini Tripathy’s English translation of Manglu Charan Biswal’s *ଭୂଖା (Bhukha)*.[^translation] For someone who knows the title through cinema, the book offers another encounter: with the writing from which the film began.

Biswal was a teacher, poet and playwright.[^translation] His literary life extended across plays, poems and children’s stories in both Sambalpuri and Odia.[^life] Following him beyond a single adaptation means making room for those different readers and listeners too.

## A place, and a life of writing

He was born in Pitapali in 1935.[^translation] The village was later submerged following the construction of Hirakud Dam. Reporting his death in 2022, PTI named works including *Daria Parira Kabita*, *Astaragara Kabi*, *Di Dhara Luha*, *Agnisambhaba* and *Ananya*.[^life]

These titles open a larger writing life beyond the familiar connection between Biswal and *Bhukha*.

## The people behind the performance

His other plays include *Luraa*, *Ulgulaan*, *Baruaa*, *Maa Samlei* and *Udlaa Patar Budlaa Danga*. Hachette’s introduction to *The Starved* describes five Bajnias, professional rural folk performers, facing poverty, caste discrimination and a declining livelihood.[^translation]

The performers’ circumstances belong beside their art. A play can keep both within view: the pleasure of a performance and the lives its makers must sustain between engagements.

*Bhukha* belongs to his Sambalpuri writing; his wider literary life also includes work in Odia.

## Returning from the screen

Biswal’s Sambalpuri play became the basis of [Sabyasachi Mohapatra](sabyasachi-mohapatra.md)’s film *Bhukha*.[^film] The [film reading](../culture/cinema-series/bhukha.md) follows its music, audiences and circulation. Here, the connection brings the playwright into the foreground.

The English translation makes it possible to place the play beside the film and consider what each does with the performers’ story.

The [page behind the screen](../culture/cinema-series/literature-and-screen.md) follows other writers through these exchanges. Biswal’s name asks us to remember that a film’s source has a life of its own—and that there may be poems, children’s stories and other plays waiting beyond the work we first encountered.
