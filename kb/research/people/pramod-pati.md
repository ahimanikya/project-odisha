# Pramod Pati: A Room for Experiment

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

A door could be placed in the floor. That was one possibility explored when Pramod Pati and the artist **Abid Surti** worked on a film together. Surti remembered Pati asking him to recreate a room he had painted, this time inside a Films Division studio.[^experimenta-surti]

Surti proposed an arc in which an artist arrives, creates and disappears, while the work remains. He joined staff discussions about turning that idea into images and recalled having a say in rejecting suggestions. The room’s arrangement changed as they worked. In his account, the film *Abid* grew through the painter’s participation in its making.[^experimenta-surti]

## From public information to moving images

Pati’s connection with Odisha included practical filmmaking work. In his retrospective account, Golaka Bihari Singh traces Pati’s beginnings in Sambalpur, science studies at Utkal University and cinematography training in Bangalore. As a Film Officer for the Odisha government, he made films intended for public use. Training in puppetry and animation in Prague preceded his later work leading animation at Films Division.[^pati-review]

A camera, an object and the time between exposures could all become tools for shaping an idea. Animation offered a way to make familiar things behave unexpectedly, inviting a viewer to look again.

## Making room for other hands

Animator **Ram Mohan** remembered Pati encouraging graphic, stylised designs and experiments with materials. Puppets, objects and cutouts could be animated; live action and animation could be brought together.[^dsource-ram-mohan]

Those possibilities give a practical meaning to experimentation. A drawing need not carry every movement. An object can become part of the action, and a photographed person can enter a differently constructed world. Mohan’s recollection places that freedom within a workplace shared by people making films together.[^dsource-ram-mohan]

## Short films, shared work

*Explorer* (1968) brings youth, science and modernity into a collage without narrative commentary. Its collaborators include **B. K. Parthasarathy** on photography, **S. D. Patil** on sound, **U. Harish Rao** on editing and **Vijay Raghav Rao** on music.[^reel-explorer]

*Trip* (1970) uses time-lapse images of Bombay and an abstract soundtrack. **B. V. Dhavale** handled photography; Patil, Harish Rao and Vijay Raghav Rao also worked on its sound, editing and music.[^reel-trip] The moving city and the studio experiment belong within the same body of work, with different tasks meeting in the finished film.

These shorts widen the geographical frame of this series: an Odisha filmmaker’s work can belong to the story without being an Odia-language production.[^pati-pib]

## An invitation to look again

Pati’s experiments sit beside the listening and documentary practice explored in [Lipika Singh Darai’s story](lipika-singh-darai.md). Each offers a different way into the work between an idea and a film.

Watch [*Abid*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oqTZj4pnbTc), [*Explorer*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wiWlUEOaFuQ) and [*Trip*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZuuFyuWeBeY) on Films Division’s channel. Notice what an image asks of its sound, or how an ordinary object changes when its movement is broken into frames.
