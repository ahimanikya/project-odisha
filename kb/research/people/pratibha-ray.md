## Reading introduction · 20 October 2026

Draupadi is walking towards heaven, yet the promised destination troubles her. In Pratibha Ray’s Jajnaseni, the journey gives a woman room to question what her life has meant.

### A writer’s life in education

Pratibha Ray’s professional account traces a working life from school teaching to Odisha’s government colleges. She took voluntary retirement as a Professor of Education in 1998. That career belongs alongside the novels and stories: it is part of the life behind the writing, though it does not explain every choice a fictional character makes.

### Draupadi asks what the journey means

The English excerpt of Jajnaseni on Ray’s website places us inside Draupadi’s thoughts during the Himalayan ascent. She asks whether the wish to reach heaven can itself become an attachment. The contrast between iron and gold makes the question sharper: a desire may seem purer while still binding the person who holds it.

Her thoughts then turn to an unfinished life. What has she accomplished, and what remains wanting? She slips; her five husbands continue without looking back. Told from her position, their onward movement becomes an experience of abandonment. The episode asks us to attend to the person left behind.

Pause at the change from inward questioning to the fall. How does a sentence about spiritual purpose prepare us to read an action between people? Following that movement gives this short passage its force.

### Another form changes the telling

Ray’s account of adaptations records Hema Malini’s stage ballet of Jajnaseni. It also names an Odissi ballet drawn from Mahamoha, her novel about Ahalya. These are two examples of her fiction moving into performance; the account supplies no dates for these productions.

An inward question on the page poses a practical problem on stage. A performer may have to carry it through gesture, movement, music or speech. When comparing a book and an adaptation, choose one moment and notice what the new form makes available to its audience.
