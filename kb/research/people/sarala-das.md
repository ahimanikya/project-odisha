# Sarala Das

Sarala Das’s Mahabharata asks readers to meet familiar characters again. A prayer turns towards Chandrabhaga; a plotter has to reckon with the deaths of soldiers. The epic’s language and its moral choices belong together.

## An epic with its own choices

Sarala Das belongs to the fifteenth century. Ganeswar Mishra’s literary overview places his Mahabharata in that period; B. N. Patnaik describes the poet, remembered as Adikavi, as a creative reteller. The familiar family conflict becomes a work whose characters can act and reason differently.

[Government of Odisha · Literature](https://www.odisha.gov.in/en/about-odisha/literature) [B. N. Patnaik · Aadi Kavi Sarala and His Mahabharata](https://saralamahabharata.org/2019/11/07/aadi-kavi-sarala-and-his-mahabharata/)

The poet’s life is less securely known than his literary importance. Ujaan Ghosh connects the broad dating to a royal name within the work, while emphasising the scarcity of biographical evidence. An exact birth year would imply more certainty than this evidence offers.

[Ujaan Ghosh · Struggling with Kṛṣṇa: Alternative Imaginations in an Odia Mahābhārata](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11407-025-09407-7)

## A prayer turns towards Chandrabhaga

In the Draupadi episode examined by Ghosh, Krishna directs her appeal towards Surya. Her prayer names Chandrabhaga on the coast; the Sun sends Chaya and Maya to keep her clothed. Ghosh reads this as a redistribution of divine agency: Krishna remains involved, but another deity provides the rescue. A place in Odisha becomes part of how the crisis is answered.

[Ujaan Ghosh · Struggling with Kṛṣṇa: Alternative Imaginations in an Odia Mahābhārata](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11407-025-09407-7)

Read the prayer with that change in mind. Who receives the appeal, who acts, and whose power becomes visible? Following those decisions gives a reader more to notice than whether a remembered storyline has been reproduced.

## Sakuni and the cost of revenge

Patnaik’s account gives Sakuni a responsibility beyond his revenge. Bound to avenge his father and relatives, he could eventually return to his kingdom. Instead, he chooses death on the battlefield, holding himself responsible for innocent soldiers killed in the war. Patnaik’s reading draws attention to the people whose deaths a contest between royal families can leave out of view.

[B. N. Patnaik · Aadi Kavi Sarala and His Mahabharata](https://saralamahabharata.org/2019/11/07/aadi-kavi-sarala-and-his-mahabharata/)

The question here is not simply whether Sakuni succeeds. It is what success leaves him answerable for. A character can remain implicated in violence while also recognising its cost; that recognition changes how we read his last choice.

## The poet and the goddess

Patnaik also describes Sarala’s account of his own writing: he professes little learning and presents himself as the scribe of goddess Sarala’s inspiration. This is a religious account of poetic authority. It tells us how the poet wishes his words to be received; it cannot by itself establish a literal history of his education or a miraculous event.

[B. N. Patnaik · Aadi Kavi Sarala and His Mahabharata](https://saralamahabharata.org/2019/11/07/aadi-kavi-sarala-and-his-mahabharata/)

That distinction leaves room to take devotion seriously. The claim asks a listener to hear a voice authorised by the goddess. It can be read for what it does within the work, without turning it into a dated biographical incident.

## Know which text you are reading

Copies matter. Ghosh uses Artaballabh Mohanty’s multi-volume edition, published from 1965, and explains that it draws on manuscripts with differing readings. His citations use pages rather than verse numbers. His study does not describe a single, settled critical text. An episode encountered in one edition therefore needs its edition details alongside it.

[Ujaan Ghosh · Struggling with Kṛṣṇa: Alternative Imaginations in an Odia Mahābhārata](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11407-025-09407-7)

The Government of Odisha’s Sarala Mahabharata catalogue offers numbered PDF downloads, giving readers a way to approach the Odia text. A catalogue label alone does not identify its relationship to Mohanty’s edition. Keep the file, parva, page and any editor or publication details with a passage before comparing it with a scholar’s account.

[Government of Odisha · Sarala Mahabharata](https://odisha.gov.in/en/odisha-profile/odia-classics/sarala-mahabharata/odia-classic-data)

Begin with one episode and stay with its choices: the destination of a prayer, the reason for a decision, the person left to bear its consequences. Read the Odia with someone who knows the language when needed. Let a remembered version start the conversation, while giving this one time to speak.
