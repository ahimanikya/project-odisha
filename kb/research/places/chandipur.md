## Tidal-flat explanation · 20 October 2026

At Chandipur, low tide uncovers a broad tidal flat beside a much narrower beach. The ground slopes gently towards the sea. As the water level falls, its edge moves a long way across that shallow incline; when the level rises, the same ground is covered again. The scale of the change becomes easier to understand once you picture the shape beneath the water.

The flat is also being made and remade. A geological study describes river-borne sediment redistributed by marine processes, with bars that shelter quieter patches where mud settles. Channels carry water into and out of the flat. The apparently still surface is part of a moving system of sand, mud and water.

Evidence: Saha, Samanta, Mukhopadhyay and Eriksson, Journal Indian Association of Sedimentologists40(1),2023, Study Area pp6–7. The relation between slope and horizontal exposure is a plain geometrical explanation of the described terrain.
