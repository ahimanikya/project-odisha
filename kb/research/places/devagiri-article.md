Devagiri rises near Kalyansinghpur in Rayagada district. A cave shrine dedicated to Shiva and pools on the hill draw pilgrims into this rocky setting. Odisha’s Tourism Department includes Devagiri among Rayagada’s scenic destinations.[^place][^census]

## A hill of shrines and water

The Rayagada handbook for the 2001 Census records worship of Mahalaxmi at Devagiri as well as images of Shiva and other deities inside the cave. It names the hill’s pools Ganga, Jamuna, Saraswati and Indradyumna. These names belong to the religious geography of the place, connecting its water bodies with the wider vocabulary of Hindu sacred places.[^census]

The cave and pools were already described in W. Francis’s 1907 gazetteer. His account places Devagiri southwest of Kalyana Singapur, the older spelling used for Kalyansinghpur, and records a lingam in the cave and a Shivaratri gathering. That brief description brings the same three features—rocky hill, water and worship—into an early written account of the site.[^history]

## Pilgrimage through the year

Shivaratri is one of Devagiri’s documented occasions for collective worship; the Census handbook also records the festival’s congregation.[^census] Kartik brings another gathering. In a report dated 24 November 2026, The New Indian Express described devotees from Odisha and neighbouring states climbing to the cave shrine on the final Monday of Kartik. After prayer, some visited the water bodies across the hilltop.[^kartik]

The climb is part of a visit to Devagiri. The 2026 report describes a stepped ascent to the cave; allow for an uphill approach and check current local access and festival arrangements before setting out.[^kartik]
