Gandhamardan is a forested hill range across Bargarh and Balangir. Pilgrimage places stand at its northern and southern foothills, drawing visitors into a landscape whose medicinal-plant diversity also gives it conservation significance.[^gandhamardan-bhs-notification-2023]

Nrusinghanath, near Paikmal on the Bargarh side, is dedicated to Nrusingha, an incarnation of Vishnu. Harishankar, on the southern slopes in Balangir, brings together worship of Shiva and Vishnu. Forest and waterfalls form the setting for both shrines.[^gandhamardan-tourism]

## A forest with several claims on it

On 20 March 2023, Odisha’s government notified about 18,964 hectares of Gandhamardan Reserve Forest as a Biodiversity Heritage Site. The designation recognises a forest whose natural diversity and pilgrimage traditions occupy the same hills.[^gandhamardan-bhs-notification-2023]

The work of protecting it has a longer local history. In their 2001 study of Harishankar and adjoining Nandupalla, Pritam Kumar Nanda and Pravat Chandro Sutar connected village organisation with resistance to proposed mining. Their account also described dependence on kendu and mahua for livelihoods, alongside continuing forest fires. Protecting the hills from mining and sustaining the forest in everyday use were connected concerns, with tensions that the authors did not gloss over.[^forest-vasundhara2001]

## Choose your approach

Plan around the shrine you want to visit: Balangir is an approach for Harishankar, while Bargarh and Padampur serve the Nrusinghanath side. Odisha Tourism recommends October–March for both.[^gandhamardan-tourism] Confirm the final road, walking approach and return transport locally before setting out. Allow time for the shrine and its surroundings rather than treating the two sides as a hurried circuit.
