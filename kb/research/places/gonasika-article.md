Gonasika is a place of hills, valleys and worship in Kendujhar. The Brahmeswar Mahadev shrine stands beside the Baitarani,[^kendujhar-gonasika-temple-20261125] the river that begins here.[^dowr-annual-2020-21-baitarani]

## A sacred source

The district’s sacred account connects the source with Brahma and describes water emerging through the nostrils of a cow. It describes the river disappearing underground and re-emerging at Brahma Kunda, and calls it Guptaganga.[^kendujhar-gonasika-temple-20261125]

Baruni Jatra brings another religious association: the district links the festival with pilgrimage to the shrine and ritual bathing at Brahma Kunda.[^kendujhar-gonasika-culture-20261125]

## From the hills to the sea

The Baitarani travels north towards the Jharkhand border before turning towards Anandapur and Bhadrak.[^kanjhari-esdd-2020-baitarani-course] Odisha’s Water Resources Department traces its journey from Gonasika to the Bay of Bengal.[^dowr-annual-2020-21-baitarani]

## Planning a visit

Kendujhar is the approach town; confirm onward access locally before setting out.[^kendujhar-gonasika-temple-20261125] Allow time for the source setting and worship, and ask before photographing rituals.
