A Tibetan monastery stands against the hills at Jirang in Gajapati. Known as **Rigon Thupten Mindrolling** and **Padmasambhava Mahavihara**, it belongs to the Ripa tradition.[^jirang-ripa-monastery] The wider Chandragiri area is also home to Phuntsokling, a Tibetan settlement with a history that reaches back to 1963.[^phuntsokling-ctrc-settlement]

## Making a home in Odisha

The Central Tibetan Relief Committee dates the settlement’s establishment to **1963**, with assistance from the Indian government and Swiss Technical Co-operation. Its account traces the first settlers’ journey from road-construction camps at Chamba, Dalhousie and Bomdila, followed by further arrivals between 1964 and 1967.[^phuntsokling-ctrc-settlement]

Farming, trade and other livelihoods form part of that account. It also describes a cooperative role in preserving carpet weaving.[^phuntsokling-ctrc-settlement]

## Looking at the monastery

The Dalai Lama inaugurated the monastery in **2010**.[^jirang-chandragiri-gajapati-district] That date belongs to the monastery’s inauguration; the settlement was already decades old.

In the September 2021 photograph above, blue window frames run across the façade, beneath gilded roof ornaments. A broad flight of steps leads towards the entrance.

The Ripa Ladrang Foundation identifies statues of the Buddha, Guru Rinpoche and Avalokiteshvara in the central hall. Its account places the monastery within a tradition of Buddhist teaching, study and practice.[^jirang-ripa-monastery] Allow time to look, while leaving worship and learning undisturbed.

## Planning a visit

Odisha Tourism recommends **October to March**. It identifies Brahmapur as the rail gateway, with the road approach continuing through Chandragiri.[^jirang-odisha-tourism]

Confirm visitor access and photography rules before entering, and follow the monastery’s directions in prayer spaces. Arrange the final road journey and return pickup in advance. Beyond the monastery, ask before photographing people or entering homes, fields and workplaces; this is a community’s everyday surroundings.
