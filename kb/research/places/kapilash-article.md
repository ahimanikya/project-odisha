Kapilash, also written Kapilas, is a hill pilgrimage in Dhenkanal where Shiva is worshipped as Chandrasekhar.[^kapilash-dhenkanal-district] The wooded hills and the ascent give the shrine its setting.[^kapilash-odisha-tourism]

## Pilgrimage on the hill

The district’s account connects Kapilash with Kailash through legend and identifies Mahashivaratri as a major pilgrimage occasion.[^kapilash-dhenkanal-district] For visitors, it is a place of worship as well as a setting for looking at temple architecture.

Anup525’s 2019 photograph shows white towers rising behind smaller shrines, with palms above the courtyard. Carved details cover the nearer structures; their red bases contrast with the pale stonework.

## An older temple’s trace

In November 1957, D. C. Sircar examined three inscriptions on a detached stone *kalasa*, a vase-shaped architectural piece. He inferred that it had crowned an older temple later replaced on the same site. His reading of the first inscription’s calendar points to **1246**, with **Narasimhadeva I** as a possible identification of the king; comparison with a second inscription strengthens that reading.[^kapilas-sircar-inscriptions-ei33] This is evidence for an earlier building, not a secure date for the temple now standing.

The Archaeological Survey of India lists Chandrasekhar Temple, its group of monuments and ancient steps as centrally protected.[^kapilas-asi-protected-monuments]

## Planning the ascent

Kapilash has both a stair approach and a winding hill road. Odisha Tourism recommends **October to March** and identifies Dhenkanal town and railway station as gateways.[^kapilash-odisha-tourism]

Before travelling, confirm road restrictions and the final walking approach, especially if anyone in your group needs assistance or a step-free route. Arrange the return journey, allow time for pauses and follow directions around worship spaces.
