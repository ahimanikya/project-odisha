## Editorial distinctions

A Nayika is a heroine within dramatic or poetic analysis; Ashta Nayika describes eight situations in love. It is not the name of another Nataka form. Theatre traditions, literary works, dramatic categories, performance techniques and modern events therefore need separate KB records joined through evidence.

The eventual full-post audit should record each permalink, creator/collaborator, displayed date, language, caption and media inspection depth, texts named, people credited, cultural claim, independent source and article destination. A caption-only item must not become a claim that its performance was watched. The signed-in follow-up below supersedes the initial archive-access limit; full audiovisual inspection remains outstanding.

## Signed-in Chrome follow-up

With the user’s explicit direction to use Chrome, the profile archive was traversed to its earliest introductory post. **140 unique rendered post-caption records** were inventoried against a displayed profile count of **145**. The five-item discrepancy is unresolved. This is a broad caption-level concept survey, with some long captions sampled; it is not a claim to have watched every reel, transcribed every song or read every carousel. Original captions for all eight Nayika episodes became available, removing reliance on three highlight excerpts for locating the series.

The strongest finding is a correction, not a new label: the [Kalahantarita episode](https://www.instagram.com/odissiandbeyond/reel/DPEGDGcEwM4/) explicitly corrects its song choice to Khandita. The [Vasakasajja episode](https://www.instagram.com/odissiandbeyond/reel/DO7uGGuiOmc/) also distinguishes a relevant stanza from the entire song. Both demonstrate why an article must cite the actual passage and preserve the creator’s qualifications.
