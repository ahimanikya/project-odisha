# Prahallad Nataka: evidence, conflicts and research questions

This dossier supports the [feature article](../culture/prahallad-nataka.md), [comparative theatre guide](../culture/odisha-theatre-traditions.md) and [dated event record](../events/baithak-17-2026.md). Research is a literature and web-source review. It does not claim attendance, interviews, full-film analysis or a verified census of working companies.

## Editorial decisions

| Question | Evidence conflict or limit | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How old is it? | Mahapatra: around 1860; Emigh: 1870–80; Pattanaik’s profile: early nineteenth century. | Use nineteenth century; distinguish the age of the sacred narrative from the theatrical form. |
| Who wrote it? | Scholarly discussion distinguishes royal attribution from Gourhari/Goura Hari Parichha’s contributions. Some derivative pages use Gopinath. | Retain the court association and contested composition; do not declare a single proven author or silently equate names. |
| Which musical tradition? | Impart emphasises Odissi; Emigh discusses Carnatic connections. | Attribute terminology; comparison needs a teacher-led, recording-specific musical study. |
| How many songs? | Impart gives 126; Odipedia gives 133, while broader online accounts circulate larger totals. Editions and counting methods are not aligned. | Keep the article’s number explicitly source-specific. Do not add song, verse, raga or repertoire totals across sources. |
| Is the text wholly fixed? | Impart says no improvisation; performer documentation describes individual nuances. These could concern different levels of performance. | Avoid a universal ban-on-improvisation claim; ask separately about text, melody, delivery, commentary and episode selection. |
| Where is the manuscript? | Impart says Madras; Mahapatra’s indexed account traces a transfer from Madras to Cuttack in 1938 and later Bhubaneswar. | Current custody unresolved; verify a catalogue/accession record before giving an archive location. |
| How many troupes remain? | Scholarly and promotional descriptions belong to different dates and scopes. | No current statewide total or extinction forecast. |
| Which artist is appearing? | Baithak uses conditional language. Historical documentation credits a separate ensemble. | Do not transfer a documentary cast into the event listing. |
| Which Simanchal birth date? | Official citation gives 7 July 1927; Wikipedia’s indexed entry gives 2 February. | Omit exact birth date from the article. Retain award evidence independently. |
| Is every primary-source statement reliable? | Official citation broadly calls the form Mahabharata storytelling; specialist descriptions identify its particular Puranic plot. | Use the citation for the artist’s recognition and attributed career; use specialist research for dramatic content. |

## A fieldwork agenda arising from the research

These questions are prepared for later use; nobody has been contacted.

1. **Text and lineage:** Which manuscript, printed edition or teacher’s notebook does this troupe use? Can its title page, ownership, date and variant passages be documented with permission?
2. **Repertoire:** Make a song list for one identified performance. Separate song, Sanskrit verse, spoken passage, reprise and omitted episode. Have a musician check raga and tala names.
3. **Training:** Who teaches the Raja, Prahallad, Lilabati, gahaka and instrumental roles? What is learned by demonstration, memorisation and rehearsal? Record women’s roles in performance, audiences, organisation and teaching from current testimony rather than extrapolating old all-male cast accounts.
4. **Space and ritual:** Draw the troupe’s stage and entrance routes. Ask which preparations may be documented, who controls the mask and which festival adaptations matter to the performers.
5. **Livelihood:** Establish a dated season of performances, rehearsal time, ensemble payments, travel costs, teaching income and support. A single event cannot establish economic sustainability.
6. **Archive and rights:** Locate the 1973 cultural-department edition; reconcile manuscript custody. Catalogue available films by date, village, troupe, episode and rights holder. Verify subtitle completeness and language review.
7. **Comparative research:** Prioritise Bharata Lila, Danda Nata, Mughal Tamasha, Desia Nata and Ravana Chhaya as separate dossiers; extend existing Pala and Daskathia entries with their stable identities.
