# Ratnagiri: deep evidence review

Researcher: Anvesha. Date: 26 October 2026. Input site-publication head: `651feec`. Read-only research; this packet is not publication clearance, a deployed article, or social approval.

## Scope and result

Ratnagiri supports a substantial article about a named Buddhist monastic community, successive buildings, ritual texts, sculpture and conservation. The best directly inspected primary evidence is the ASI's contemporary annual excavation reporting. It gives a stronger account than the recurring promotional assertions about a university, a unique international ranking, or the origin of Kalachakra. Those assertions should not be used as settled facts.

The investigation recovered and read the relevant full pages of three ASI reports, plus two full scholarly papers. It identified the two-volume final excavation monograph, but did not obtain a readable full copy. A chapter by Natasha Reichle was available only as a summary. These distinctions are material: a bibliography entry or a later author's page reference is not evidence that the underlying page has been checked.

## Primary evidence actually inspected

### A. ASI, Indian Archaeology 1957–58—A Review, printed pp. 39–41

Full PDF downloaded from [ASI Aurangabad Circle](https://www.asiaurangabadcircle.com/assets/files/publications/1957-1958.pdf). Relevant PDF pages 48–50. Local examination copy: `/tmp/ratnagiri-iar5758.pdf`.

The Eastern Circle excavation under D. Mitra exposed the main brick stupa. Printed p. 39 supplies its measured base and complex projections. Page 40 records a central pit without relics, while explicitly leaving open deposits elsewhere or above the surviving height. It distinguishes miniature stupas with bone deposits from others interpreted as votive. A loose slab bearing the Pratitya-samutpada-sutra in Gupta-period script supports an earlier Buddhist phase. Another inscription, palaeographically late eighth/early ninth century, provides a relative chronological argument for the main stupa. Page 41 records later circular alterations and additional inscribed material. The early report's proposed workshop/sale explanation for a concentration of miniature stupas is an alternative hypothesis, not a demonstrated commercial industry.

**Use:** layered construction; different functions of small stupas; written doctrine as archaeological evidence. **Do not use:** absence of all relics at Ratnagiri; a precisely dated foundation year; an excavated intact Gupta-era campus.

### B. ASI, Indian Archaeology 1958–59—A Review, printed pp. 33–36, 88

Full PDF downloaded from [ASI Aurangabad Circle](https://www.asiaurangabadcircle.com/assets/files/publications/1958-1959.pdf). Relevant PDF pages 40–43 and 95. Local copy: `/tmp/ratnagiri-iar5859-alt.pdf`.

Pages 33–35 describe two south-facing monasteries: Monastery 1 roughly 180 feet square, 24 cells, an approximately 88-foot-8-inch courtyard and a staircase suggesting an upper storey; Monastery 2 roughly 95 feet square, 18 cells, arched anterooms, windows and drains. These are reported excavated configurations, not lifetime totals. Monastery 1 underwent major ancient rebuilding; relative floor levels place Monastery 2 between its first and second phases. Page 35 expressly withholds precise absolute chronology, suggesting an eighth-century comparison for Monastery 1's early decoration. Page 36 records the institution's name on sealings, five dharani plaques inside a second stupa, and small funerary deposits. Page 88 describes modern stupa conservation. The large seated Buddha was reconstructed from scattered pieces (p. 34).

**Use:** architecture, institutional identity, rebuilding, textual deposits. **Do not use:** cells equal student enrolment; every room a classroom; all standing sculpture in untouched original condition; a simple one-date building history.

### C. ASI, Indian Archaeology 1960–61—A Review, printed pp. 28–30, 81

Full PDF downloaded from [ASI Aurangabad Circle](https://www.asiaurangabadcircle.com/assets/files/publications/1960-1961.pdf). Relevant PDF pages 36–38 and 89. Local copy: `/tmp/ratnagiri-iar6061.pdf`.

Pages 28–30 describe temples and sculptures south of Monastery 2. Temple 4 contained Vajraraga-Manjusri, Vajradharma-Lokesvara and Vajrasattva; the first two carried creed inscriptions attributed to around the tenth century by letter forms. Temple 5 held a 2.34-metre Manjusri. The report counts 125 in-situ stupas in that season's excavated area, not across the entire site. It distinguishes integral and inserted images. Recovered architectural pieces were assembled into a shrine façade more than 8.7 metres long. Page 81 documents extensive consolidation and resetting of masonry, old bricks, porch stones and sculptural fragments. Page 30 also refers to copperplates involving a Somavamsi ruler, Queen Karpurasri and Salonapura Mahavihara; the original epigraph and its grant geography were not independently read.

**Use:** particular sculptures, local dates, shrine reconstruction. **Hold:** a named royal founder or an unqualified claim that the mentioned grant funded Ratnagiri itself. OCR renders the king's name inconsistently; do not transcribe from OCR alone.

## Chronology: distinguish the things being dated

| Proposition | Evidence | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Earlier Buddhist activity preceded the prominent monasteries | Gupta-script text slab, IAR 1957–58 p. 40 | Supported; script dates a text/object, not every surviving building. |
| Monastery 1 was built in the eighth century | IAR 1958–59 p. 35 gives a stylistic comparison and explicitly limited precision | Attribute the estimate to the excavators; avoid a fixed foundation year. |
| Monastery 2 falls between two Monastery 1 phases | IAR 1958–59 p. 35, superimposed passage floors | Strong relative sequence; do not manufacture calendar dates. |
| Some shrine images belong around the tenth century | IAR 1960–61 p. 29, inscription letter forms | Keep the date attached to those images/inscriptions. |
| Ratnagiri begins in the fourth–fifth century | India's 2025 tentative-list submission describes sealing palaeography | State as the submission's estimate if needed; do not flatten into a fourth-century date for Monastery 1. |
| Ratnagiri's last occupation ended exactly in year X | Not established by sources examined | Omit. Later sculpture and successive reuse do not yield one closure date. |

The old reports place Ratnagiri in then-Cuttack district. The current district is Jajpur. Historical citation labels should remain intact, with current geography in public narrative.

## Ritual texts, iconography and international comparisons

### Umakanta Mishra, “Dhāraṇīs from the Buddhist Sites of Orissa,” Pratnatattva 22 (2016), pp. 73–84

[Full author-hosted PDF](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Umakanta-Mishra/publication/344627634_Dharanis_from_the_Buddhist_Sites_of_Orissa/links/5f8572fe458515b7cf7c5f80/Dharanis-from-the-Buddhist-Sites-of-Orissa.pdf) read, especially pp. 76–79 and 81–82. This is epigraphic analysis rather than the original excavation record. It compares Ratnagiri dharani plaques and image inscriptions with material at Nalanda, Bodh Gaya and beyond; discusses a text on the back of a Jatamukuta-Lokesvara from Temple 7; and relates deposits to religious merit. These are good grounds for shared textual and ritual worlds. A slab in Jajpur museum has uncertain Ratnagiri/Udayagiri provenance and must not be assigned exclusively to Ratnagiri. A Blue Annals reference is an indirect Kalachakra lead, not directly checked Tibetan testimony. Mitra volume I page references 43, 99, 104 and 230–232 remain secondhand locators pending consultation of that volume.

### Sindhu M. J., “Dhyani-Buddhas with Four Attending Bodhisattvas from Ratnagiri, Odisha and Its Importance in Buddhism,” Heritage 7 (2019), pp. 289–300

[Full PDF](https://www.heritageuniversityofkerala.com/JournalPDF/Volume7/15.pdf) read. The paper documents sculptural groups and proposes a Vajradhatu-mandala interpretation, comparing Ratnagiri material with Java. It includes damaged and incompletely identifiable figures. Use this as an explicitly scholarly reconstruction of iconographic relationships; it does not establish an intact excavated mandala, a named traveller, or a proven direct shipping route. Its cited Mitra pages 423, 458 and 465 were not separately inspected in the monograph. Its “so far” uniqueness statements describe the author's knowledge in 2019, not a current worldwide inventory.

## Unresolved claims requiring restraint

- **“University,” “second Nalanda,” or international ranking:** mahavihara and a learned monastic community are defensible; modern enrolment, curriculum, degrees and ranking are unverified. Architecture alone cannot reconstruct timetables or departments.
- **Kalachakra originated here:** requires close reading of the relevant Tibetan source and modern specialist debate. The indirect references encountered do not settle origin, identity or transmission. Omit from the article's factual spine.
- **Pushpagiri:** do not identify the modern three-site triangle with one ancient university. Existing project research treats Langudi/Pushpagiri identification and traveller testimony separately. Regional monastery counts in Xuanzang cannot become Ratnagiri student numbers.
- **Royal founders/patrons:** tentative-list text invokes Bhaumakara patronage broadly. A specific founding monarch, foundation year or grant requires the relevant inscription, not repetition of a tourism paragraph. Taranatha's Buddhapaksha appears in IAR 1958–59 p. 36 as an unidentified ruler in later testimony.
- **Asia connections:** inscription/text and art comparisons show connected Buddhist traditions. Named teachers, exact voyages and direct routes require separate documentation. Similarity is not an itinerary.
- **Relics:** distinguish Ratnagiri small funerary stupas from the main-stupa test pit and from Lalitgiri's nested reliquaries. Do not call Ratnagiri bones the Buddha's remains.
- **2024–25 excavations:** the current official tentative submission confirms renewed ASI work. No accessible full contemporary excavation report, context register or scientific dating dataset was obtained. News accounts of newly found heads and palms cannot substitute for final stratigraphic conclusions. Do not present a new chronology as settled.

## Recent status and museum

The [UNESCO-hosted tentative entry 6926](https://whc.unesco.org/en/tentativelists/6926/) was checked on 26 October 2026. India submitted the three sites on **22 December 2025**. This is a **Tentative List entry**, not World Heritage inscription; its descriptive claims are India's submission, not an independent UNESCO archaeological verdict. It identifies Ratnagiri and Buddhist Udayagiri in Jajpur, Lalitgiri in Cuttack, and mentions the 2024–25 work.

The Ministry of Culture's [Directory of Indian Museums](https://www.culture.gov.in/files/inline-documents/Directory_of_Indian_Museums_080620231.pdf), printed p. 424, lists Ratnagiri's ASI museum, established in 1998, and its archaeological collections. Dated opening hours and fees are not reissued as current advice. Institutional existence is supported; current visitor arrangements need a fresh operational check.

The proposed [NMA bylaws link](https://www.nma.gov.in/showfile.php?lang=1&level=2&lid=1277&ls_id=1018&nma_type=0) was opened and is **Rajgir, Nalanda, Bihar**, not Ratnagiri, Odisha. Excluded to prevent a place-name substitution.
