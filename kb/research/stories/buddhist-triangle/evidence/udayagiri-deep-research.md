## Main improvement over the earlier surface article

The 2007 report concerns the main excavated Simhaprastha monastery and assigns that complex approximately eighth to early twelfth century dates. The 2012 report expands excavation into the chaitya/stupa enclosure, pathway and residential/kitchen area. It proposes a much earlier beginning for those parts. These are different excavation scopes, not two interchangeable dates for an identical building. An article can explain this without declaring one source simply wrong.

## Contradictions and interpretive traps

**Relics:** Trivedi p.14 says no ash or casket had been found in a stupa, while p.214 describes a loose inscribed reliquary. These can be compatible because find context matters. Do not paraphrase as “Udayagiri yielded no casket,” or invent bones/Buddha identification. Lalitgiri's nested caskets belong to another site.

**Dynastic support:** Bandyopadhyay p.41 says there is no direct evidence identifying patrons, then offers a Bhaumakara/Somavamsi explanation for decline. Keep the absence of specific evidence; do not turn the proposed political explanation into a dated destructive event. Damaged sculptures cannot by themselves identify an attacker, religion or invasion.

**Pushpagiri:** Bandyopadhyay pp.12–17 sets out older identifications, Langudi inscription claims and the excavator's doubts after inspection. Trivedi's 2012 foreword rejects identification of the three named monasteries with Pushpagiri. The responsible public conclusion is that Udayagiri has securely recovered names of its own, and is not demonstrated to be a campus of one three-site Pushpagiri University. Do not erase disagreement about Langudi by claiming everyone agrees. Xuanzang's regional count is not an institutional student register. Quotations and historical hypotheses within a report are not automatically the excavator's final findings.

**Foreign links:** Regional accounts of Prajna and a manuscript sent to China (Bandyopadhyay p.17) do not name Udayagiri securely. Do not turn an Odra monastery into this specific monastery. The local material can be situated within wider Buddhist traditions without a fabricated itinerary.

**Living worship:** The reports record local reuse/identification of some sculptures and shrines, including Mahakali. Avoid treating local worship as archaeological ignorance or assigning today's religious identities solely from an old catalogue. No present-day ritual observation made in this task.

All dates of specific objects are attached to source and context. The 2012 source is fully inspected for used claims, not merely an abstract.
