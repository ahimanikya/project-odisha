# Forest work: understanding the support behind the trade

A market story often starts with what was sold. A support story asks a different question: what happens when someone needs help? Both belong beside the work, but they should not be folded into one triumphant number.

Odisha’s Kendu Leaf Organisation records compassionate grants associated with death or permanent disability. That is a serious human context for a financial table. Read it with care: the amount tells us about a specific recorded category of assistance, not the quality of a person’s life or the full experience of a family.

## Read the total through its parts

In the FY2025–26 row, the page lists **1,132 records and ₹225,950,000** for pluckers and binders, and **103 records and ₹20,400,000** for seasonal staff. The count and amount add to the reported total retained below. The page’s “Nos” column does not establish a deduplicated count of people or households. [Compassionate Grant table](https://kenduleaves.odisha.gov.in/welfare-measures).

A count answers how many entries the table records. An amount answers how much it records for that category. Dividing one by the other would produce an arithmetic average per record, not a standard entitlement, a typical wage or the amount every family received. We do not use that shortcut to tell someone’s story.

## The dated support evidence

**₹24.635 crore in compassionate grants is recorded for FY2025–26** on Odisha's Kendu Leaf Organisation welfare page. The accompanying total is **1,235 grant records** across pluckers/binders and seasonal staff. [Official table](https://kenduleaves.odisha.gov.in/welfare-measures).

This is one specific form of support associated with death or permanent disability. It is not the whole welfare budget, the trade's sales value or the earnings of a typical collector. The page was read on 1 October 2026 and displays a 9 September update; individual payments have not been independently audited here.

## Follow a record towards an actual receipt

A fuller account would follow a permitted case through its stages. Application, decision and receipt answer different questions. None should silently stand in for the next.

| Reporting question | Evidence to seek, with permission | What remains unproven |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Was support requested? | Dated application or acknowledgment, with unnecessary personal information excluded | A request does not establish eligibility or approval |
| What decision was made? | Applicable dated decision and the category it covers | Approval does not prove money reached the recipient |
| What was received, and when? | A privacy-protected payment record and the willing recipient’s account | An aggregate transfer does not establish each individual receipt |
| What did the support change? | The person’s permitted account, its timing and other relevant circumstances | One account cannot measure programme-wide wellbeing or establish causation |

These are reporting questions, not a claim that Utkal has inspected private files or interviewed a recipient. People can share an experience without sharing a diagnosis, bank information or the details of a bereavement. Permission to speak, to be named and to use a portrait are separate choices. Keep the person’s own words distinct from the department’s aggregate figures.

The [Kendu patra introduction](../economy/kendu-patra.md) opens the work-and-trade context. The [forest-enterprise story](van-dhan-enterprises.md) follows a different programme. Read them together without implying that their totals refer to the same recipients, financial years or payment categories.

## Let the visual explain the distinction

A later graphic should show four separate labelled cards: sale proceeds, work payments, bonuses and compassionate grants. Do not connect them with arrows that imply a verified money flow or add the figures into a household-income total. Pair the explanation with a verified, permitted image of actual work where useful; a photograph cannot prove benefit receipt. Avoid staged distress or a fictional beneficiary used to make the grant number emotional.
