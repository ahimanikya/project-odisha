# Bande Utkala Janani from poem to public ceremony

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

“Bande Utkala Janani” brings a landscape into the form of an address to a mother. Kantakabi Laxmikanta Mohapatra’s poem moves through coast, rivers, forests, hills, fields, learning and art. Its repeated return to the mother figure holds those images together, making a region something that can be addressed collectively. [^1]

The imagery gives the song a range beyond one historical event. Natural beauty appears beside cultivation and cultural achievement. Read as a poem, it assembles the things through which attachment to a place can be expressed; sung by a group, it turns that expression into a shared act.

Mohapatra’s writing also belonged to a period of political mobilisation. An *Orissa Review* study discusses his patriotic songs and his engagement with Gandhian programmes, including the promotion of spinning and khadi. The literary background matters because public singing was a means of circulating ideas as well as presenting art. [^2]

Formal state recognition came much later. Odisha’s gazette archive records the 7 June 2020 decision according state-song status to “Bande Utkala Janani” and names Mohapatra as its composer. The administrative milestone gave an established cultural work an explicit institutional role. [^3]

The decision followed a particularly visible performance. On 30 May 2020, people were invited to sing the song in solidarity with frontline workers responding to COVID-19. Reporting on its recognition linked that collective recital to the cabinet decision a week later. The song had become a way to express gratitude during a new crisis, far removed from the political circumstances of its composition. [^4]

In November 2020, the government announced its inclusion in the Class IX and X syllabus. Contemporary reporting also described its use at the conclusion of Assembly sessions. Classroom study and ceremonial singing gave the work different kinds of public presence: one invites attention to the words, the other creates a moment of collective recognition. [^5]

The poem’s continuing life rests in that movement between reading and performance. Its images can be considered individually, but the refrain gathers them into a voice that many people can share. Official status marks one chapter in that life; the act of singing keeps returning the work to an audience.
