# Raja’s days of rest, swings and renewal

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Raja gives a pause in agricultural work a place in Odisha’s festive calendar. In the belief underlying the celebration, the earth menstruates and is allowed to rest. Activities that disturb the soil are suspended while swings, games and prepared foods move into the foreground. [^1]

The principal observance spans three days around the transition from Jyestha to Asadha. Kendujhar’s account names Pahili Raja, Raja Sankranti and Bhuindahan. An *Orissa Review* essay also records Basumata Puja on a fourth day in some local observances. The festival’s shared identity accommodates differences in how its closing rituals are arranged. [^2], [^3]

Women and girls have a prominent place in the celebration. The Ministry of Tourism’s account describes dressing for the occasion, enjoying podapitha and taking turns on rope swings. These pleasures give rest an active form: time can be spent singing, visiting and playing, rather than simply stopping work. [^4]

The earth’s fertility and the human body meet in the festival’s symbolism. That association belongs to belief and cultural practice. The meanings people draw from it can also change, as Raja moves between villages, cities and Odia communities elsewhere.

A June 2025 report on preparations for Raja Mahotsav in Pune shows one such adaptation. Odissi dancer Madhumita Mishra described plans to bring the swing and festival foods onto the stage and to connect the programme with menstrual-health awareness. The programme was to bring several Indian dance forms into an Odia celebration. This was a contemporary interpretation by its organisers, rather than a description of how every household observes Raja. [^5]

Such adaptations keep a festival in conversation with the people celebrating it. The agricultural pause remains an important reference, while the swing can become a remembered childhood pleasure, a public performance element or a reason to gather away from home.

Raja’s appeal lies in how readily these large ideas acquire everyday forms. Renewal becomes a few days set apart. Celebration becomes food made in advance, clothes chosen for the occasion and a shared turn on a swing. The farming year is marked through leisure, giving rest a visibility that ordinary work seldom allows.
