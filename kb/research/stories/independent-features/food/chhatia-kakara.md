# Chhatia’s chhena kakara and the roadside economy

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Chhatia’s chhena kakara is associated with a particular kind of eating: stopping during a journey and buying a familiar sweet from a roadside shop. Its local identity belongs as much to that repeated encounter as to the wider family of Odia kakara pitha.

Food writer Sangram Keshari Senapati described his habit of seeking chhena kakara when passing Chhatia or Badachana in a 2020 account. He identifies it as a chhena-based form of the deep-fried sweet. His personal account records the association between the place and the purchase; individual shops may prepare the sweet differently. [^1]

Jajpur’s tourism planning also recognises kakara among the district’s foods and Chhatia Bata among its attractions. Food and pilgrimage appear within the same regional visitor economy, although the documents do not establish a single origin story for Chhatia’s sweet trade. [^2]

The importance of the roadside setting became sharply visible in September 2026. *The Times of India* reported an eviction drive by NHAI, the Jajpur administration and police along NH-16 at Chhatia. Officials said removal of encroachments was intended to improve visibility and road safety. Kakara seller Maheswar Sahoo told the newspaper that approximately thirty shops had been demolished and that many businesses had operated there for more than three decades. The number and duration were the seller’s account. [^3]

That report exposes the dependence of a food stop on its physical position. A recipe may survive a change of premises, but a roadside business also needs passing customers to notice it, stop and buy. The officials’ safety concerns and the sellers’ livelihood concerns meet at precisely that point of access.

Chhatia’s kakara therefore deserves a history of commerce alongside a description of taste. The sweet is carried away, while the work of preparing and selling it remains tied to a location. A familiar halt can become part of a traveller’s memory through repetition, yet the businesses sustaining it face decisions about roads, space and relocation.

The future of the familiar stop depends on more than preserving a recipe. It also depends on where the sellers can work and how their customers reach them.
