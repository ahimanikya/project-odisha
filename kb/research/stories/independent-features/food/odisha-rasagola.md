# Odisha Rasagola from the kneading board to Niladri Bije

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

The making of Odisha Rasagola turns on the handling of chhena. The fresh milk curd must become a smooth mixture that holds its shape in syrup while retaining a tender texture. Before the sweet reaches a festival plate or a shop counter, that transformation takes place under the sweetmaker’s hands.

The geographical-indication specification describes kneading on a board called a chhenapata. Chhena is repeatedly spread, gathered and pressed with the other ingredients; the specification includes semolina and refined flour as binders and small cardamom for flavour. The mixture is shaped and cooked in sugar syrup. Its defining characteristics include a juicy, yielding consistency, with both white and off-white versions recognised. Colour alone is therefore a poor test of whether a rasagola belongs to the Odisha tradition. [^1]

Its ceremonial setting is particularly distinctive. During Niladri Bije, the concluding return of the deities to the Jagannath temple after Ratha Jatra, rasagola enters the ritual exchange between Jagannath and Lakshmi. In the tradition, Lakshmi is displeased at being left behind and the sweet accompanies reconciliation. Here a confection becomes part of a dramatised relationship, carrying a meaning beyond its ingredients. [^2]

Formal recognition came on 29 July 2019, the certificate date recorded by India’s GI Registry for “Odisha Rasagola”. The application, filed in February 2018, names the Odisha Small Industries Corporation and Utkal Mistanna Byabasayee Samiti as applicants. The producers’ association belongs in this history alongside the government undertaking: the registered name concerns a product made and sold by a working trade. [^3]

The registry separately records “Banglar Rasogolla”, certified in November 2017. These are geographically qualified registrations, rather than a single award deciding every claim about the invention of the wider family of sweets. [^4]

The certificate identifies a regional product; the kneading board is where its texture begins. The sweetmaker’s handling of chhena connects the registered description with the soft confection offered at a temple or carried home from a shop.
