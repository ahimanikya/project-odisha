# Kharaldhas and the forts along the Jonk

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

The Jonk connects Kharaldhas to a landscape of ruined defences and excavated settlements in Nuapada. Following the river beyond the waterfall reveals a valley that has attracted more than scenic attention: its bends and neighbouring heights also frame the remains of earlier occupation.

Nuapada’s district account describes the river passing Beniadhas and Kharaldhas before continuing towards Maraguda. It places Jumlagarh within a pronounced bend of the Jonk and Manikgarh beside Kharaldhas. The account records masonry at these hill forts, giving the river corridor a built history alongside its rock and water. [^1]

Two stone lions associated with Manikgarh’s entrance are recorded as preserved in Sambalpur University’s Dr N. K. Sahu Museum. The detail is a reminder that the remains of a site can be divided between the landscape and a collection elsewhere. A fragment in a museum may help explain a ruined entrance whose original appearance is otherwise difficult to recover. [^2]

Farther along the valley, Maraguda has a history of archaeological investigation. A review in the *Utkal Historical Research Journal* describes work on several mounds, including excavations that exposed brick structures and sculptures associated with Shiva and Kartikeya. It also recounts investigations in 1982–83 of a substantial building with multiple rooms. These are traces of an organised settlement, adding domestic and religious dimensions to a landscape also marked by fortifications. [^3]

The waterfall and the archaeological sites need not belong to a single historical narrative to be considered together. Their value is in showing how a river valley holds different kinds of evidence: the channel describes the course of water, masonry marks construction, and excavated objects reveal some of the activities once carried out there.

At Kharaldhas, the view can therefore extend in two directions. One follows the fall itself; the other follows the Jonk towards the forts and settlement remains. That wider frame gives the waterfall a specific place in Nuapada’s history, beyond its appearance as an isolated cascade.
