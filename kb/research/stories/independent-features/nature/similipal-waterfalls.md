# The rivers beyond Similipal’s waterfalls

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Barehipani and Joranda offer two different forms of falling water within Similipal. Barehipani descends in tiers; Joranda is presented as a single drop against rock. Their contrast is an introduction to the forest’s terrain, where streams cross changes in elevation before continuing into a much larger drainage network. [^1]

Barehipani has a particularly clear downstream connection. Balasore’s district survey describes the Budhabalanga rising in the Similipal hills and passing through the falls. The river carries the forest’s water towards settlements and agricultural country beyond the reserve. What appears at the viewpoint as a contained spectacle is one reach of a continuing river. [^2]

The Tiger Conservation Plan develops that wider picture. It identifies Similipal as a watershed for rivers and streams including the Budhabalanga, Khairi, Bhandan, West Deo, Khadkei, Sanjo and Palpala. It also describes contrasts in altitude, rainfall and temperature, including frost valleys. These variations help explain why the forest contains many different ecological conditions within one named landscape. [^3]

UNESCO’s biosphere-reserve account likewise places perennial streams, hills and waterfalls within a diverse forest setting that includes sal. The vegetation and water belong in the same account: the attraction is the wooded catchment as well as the point at which a stream drops over rock. [^4]

Other named waterfalls have their own contexts. Mayurbhanj district describes Devkund as a destination combining a waterfall with religious visiting. It should not be treated as another name for the Barehipani–Joranda excursion. [^5]

Access, too, belongs to the management of a living forest. The reserve issues dated opening and closure notices, including seasonal restrictions. Those notices govern the available visit; an evergreen description cannot substitute for them. [^6]

At Barehipani, the Budhabalanga drops through the hills; farther downstream it enters a landscape of settlements and fields. The waterfall is a conspicuous moment in that journey, while the river connects the forest to places beyond the reserve.
