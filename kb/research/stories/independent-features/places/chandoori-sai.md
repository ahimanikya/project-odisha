# Chandoori Sai and the craft within its walls

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

The construction of Chandoori Sai offers a useful way into the guesthouse’s story. In Goudaguda, Koraput, floor tiles, earthen walls, pottery and ironwork became parts of a building whose character depended on local work as well as its founder’s plans.

Reporting in 2012, Debabrata Mohanty described how Leon Mahoney, an Australian who had worked in the oil industry, developed the guesthouse after visiting the region. Mahoney worked with local Paraja labourers to make floor tiles from local soil and finish them with a glossy surface. The report also described coloured earthen walls, pottery throughout the property and pieces commissioned from local blacksmiths. [^1]

Those details give the architecture an identifiable material history. The floor was made through a process involving soil, firing and finishing. Ironwork brought another set of skills into the rooms. The building’s appearance emerged from those operations, rather than from a decorative label applied afterwards.

Goudaguda’s pottery provides a broader setting. In an *Outlook Traveller* account, Jyothy Karat described potters working in the village square and nearby weekly markets as places of exchange. The guesthouse was one part of a village with its own productive and commercial life. Pottery was work carried out there, not simply an amenity supplied for travellers. [^2]

The operator’s present room description continues to emphasise earthen surfaces, terracotta floors, bamboo ceilings and carved furniture. That is a description supplied by the business, not an independent assessment of service or comfort. It nevertheless shows how central the materials remain to the property’s identity. [^3]

The most interesting question this history raises is what a visitor learns to recognise. A floor tile can be noticed as an object with a method of manufacture. A pot can lead to an interest in the people making and selling pottery beyond the guesthouse. Architecture can direct attention outward towards the place that helped produce it.

Mahoney’s plans took material form through the work of tile makers, builders and metalworkers. The earthen surfaces and iron fittings preserve that collaboration in the rooms themselves.
