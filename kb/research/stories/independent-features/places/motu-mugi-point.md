# Motu where a confluence connects three states

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

At Mugi Point near Motu, the Sabari and Sileru meet in a border landscape shared by Odisha, Chhattisgarh and Andhra Pradesh. Odisha Tourism identifies the confluence and nearby Madhusudan Park as features of the destination. The view offers an unusually direct encounter with geography: rivers divide jurisdictions while joining their waters. [^1]

The Godavari belongs to the wider system, but it does not form a third river at this confluence. A technical paper presented at India Water Week in 2024 locates Konta just downstream of the Sabari–Sileru meeting and roughly 30 kilometres upstream of the Sabari’s junction with the Godavari. Keeping those two junctions distinct makes Motu’s flood history easier to understand. [^2]

In July 2022, water arriving from the wider basin transformed the local scene. PTI reported that Godavari backwaters had entered the Motu area along the Sabari and Sileru. Officials identified Binayakpur, Alama and Peta among inundated villages, and residents were moved to safety. The confluence that attracts visitors was also part of a river system capable of cutting off homes and roads. [^3]

The 2024 technical analysis helps explain the connection. Its authors found that high downstream water levels influenced levels at Chinturu and Konta for about twenty days during July 2022. The usual relationship between water level and discharge became unreliable under these backwater conditions. In plain terms, a high river at Motu’s neighbouring gauge could reflect water held up downstream, rather than only a surge arriving from upstream. [^4]

This makes the landscape intelligible at two scales. The immediate scene is a meeting of two rivers. The larger setting includes rainfall, channels and water levels well beyond the visible banks. Administrative borders matter for people and services, but the hydraulic connection continues across them.

At Mugi Point, two rivers meet within view. The July 2022 floods showed how strongly that visible junction remains connected to water levels farther downstream.
