# Rabandhara from a seasonal gorge to a nature camp

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Rabandhara’s waterfall occupies a small mountain gorge near Bhawanipatna. Kalahandi’s district account places it roughly 13 kilometres from the town and describes a stream that becomes very low in the hot season. Downstream, a minor irrigation project uses its water for agriculture. The same flow therefore belongs to both a scenic destination and a working rural landscape. [^1]

Seasonality is central to that description. A waterfall photograph captures a particular volume of water; it cannot stand for the gorge throughout the year. At Rabandhara, the district’s account makes the changing stream part of the place’s identity, rather than an inconvenience to leave out of a visitor narrative.

Tourism has added another use to the area. Odisha’s 2024 forestry report lists Rabandhara Nature Camp under Kalahandi North Division. A local feature published in October 2026 also records the camp in its discussion of the waterfall and the division’s development work. [^2], [^3]

The forestry report explains the wider nature-camp model through the work of community members: reception, cooking, housekeeping, stores and accounts. Its income-sharing framework provides for wages, recurring expenses, infrastructure maintenance and local development. These are statewide programme arrangements, not published earnings for Rabandhara itself. [^4]

That framework makes hospitality visible as labour. An overnight visitor needs food, a clean room, information and someone to manage the booking. The value retained locally depends on how those responsibilities and payments are organised. The waterfall may draw attention, but hosting creates a different set of daily tasks.

Rabandhara consequently brings three uses of water into one short regional story: a seasonal cascade, irrigation downstream and a setting for tourism. They do not have identical needs. Agriculture depends on usable supply, while visitors often seek a particular appearance and experience.

The gorge, the irrigation works downstream and the nature camp all depend on the same seasonal setting. A fuller account of Rabandhara includes the water available for farming and the work of receiving guests alongside the appearance of the falls.
