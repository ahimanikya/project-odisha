# Sonapur where beach management meets a river mouth

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Sonapur’s geography gives it two waterfronts to think about. At this Ganjam destination, the Bahuda reaches the Bay of Bengal, bringing a river-mouth setting to the experience of the open coast. The beach is also part of Odisha’s effort to develop managed visitor facilities under the Blue Flag programme. [^1]

The Tourism Department’s 2023–24 annual report records Sonapur’s certification in January 2024. It describes showers, changing rooms, toilets, parking, a pedestrian route, waste-management arrangements and facilities for visitors with disabilities. These are practical interventions in how a beach is used: they influence where people move, how long they can stay and whether basic services are available. [^2]

Recognition continued in a later award period. Odisha’s Economic Survey for 2025–26 records renewed Blue Flag certification for Pati Sonapur and Puri for that season. The dated record is more useful than treating the flag as a permanent accolade. [^3]

Blue Flag’s requirements explain what the recognition is intended to support. They cover bathing-water quality, environmental information, waste, habitat management, accessibility and safety. Water quality depends on sampling and analysis, while visitors must be able to find relevant information. The programme also expects compliance to continue throughout the applicable award period. [^4]

That makes upkeep central to the beach’s public value. A changing room is useful only while it is maintained; waste collection needs a route beyond the bin; an accessible path must lead to facilities people can actually use. These are recurring services, rather than achievements completed when construction ends.

The environmental requirements extend beyond tidiness. Blue Flag calls for appropriate treatment of sensitive habitats, coastal vegetation, dunes and natural debris. A well-managed shore therefore needs more judgement than simply removing everything that washes onto the sand. [^5]

At a river mouth, beach management has to accommodate visitors and a changing natural shore. Sonapur’s awards recognise periods of that work; its usefulness to people depends on the paths, services and environmental care continuing after the flag is raised.
