# Institution building, measured one stage at a time

An education story begins with a possibility: someone gets the chance to study, and a different future becomes imaginable. How do we tell that story without making the possibility stand in for the result? Follow three separate questions—what institution is being described, what resources it reports, and what students experience.

KISS Deemed to be University provides a dated case for that approach. Its records give us something concrete to read. They also show why an institution’s scale and a student’s outcome need different evidence.

## First, know which institution is in the frame

The **25 August 2017 notification** explicitly distinguishes the proposed deemed university from the school run by KISS. Its school de-linking condition and stated compliance appear on page 2. That is a historical legal stage, not a current programme-approval check. University accounts and university student rows should not be turned into combined figures for the school, Foundation or KIIT. [Notification, p.2](https://university.kiss.ac.in/docs/IIQA/KISS-DU-NOTIFICATION-DT-25.08.2017.pdf).

This boundary makes the story more useful: a reader can follow a named institution instead of an impressive number whose population changes from sentence to sentence.

## What the accounts let us see

The university-labelled income and expenditure statement reports **₹25.20 crore in total expenses for FY2024–25**. Its prior-year comparative column reports **₹24.02 crore for FY2023–24**. The exact totals are ₹252,023,295 and ₹240,170,499; their change is about **4.94% in nominal rupees**. These are expense figures including depreciation and amortisation, not cash payments or a measured improvement in education. Signatures are visible on the statement; a separate audit opinion has not been established by this review. [Statement, p.2](https://university.kiss.ac.in/docs/Accounts/Balance_Sheets/KISSDU_FY_2024-25.pdf).

For a later illustration, two bars could show that financial scale, with the institution, financial years and nominal basis printed directly beside them. A larger bar would mean a larger reported expense total. It would not mean a larger change in students’ lives.

## A different lens: tuition support

The **2025 NIRF SDG submission** reports **948 students across two-year postgraduate programmes**, with full tuition reimbursement from institutional funds recorded for all 948. The row covers programme years together and supplies no explicit census day. It is a university submission about tuition support, not a current enrolment total, a promise to applicants or evidence that every living cost is covered. [Submission, p.1](https://university.kiss.ac.in/docs/NIRF/SDG_Institutions.pdf).

The human question is easy to understand: what does removing a tuition charge make possible for a student? The row gives a reason to ask that question. It does not supply the student’s answer, demonstrate learning gains or establish an employment outcome. No participant account has been collected for this draft.

Keep the support count beside the accounts as a separate panel. **Do not divide total university expenses by 948 to invent a cost per student:** the expense statement and that particular PG population do not establish a matching accounting scope and period.

These are reporting questions, not a new evaluation of the institution. With permission and suitable evidence, a future student account could explain a course, an aspiration or what changed after study. We cannot substitute an imagined voice for that work.

## A second lens: access to postgraduate study

**948 postgraduate students with institution-funded full tuition reimbursement** are reported in KISS’s 2025 NIRF SDG submission. This covers two-year PG programmes across study years, with no explicit census day on the row. It is institution-reported tuition support, not proof of independently measured learning or employment impact. [Submission, p.1](https://university.kiss.ac.in/docs/NIRF/SDG_Institutions.pdf).

Use this as a separate access card alongside the financial story; do not divide expenses by this PG count to invent per-student costs. [Detailed outcomes and limitations](../people/achyuta-samanta.md).
