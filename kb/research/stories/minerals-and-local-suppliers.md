# Beyond the metal: Odisha’s small suppliers

An industrial story need not stop at a mine or a plant. Ask who supplies the goods and services that keep the work moving. That question opens a door towards smaller businesses—and towards the records that can connect a company’s purchasing to a particular enterprise.

NALCO’s reports provide a starting point. They identify procurement from Odisha-based micro and small enterprises (MSEs). The figures show a business connection worth investigating; they do not yet tell us a supplier’s profit, a worker’s pay or a family’s experience.

## A local connection, with its denominator visible

For **FY2025–26**, NALCO reports **₹652.19 crore** in goods and services purchases from Odisha-based MSEs, within **₹1,447.94 crore** from MSEs across locations. The Odisha-based portion is about **45.04% of that MSE total**. It is not a share of all company purchases. “Odisha-based” describes the supplier location in the disclosure; it does not prove where every purchased item was manufactured. [Annual report, printed p.56 / PDF p.76, section19(a)](https://nalcoindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/45th-Annual-Report-2025-26.pdf).

The preceding report year gives the comparison another dimension.

| NALCO reporting year | Purchases from Odisha-based MSEs, ₹ crore | Purchases from all-location MSEs, ₹ crore | Odisha-based share of this MSE total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FY2024–25 | 570.46 | 898.76 | 63.47% |
| FY2025–26 | 652.19 | 1,447.94 | 45.04% |

The earlier values come from NALCO’s sustainable-development report, printed p.41 / PDF p.43. Shares are our calculations from the displayed purchase values, rounded to two decimal places. Both publications have corporate provenance; two report editions are not independent corroboration. [FY2024–25 report](https://nalcoindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/NALCO-SD-Report-2025-Final.pdf).

**The reported Odisha-based value rose, while its share of the MSE total fell.** Those statements can both be true because the denominator also changed. Neither establishes the cause, the number of businesses benefiting or higher household incomes. The reports’ separate policy-related procurement percentages answer a different denominator question and should not replace the location shares above.

## Follow the paper trail towards one enterprise

To turn this aggregate into a human story, begin with an actual supplier and its work. The following is a reporting path, not a description of a contract we have verified:

| Stage to investigate | Evidence to look for | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entry | Dated registration and enterprise classification | That the business received an order |
| Award | Named purchase order or contract with value and scope | That work was completed or paid |
| Delivery | Completion or acceptance record | The supplier’s margin or household income |
| Payment and experience | Invoice/payment evidence and permitted participant account | A statewide benefit from one case |

A supplier could explain a difficult specification, the work behind a delivery or what receiving payment meant. These are questions for future reporting. No supplier interview, award, receipt or endorsement has been collected for this draft.

## Keep neighbouring industrial cases separate

The [bauxite-to-aluminium case](../economy/bauxite-to-aluminium.md) supplies NALCO’s wider industrial context. [Rourkela’s steel case](../economy/rourkela-steel-chain.md) follows another plant and another evidence trail. Its recorded purchase-order measure must remain separate from NALCO’s procurement figures; adding them would not produce a verified Odisha total.

For a later visual treatment, show each financial year’s MSE total as a separately labelled bar, divided by supplier location. Print the total and the Odisha-based amount beside each. Do not use equal-length share bars alone if the point is to show both amount and share. A supplier photograph would need an identified business, permission and usable rights; a generic factory image cannot stand in for their testimony.

The invitation is to look beyond the finished metal. A purchasing figure can point towards a business relationship. A carefully evidenced enterprise story can help us understand the work within it.

## What can be compared

NALCO’s FY2024–25 report records ₹570.46 crore from Odisha-based MSEs within ₹898.76 crore from all MSEs. Preserve these alongside the following year’s figures, with report editions and denominators visible. Supplier location does not prove where goods were manufactured. Rourkela’s ₹133.46 crore disclosure measures purchase orders placed on Odisha-based LSSIs. It is a separate measure, so we do not combine it with NALCO’s purchases into an Odisha total. [RSP source](../sources/mining-rsp-subplac2026.md).
