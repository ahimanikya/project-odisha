# Puri, Digha and the meaning of Dham

*Odisha Roots · Story 5*

**One word on a temple’s name brought two governments into an argument about sacred belonging.**

Begin with an inauguration notice. It gives a place, a ceremony and a name: **“Jagannathdham” at Digha**. The West Bengal government notice announces consecration for **30 April 2025**.

The word that would become contentious was already there, in an official invitation to a new institution. It was more than a caption added by an enthusiastic visitor.

For a traveller, a name can look like a simple aid to finding somewhere. For a community, it may carry a much longer account of why that place matters.

## What Odisha asked to protect

On **6 May 2025**, Odisha Chief Minister Mohan Charan Majhi wrote to his West Bengal counterpart, Mamata Banerjee. As reported by *The New Indian Express*, he asked her to reconsider the use of “Dham” in the Digha temple’s official name, communications and promotion.

His stated concern was Puri’s distinctive sacred identity and the possibility of confusion or its dilution. He also acknowledged the devotion of people in West Bengal. The request addressed what the new temple should be called; it did not amount to a demand that Jagannath should have no devotees or temples beyond Odisha.

That distinction is worth carrying through the argument. Affection for a deity and recognition of a particular sacred centre are connected questions, but they are not identical.

For Odisha’s tourism storytelling, it is also a practical distinction. Introducing Puri well means explaining its own history and religious significance. A visitor should understand why this place matters before being asked to take a side in a naming dispute.

## Another meaning of the same word

Radharamn Das, vice-president of ISKCON Kolkata, offered a different explanation in a **6 May 2025** interview with *Millennium Post*. Following consecration, he argued, a temple becomes an abode of the deity, and “Dham” can describe that abode. He also said that recognising Digha in this way did not diminish Puri.

This was a stated religious interpretation from a named participant. It should be heard as such, without making it the final ruling for every tradition.

The disagreement becomes easier to understand when both meanings are visible. One argument sought to protect an established place’s particular standing. The other defended a broader devotional use of a word. Repeating only the accusation or only the dictionary-like explanation would leave much of the dispute unexplained.

## The story changed in 2026

There is a later chapter that accounts frozen in 2025 will miss.

On **10 June 2026**, *The Indian Express* reported that the new West Bengal government had announced the previous day that it would remove “Dham” from the Digha temple’s name, accepting Odisha’s request. Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari said the complex would remain and worship would continue.

This establishes a reported government announcement. For this investigation, the administrative order and completed changes to signage and records have not been independently verified. An older inauguration page still displaying its original title cannot, by itself, tell us whether the later decision was implemented.

## A name deserves an explanation

Sacred places enter travel websites, maps and advertisements. The shorter those descriptions become, the easier it is to lose the distinctions their communities care about.

Our response can be specific: name the temple, locate it accurately, explain the tradition being described and date any account of a dispute. When quoting a religious or political position, keep its speaker attached.

Before sharing a temple photograph, look at the caption once more. Does it merely identify a building, or quietly claim a standing in a much older sacred geography? That small question is where this story begins.
