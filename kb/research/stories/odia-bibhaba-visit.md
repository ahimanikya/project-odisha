I attended the unveiling of Odia Bibhaba’s redesigned website at Lohia Academy in Bhubaneswar on 15 November. It was a good experience, and a welcome opportunity to spend a morning with people gathered around the preservation of Odia writing.

I had already written about [Odia Bibhaba and the work of keeping literature available](/stories/odia-literature-preservation/). This visit gave that article a personal connection: the archive I had been writing about was now the reason for being together in a room.

![A speaker at the lectern and an audience seated at the Odia Bibhaba event.](https://projectodisha.org/assets/odia-bibhaba-visit/meeting-hall.jpg)

*At Lohia Academy, Bhubaneswar, for Odia Bibhaba’s website relaunch.*

The banner called the occasion *Odia Bibhaba’s Nabakalebara*—a renewal, or new form. That is a fitting description. This was a new presentation of an existing collection, built through years of work by Srujanika. The handout made the distinction plainly: the information and material remained, while the website had been rebuilt with readers in mind.[^event]

The changes described were practical ones: easier use on a mobile phone, reading a book or magazine online without downloading it each time, and returning to the page where reading stopped.[^handout] Such details matter when an archive becomes part of someone’s everyday reading. Finding a title is one step; being able to settle down with its pages is another.

![Participants gathered beside a projected Odia Bibhaba website launch screen.](https://projectodisha.org/assets/odia-bibhaba-visit/website-launch.jpg)

*Gathering around the screen for the launch of the redesigned website.*

The handout also brought attention back to the work that continues beyond a launch. Rare books and periodicals remain scattered across private collections and institutions. Bibliographical records need checking, and biographical entries need additions and corrections. Keeping this material available requires patient, continuing effort.[^handout]

For me, the morning was a chance to connect an interest in Odia literature with a gathering devoted to its care. I am glad I attended. My earlier article follows the archive’s history and its place alongside other reading and listening resources; this is a small personal note from being there for its next step.
