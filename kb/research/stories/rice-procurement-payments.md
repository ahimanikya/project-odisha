# The money moving through Odisha’s paddy procurement

**A payment total opens the story. A farmer’s own accounts would carry it further.**

Rice belongs to everyday life in Odisha. Behind the meal is another set of questions: how much paddy entered government procurement, what payments the department reported, and what a grower kept after the work of producing it. The figures below help with the first two questions. The third needs evidence closer to the farm.

## Read the quantity in two seasons

The Food Supplies & Consumer Welfare Department’s *Annual Activity Report 2025–2026* lists these paddy quantities for **KMS 2024–25**, the marketing-year record.[^rice-fscw2026]

| Season | Paddy procured, tonnes |
| --- | ---: |
| Kharif | 7,345,707 |
| Rabi | 1,918,528 |
| **Derived total** | **9,264,235** |

The total is about **92.64 lakh tonnes**, consistent with the report’s rounded KMS summary. This measures paddy procured through the reported system, rather than all rice produced in Odisha. Paddy and milled rice also need their own quantity definitions.

The seasonal farmer columns require a different kind of care. Someone may sell in both seasons. Adding the columns cannot tell us how many distinct people sold paddy over the year.

## Read what the payment includes

For that same **KMS 2024–25**, the department reports **₹28,718 crore paid as MSP plus input assistance**. MSP means minimum support price. Keep both parts of the payment category beside the headline.[^rice-fscw2026]

The earlier year’s payment figure in this report covers MSP alone. A percentage comparison between those two totals would combine a change in money with a change in what the money includes. The [rice-economy research](../economy/rice-economy.md) preserves the earlier-report disagreement and the arithmetic reconciliation.

There is a second boundary around the figures: the report gives **86.30 lakh tonnes** for the financial-year window **1 April 2024–31 March 2025**, alongside its separate KMS total. Those are different reporting windows, not two interchangeable answers to the same question.[^rice-fscw2026]

## Follow the money towards a household

A reported procurement payment can tell us about the scale of a public purchasing system. It does not tell us a farmer’s profit. We would need actual receipts, production costs, labour, land and the relevant season to understand what remained for a particular grower.

For future reporting, begin with a willing farmer’s account of one harvest. What was sold, when was payment received, what expenses had already been paid, and how does the household account for its own labour? These are proposed questions, not testimony collected by Project Odisha.

The existing [cost-and-income story](rice-income-after-costs.md) offers a historical regional example of why gross value and income after defined costs differ. It cannot be used to turn this statewide procurement payment into a current profit estimate.

The desire to understand Odisha’s rice economy begins at a familiar meal. It becomes more useful when we follow a figure far enough to know whose question it answers—and whose experience still needs to be heard.
