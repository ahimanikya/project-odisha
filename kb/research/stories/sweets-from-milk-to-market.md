# From milk to market: the work behind a sweet

**The customer sees a sweet. Follow it backwards and a network of work appears.**

A rasagola offers a small invitation to look beyond the counter. Who supplied the chhena? Who brought it to the maker? Where did preparation end and selling begin? Those questions make food identity more tangible, connecting something familiar with work that is less visible.

## Begin with one documented chain

Abinash Sahoo, Shakti Ranjan Panigrahy and Arpita Mohapatra’s **December 2023 Pahala case study** describes chhena supplied from Niali in Cuttack district, traders carrying it towards Pahala, and sweet making connected with retailing. Its diagram also follows sweets towards commuters, other Odisha markets and West Bengal.

**Chhena producers → traders → Pahala making and retailing → customers**

This simplified reading of the study’s Figure 2 shows roles, not a measured division of earnings. The account interviewed **10 retailers and 200 customers**; fieldwork dates are unstated. It is one case study, not a census of Odisha’s sweet businesses.

## Give each step its own question

| Follow the work | A question for future reporting |
| --- | --- |
| Ingredient supply | Which producer supplied this batch, and what record identifies it? |
| Transport and trading | Who moved it, and which costs belonged to that journey? |
| Making | Who prepared the sweet, and which parts of the work do they want credited? |
| Selling | What was actually sold in an agreed period, and what remained unsold? |

These are reporting prompts. They are not answers collected from a maker, a recipe or instructions for handling food.

The study identifies perishability and packaging among the business questions. Those concerns give a future profile something concrete to investigate. They do not establish a growth forecast or a handling rule for a particular product.

## Keep the shop’s story larger than a revenue headline

The paper’s revenue extrapolation has unresolved quantity and arithmetic conflicts. It is excluded from this article’s account of the market. We cannot turn a shop count and a disputed calculation into measured cluster turnover, or call revenue a maker’s income after costs.

A stronger account would connect a dated batch, completed sales, expenses and actual receipts, with the people involved choosing what they wish to share. Where a record is missing, keep the question open rather than filling it with an attractive estimate.
