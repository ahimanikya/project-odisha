# Forest products, local enterprise: a documented Odisha strength

**Look beyond the finished product. Ask about the work between gathering and selling.**

A leaf plate is a useful starting point. It invites us to notice a material, a way of making and the people whose work carries it towards a customer. Odisha’s existing enterprise records offer names to begin with—and questions that a sales total alone cannot answer.

## Follow a product towards its makers

A **4 May 2021 Ministry of Tribal Affairs account** describes planned processing in named Van Dhan clusters. The table below follows that historical account; it is not a directory of businesses operating today.

| Historical lead | Place in the account | Planned product work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Luguburu, Maa Dharithri and Bhimakund, collectively | Mayurbhanj | Sal-leaf plates and cups, kusum oil and processed wild honey |
| Anchalika Khandadhar | Keonjhar | Mango preparations, turmeric powder and mustard oil |
| Ban Durga | District not stated in the relevant paragraph | Deseeded tamarind, tamarind cake, sal shampoo and packed char seeds |

Keep the three Mayurbhanj names together: the source does not assign every product to each cluster. Its plans and general early-production description do not establish which group subsequently sold what.

Still, the leads give us a way to look more closely. Processing, packaging and finding a buyer are different steps. For a future profile, follow one product through those steps with the people willing to explain their work. Ask which tasks they undertake and what they want a reader to understand. No such interview is claimed here.

## Put the sales figure in its own frame

An **11 December 2023 parliamentary reply** reports **₹1,072.01 lakh**, or **₹10.7201 crore**, in cumulative Odisha Van Dhan sales. Odisha had the highest reported amount among that annexure’s 28 state/UT rows: about **26.1%** of its ₹4,102.95 lakh total.

This is historical programme reporting, covering both minor forest produce and non-MFP activities. It is not annual turnover, Odisha’s whole forest economy or a present-day ranking. The reply does not specify a precise common sales cutoff.

Nor can the statewide sum be distributed across the named clusters above. Their individual sales and collector payments require separate evidence. A sanctioned centre is not proof of an operating shop, and a beneficiary count is not an income statement.

## What would make the next account stronger?

Start with one product, a defined period and an agreed account of the work. Match completed sales with processing costs and payments actually received by collectors or producers. Keep these different measures beside one another rather than letting a large headline stand in for all of them.

For readers, the invitation can be smaller: ask who made a product and how its material was processed. Record or publish someone’s explanation only with their permission and agreed credit. These historical cluster names confer no access to a community or workshop, and this page makes no purchase or visit promise.

Continue through the [forest-product economy research](../economy/forest-products.md), or read how [worker support](forest-worker-support.md) differs from trade earnings. The story worth pursuing is what the people doing the work choose to share, supported by records that show where the value went.
