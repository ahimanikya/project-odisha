I finally watched Prahlad Natak, something I had been looking forward to for a long time. I loved its distinctive dance style, and I came away just as impressed by the care with which the occasion had been organised.

Under a yellow-and-white canopy edged in pink, the performers shared an open space with their audience. People sat on mats along the sides, with rows of chairs farther back. A red carpet ran towards the painted, stepped platform at the other end. It was a setting in which we could watch the performers at close quarters.

![Performers and audience sharing a red-carpeted space beneath a yellow-and-white canopy.](https://projectodisha.org/assets/watching-prahlad-natak/performance-space.jpg)

*The performance space, with the audience seated along its sides.*

## Watching from close by

The costumes were extraordinary to look at: tall, intricate headgear, red jackets, layers of bright colour, striped leggings and ankle bells. The large dark beard of one character made a striking contrast with the elaborate decoration around the face. There was plenty to notice before even turning to the movement.

But it was the dance style that especially held my interest. Seeing the gestures, stances and movement together gave the costumes a different presence. The photographs keep individual moments—a raised hand, two performers facing one another, figures arranged on the stepped platform. Watching them unfold in a live performance was the part I had waited for.

![Two Prahlad Natak performers in elaborate headgear, red jackets and layered, brightly coloured costumes.](https://projectodisha.org/assets/watching-prahlad-natak/performers-close.jpg)

*The headgear and layered costumes seen at close quarters.*

The proximity of the audience was part of the experience. The performers were close enough for us to see the details of their dress and expressions, while the people sitting along the sides remained part of the scene. I liked that sense of sharing the space.

![Costumed performers arranged on and beside a blue-and-red stepped platform.](https://projectodisha.org/assets/watching-prahlad-natak/stepped-platform.jpg)

*The painted platform at one end of the performance space.*

## The people behind the occasion

I was very impressed by how the event was organised. There is considerable work behind bringing a performance and an audience together, and I want to acknowledge that care alongside the work of the artists.

It was a pleasure to meet the organiser and Odissi singer Soumya Biswajit. He kindly gifted me his book, *Kishorachandrananda Champu*. That generous gesture added a personal memory to an occasion I had already enjoyed so much.

![Three people posing at the venue, with a copy of Kishorachandrananda Champu held in the centre.](https://projectodisha.org/assets/watching-prahlad-natak/book-at-venue.jpg)

*A photograph at the venue with the gifted book, Kishorachandrananda Champu.*

My thanks to Soumya, the performers and everyone who helped organise the event. I had wanted to see Prahlad Natak for a long time. Now I have the pleasure of remembering the performance—and a book to spend time with next.
