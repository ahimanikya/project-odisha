# Odisha merchandise exports: five-year scale change

An export figure is a way of seeing a connection that may otherwise be invisible. Somewhere beyond the familiar place of making, goods find another market. A statewide value gives that connection a scale. To understand what it means, we need to keep the kind of export, the years and the price basis beside the number.

The Odisha Economic Survey’s goods-export endpoints show a larger nominal rupee value in FY2024–25 than in FY2019–20. That is an opening for a story about markets and work. It does not yet tell us how much more material travelled, whether the same products were sold, or what a particular household earned.

## Begin with the right basket

The survey separates merchandise from services. Its section 1.7.2 supplies the goods comparison retained below. Section 1.7.1 and Figure 1.15 describe the larger goods-plus-services total; that chart must not be used as if it were the merchandise comparison. Figure 1.16 instead shows the composition of merchandise exports for FY2024–25.

These distinctions make the story clearer. We can ask about the value of goods without quietly changing the basket midway through the comparison.

## The dated goods-value evidence

The survey reports **₹47,240 crore in FY2019–20** and **₹85,540 crore in FY2024–25**. These endpoints imply **81.1% nominal growth**, or **12.61% CAGR**.[^maritime-exports-2026]

## Reuse

Use “81% higher over five years” with the years and current-price basis visible. This endpoint comparison does not establish growth every year, an inflation-adjusted increase or a record peak.

## Measure boundaries

Merchandise means goods. Do not substitute the survey’s larger goods-plus-services figure or port tonnage. A country-share table also needs its own denominator; do not infer a rupee amount for a destination until that basis is reconciled.

## Growth has more than one meaning

The endpoint change describes how much higher the reported nominal value is at the end than at the beginning. The compound annual growth rate expresses an equivalent constant annual rate across those five intervals. It is a calculation from the endpoints, not a report that every intervening year grew by that rate.

Nominal rupees do not isolate price changes from quantity or changes in the mix of goods. An inflation-adjusted comparison needs a suitable, documented price basis. We should not apply an unrelated inflation measure and call the result real export growth. Keep the endpoint account useful on its own terms before adding explanations it cannot support.

## Follow one product before drawing its voyage

A fuller reported story could begin with an actual producer or exporter willing to describe one product and a dated transaction. What was made, who performed the work, what quantity was sold, and which records connect it to a destination? Which costs sit between sales and what the business retained? If a worker speaks, distinguish their experience from company turnover and the state total.

Those are reporting questions, not interviews or inspected commercial records. Do not infer a particular port or foreign buyer from a statewide export amount. The [Paradip cargo story](paradip-throughput.md) measures handled tonnes at a port; the [ferrochrome story](../stories/odisha-ferrochrome-markets.md) describes a company. They bring different scales into view without creating a verified supply chain between them.

The [destination-share record](export-destinations.md) retains its denominator question. A country percentage is not permission to multiply this merchandise total into a country-level rupee estimate. Keep the original scope hold until the matching underlying table is reconciled.

## A picture that helps the reader count correctly

For a later graphic, use two same-scale bars titled “Odisha merchandise exports”, label the full financial years and nominal ₹crore, and show the endpoint comparison. Keep an annual-rate annotation separate from any actual annual series. A composition graphic needs its own FY2024–25 label and original categories; do not use decorative product pictures to imply measured category weights or specific shipments.

A verified, permitted photograph can introduce actual making and its setting. It cannot prove the photographed object was exported. An honest visual leaves the viewer curious about that next connection rather than pretending it has already been established.

### What state origin means

DGCI&S compiles declared state-of-origin codes from Customs returns and does not validate them. Its disclaimer notes missing or mistaken codes and mixed-origin shipment limitations. This supports “exports attributed to Odisha”, not verified production origin for every item. No Odisha-specific misclassification rate is known. Port tonnage is a different measure.
