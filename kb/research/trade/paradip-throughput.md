# Paradip: cargo has more than doubled over a decade

A port is a place to imagine connections. A cargo figure gives that imagination a scale—but it does not tell the whole journey. Before drawing a line from Odisha to the world, ask what was counted, where it moved, and which part of the journey the record can actually show.

Paradip’s reported cargo handled in FY2025–26 was more than twice its FY2015–16 total. That is a substantial change in activity at a named port. It is not a statement that Odisha’s exports doubled, that every cargo originated within the state, or that an individual worker’s earnings rose with the tonnage.

## The sea also connects places within India

The coastal-cargo component matters to this story. It sits inside the full cargo total, rather than alongside it as another amount to add. Keeping that component visible stops a picture of a ship from becoming an automatic picture of international exports.

The original endpoint and coastal figures are retained in the evidence panel below. The port’s 1 April 2026 release reports the FY2025–26 outcome; its forecasts and proposed developments are separate claims and are not treated here as completed capacity.

## The dated cargo evidence

Cargo handled rose from **76.39 million tonnes in FY2015–16** to **156.45 million tonnes in FY2025–26**: **104.8% growth**, calculated from the reported endpoints.[^maritime-paradip-2016][^maritime-paradip-2026]

The latest port release identifies **65.81 million tonnes**, or **42.06%**, as coastal cargo.[^maritime-paradip-2026]

## Interpretation

This is all-cargo throughput, not Odisha-origin exports or international trade alone. It measures a port’s activity, including cargo serving a wider hinterland. Capacity is a different variable.

## Reuse

Headline: “More than twice the cargo. One decade.” Place the port name, financial years and unit immediately below it. The comparison is not an all-India ranking.

## Three questions before the headline travels

| Question | What this record supplies | What needs different evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How much cargo was handled? | A physical throughput total at Paradip for a financial year | A comparable annual series for the years between the endpoints |
| Where did it come from, and where did it go? | A coastal component within the overall port measure | Dated commodity, inward/outward and origin/destination records for a specific cargo |
| What did the activity mean for people? | A reason to investigate work and services around the port | Actual employment, compensation, business costs and permitted participant accounts |

Throughput is activity measured in tonnes. Installed capacity describes a different capability; a project announcement does not establish that capability is operating. Cargo tonnage also cannot be turned into export value or household income by changing the caption. Each next question deserves a matching record.

## Put people into the reporting, not into an invented scene

A future account could follow a willing worker’s part in handling, planning or moving cargo. Begin with the actual role, date and permission. What task does the person perform? Which record explains the cargo or operation being described? What can be shown without exposing restricted operations or someone’s private information? Distinguish their own experience from the port’s aggregate disclosure.

No worker interview, operational access or identified shipment is claimed here. A port photograph can establish its documented setting and capture date; it cannot prove the origin, destination or value of a cargo visible in the frame.

The [institutional bridge](paradip-bridge.md) offers a different connection: a dated maritime commemoration organised by the port authority. The [merchandise-export record](merchandise-exports.md) follows a different measure. Read these chapters together, keeping remembrance, handled cargo and export value separate rather than presenting them as one continuous business lineage.

## Cargo is almost flat so far this financial year · 11 October 2026

**Paradip’s cargo was up just 0.16% through 10 October**, with 80.691 million tonnes in FY 2026–27 against 80.561 million tonnes through the same date in FY 2025–26. The increase was 129,652 tonnes. This is a partial-year comparison, separate from the full-year figures above.[^weekly-paradip-oct2026]

| 1 April–10 October | 80,561,351 | 80,691,003 | +0.16% |
| 1–10 October | 3,781,301 | 4,727,031 | +25.01% |

The stronger first ten days of October have not translated into comparable growth across the financial year so far. Neither window is a forecast. These are cargo movements at Paradip, including inward and outward flows serving a wider hinterland; they do not measure Odisha-origin exports, economic impact or capacity. The report is dated 11 October and its cargo summary closes on 10 October. Exact integers and the two calculations are retained in the structured records.

**Paradip handled 86.153 million tonnes from 1 April to 23 October, 0.35% above the same period last year.** The prior-year total was 85.855 million tonnes: a difference of 297,568 tonnes. The report dated 24 October closes cargo on 23 October.[^weekly-paradip-20261024]
