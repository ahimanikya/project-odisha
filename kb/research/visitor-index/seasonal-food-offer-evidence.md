# From festival food to a dated serving record

A seasonal food guide becomes useful when it connects a cultural story to a particular meal and a particular occasion. The evidence below shows three different stages of that connection. None confirms a meal that a visitor can buy today.

## Raja food on the road in 2021

Odisha Tourism’s July 2021 newsletter describes OTDC adapting Raja food sales to pandemic restrictions. Its retrospective account places takeaway vehicles in six Bhubaneswar areas: Master Canteen Square, Khandagiri, CRP, Patia, Chandrasekharpur and Rupali Square. It names poda pitha, manda, kakara, arisa, chakuli and chandrakala, with service from 14–16 June 2021,7 am–11 pm. Separate OTDC counters in Bhubaneswar, Cuttack and Sambalpur are also mentioned; their hours are not separately specified.[^food-raja-otdc2021]

This is a documented moment in how festival food reached customers. It supports a story about changing distribution during lockdown. It does not establish the origins of the dishes, the extent of household practice, prices or continued operation at those locations. The assortment is described for the programme, not verified as stock at every stop.

## A named operator still needs a year

OTDC’s own Raja Mahotsav card describes sales through hospitality units, including Nimantran in Bhubaneswar and Silver City Boat Club in Cuttack, and takeaway vans at named city areas. The card gives 14–16 June without a year. It is therefore an undated operator account, not a confirmed 2026 or 2027 offer. Its wording and locations must not be merged into the 2021 programme.[^food-otdc-raja-undated]

## A calendar can promise food without documenting delivery

The saved Ramakrishna Math, Bhubaneswar calendar marks Guru Purnima on 29 July 2026 and indicates cooked prasad. That gives an institution-specific announced date and food category. It does not name dishes or prove that distribution happened. It also does not settle conflicting household-vow month labels.[^osha-guru-rkm2026]
