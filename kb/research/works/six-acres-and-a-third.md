# Chha Mana Atha Guntha / Six Acres and a Third

The novel introduces Ramachandra Mangaraj as both landlord and moneylender, wrapped in a reputation for religious observance. The narrator appears to defend him, then supplies details that make the defence increasingly doubtful. This is how the satire works: readers must hear the gap between a respectable description and what the scene reveals. In the opening of the Bhagia-and-Saria chapter, the narrator mocks educated men who know foreign genealogies but neglect their own neighbours and institutions. Local knowledge becomes a challenge to fashionable claims of learning. These readings come from particular passages, not a completed reading of the entire novel.

## Original and translation chronology · 1 October 2026

Peter Samuels’s scholarly teaching essay in Education About Asia identifies the Odia original as Chha Mana Atha Guntha (1902). This is an attributed chronology; the original imprint has not been examined. December 2005 remains the saved UC Press English paperback date.

The essay prints translator Jatindra K. Nayak where the publisher’s captured display says Jatindra N. Nayak. Preserve the source-specific credits until title-page or authority-record comparison resolves the initial. The essay’s 1919 death year also differs from the publisher’s 1918 and is not adopted as a correction.
