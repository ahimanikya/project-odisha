# Chha Mana Atha Guntha / Six Acres and a Third

A cow can be a daughter to one household and a source of milk to a man discussing its yield. In Fakir Mohan Senapati’s novel, such differences in how people value things help explain why a small holding matters.

## A household before a holding

Chha Mana Atha Guntha, known in English as Six Acres and a Third, is a novel by Fakir Mohan Senapati. In the chapter introducing Bhagia and Saria, the couple work together: she helps with his weaving, and he tends the fire and brings water while she cooks. Their cow, Neta, is treated as a daughter. The narrator counts her as a third member of the household.

[Chha Mana Atha Guntha: chapter10 Bhagia and Saria](https://or.wikisource.org/wiki/ଛମାଣ_ଆଠଗୁଣ୍ଠ/ଭଗିଆ_ଆଉ_ସାରିଆ)

The same chapter gives the land a history. Bhagia’s father bought the fertile, rent-free holding; Bhagia receives a share of its grain while someone else cultivates it. He is a weaver with an inherited source of support. The narrator sets this household security beside Mangaraj’s interested reaction to the cow’s milk yield. What Saria loves as family can also be appraised as an asset.

[Chha Mana Atha Guntha: chapter10 Bhagia and Saria](https://or.wikisource.org/wiki/ଛମାଣ_ଆଠଗୁଣ୍ଠ/ଭଗିଆ_ଆଉ_ସାରିଆ)

## How a defence becomes an accusation

The opening chapter introduces Ramachandra Mangaraj as a landlord and moneylender with a reputation for religious observance. The narrator seems eager to protect that reputation. Yet the account of his fasting includes food disappearing at night, followed by an elaborate defence. The details make the praise difficult to accept at face value.

[Chha Mana Atha Guntha: chapter1 Ramachandra Mangaraj](https://or.wikisource.org/wiki/ଛମାଣ_ଆଠଗୁଣ୍ଠ/ରାମଚନ୍ଦ୍ର_ମଙ୍ଗରାଜ)

This is a useful way into the novel’s humour: listen to what the narrator claims, then look at the evidence he has just supplied. His apparent loyalty to a respectable man can give the reader reasons to distrust that man. The joke asks us to do some of the judging.

## The reader is also under examination

In Bhagia and Saria’s chapter, the narrator anticipates a reader’s contempt for the weavers’ hereditary leadership. He then turns to inherited religious authority among the reader’s own people. The criticism changes direction. Customs dismissed as foolish in one community look less easy to dismiss when their logic appears closer to home.

[Chha Mana Atha Guntha: chapter10 Bhagia and Saria](https://or.wikisource.org/wiki/ଛମାଣ_ଆଠଗୁଣ୍ଠ/ଭଗିଆ_ଆଉ_ସାରିଆ)

His interruptions matter. He addresses the reader, imagines objections and moves between an account of village life and an argument about the person reading it. Following the plot therefore also means asking what assumptions the narrator has caught us making.

## Who gets heard in the courtroom?

The opening of the Cuttack Sessions Court chapter places Mangaraj before a crowded court. In the narrator’s satirical account, the judge ordinarily leaves much of the work to a subordinate while reading or writing letters. Today an English witness requires his own attention, and the judgment must be written in English.

[Chha Mana Atha Guntha: Cuttack Sessions Court chapter](https://or.wikisource.org/wiki/ଛମାଣ_ଆଠଗୁଣ୍ଠ/କଟକ_ସେସନ୍_ଜଜକୋର୍ଟ)

Then the narrator pauses: he and his readers are Odia, and the printing letters are local, so he must translate the proceedings. The aside makes language part of the scene’s unequal authority. It also reminds us that the court reaches us through a storyteller who chooses how to present it. This is a fictional scene, not a record of an actual trial.

[Chha Mana Atha Guntha: Cuttack Sessions Court chapter](https://or.wikisource.org/wiki/ଛମାଣ_ଆଠଗୁଣ୍ଠ/କଟକ_ସେସନ୍_ଜଜକୋର୍ଟ)

## An original novel and an English edition

The University of California Press paperback dates from December 2005, ISBN 9780520228832. Its listing credits Rabi Shankar Mishra, Satya Mohanty, Jatindra N. Nayak and Paul St.-Pierre as translators. Those are the publisher’s display names; the date identifies this English edition.

[University of California Press · Six Acres and a Third](https://www.ucpress.edu/books/six-acres-and-a-third/paper)

The original Odia publication chronology remains open in the project’s evidence. Peter Samuels’s teaching essay gives 1902; Ramakanta Rout’s historical account gives 1905. Original serial and book imprints have not been examined, so these dates are kept distinct from the translation’s publication date.

[Teaching colonial India with Six Acres and a Third](https://www.educationaboutasia.org/article/id/1629/) [Vyasakavi Fakirmohan Senapati: Leader of Odia Nationalist and Linguistic Movement](https://magazines.odisha.gov.in/Orissareview/2010/April/engpdf/14-17.pdf)

## Carry these details into a complete reading

Begin with the narrator’s defence of Mangaraj, then keep Bhagia and Saria’s shared work, cow and grain income in mind as the story proceeds. Notice whose account of an event becomes persuasive, and what language or social position helps it do so. These selected passages offer a way into the novel; the later turns of the plot need their own reading.

The readings here use selected Odia community transcriptions, not a text checked against a printed critical edition. Keep the language, translator and publisher of your own copy beside your notes: an observation about one passage or edition need not explain every version of the work.
