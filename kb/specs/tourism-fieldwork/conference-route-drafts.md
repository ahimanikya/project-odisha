# Three conference visitor routes for local confirmation

Give a conference visitor one memorable encounter with Odisha and a dependable return to their next commitment. Begin with one anchor experience; add a second stop only when the time, access and host arrangements support it. A shorter visit can leave more room for attention and conversation.

These are **three desk candidates**, prepared on 10 October 2026 for the Founder and a future appointed coordinator. They are not timed itineraries, offers or bookings. All locations reuse the [existing experience shortlist](../../research/collections/tourism-experience-shortlist.md); that evidence does not confirm current availability. The Founder remains the decision owner. No coordinator, host, participant or transport provider is appointed here.

Created for Ahimanikya Satapathy’s Project Odisha direction, with AI-assisted preparation. This supplies preparation for UTP-WORK-171; its requirement for locally supported itineraries remains open.

## Heritage through an object and a landscape

**Candidate H — Bhubaneswar. Anchor: Kala Bhoomi, UTP-EXP-B03. Optional extension: Dhauli, UTP-EXP-B01.**

Let the visitor begin by looking closely at one object rather than trying to absorb every gallery. The proposed question is simple: what do its material, technique and maker help us notice about Odisha? If a guide or demonstration is separately agreed, pay for that time on the agreed terms. A museum ticket alone does not establish a hosted encounter.

Dhauli could then introduce a different kind of reading: distinguish the ancient edict site from the modern peace pagoda. This is an optional second encounter, not a presumed short transfer or a guaranteed route through both parts of the site.

Before retaining either stop, complete the [Kala Bhoomi brief](kala-bhoomi.md) and, if selected, the [Dhauli brief](dhauli.md). Record current admission arrangements, opening exceptions, interpretation, the exact approach, seating and toilets. Check outdoor exposure and the return transfer for the intended date. Do not use “accessible” as a substitute for a description of the actual route.

**Reduce or defer:** retain the museum alone if a second stop crowds the return deadline. A public visit within confirmed museum rules may replace a hosted introduction, but describe that change honestly. If the remaining activity cannot meet the guest’s needs, defer it rather than silently substituting a different promise.

**A worthwhile result:** the visitor can describe one object and its credited context; with Dhauli included, they can distinguish two historical layers. These are proposed learning outcomes, not measured visitor results.

## Craft through a conversation with a maker

**Candidate C — Puri district. Anchor: Raghurajpur, UTP-EXP-P01. Optional extension: Pipili, UTP-EXP-P02.**

Build the encounter around a willing artist’s explanation of a picture: what should a newcomer look at first, and what is easy to miss? Agree whether the activity is conversation, demonstration or hands-on work. These involve different time, materials and permissions. A purchase is optional and separate from the fee for a person’s time.

A separately confirmed Pipili visit could follow how cut cloth becomes an object. Keep the making location distinct from a retail stop. The two experiences offer a comparison of materials and decisions; their relationship does not prove that they fit into one conference break.

Use the [Raghurajpur](raghurajpur.md) and [Pipili](pipili.md) briefs. Confirm the particular host, room, group size, activity, fee, toilets, steps, meeting point and language support. Permission from one artist does not cover the village, another workshop or a performance. Do not include a Gotipua performance unless separately arranged and appropriately authorized.

**Reduce or defer:** select only one willing host if there is insufficient time or capacity. A shop visit is not an automatic replacement for a maker encounter. If starting from a Bhubaneswar conference venue, establish both outward and return legs before considering this candidate; no half-day duration is asserted here.

**A worthwhile result:** the visitor can name the maker, with permission, and describe one creative decision. Photographs or a purchase are not required evidence of a successful encounter.

## Food through a meal and an everyday story

**Candidate F — Bhubaneswar. Anchor enquiry: Nimantran meal, UTP-EXP-B07. Optional extension: Old Town korakhai conversation, UTP-EXP-B05.**

A meal can open a conversation about names, ingredients and household variations. Start with a seated meal enquiry and a menu the provider can actually offer. Choose a small number of dishes to discuss with a willing person; do not promise a fixed “authentic Odisha” menu or a cooking demonstration from an institutional listing.

An optional korakhai conversation would need its own willing present-day maker. Agree what can be explained or observed without disrupting production. Keep a making demonstration, a tasting and a retail purchase as separate choices.

The [Nimantran research record](../../research/facilities/nimantran-bhubaneswar.md) and [korakhai record](../../research/food/korakhai.md) establish research leads, not a selected operating host. Before including food, confirm the precise premises, serving arrangements, ingredients, dietary handling and limitations, seating, water, price, cancellation and timing through the [confirmation checklist](host-confirmation.md). Record participant needs privately and resolve suitability with the participant and provider; do not publish health or dietary information in Git.

**Reduce or defer:** participation can be conversation without tasting, if the host and participant agree. Do not pressure a guest to taste, improvise an ingredient assurance or transfer the same menu promise to a substitute provider. If a suitable meal cannot be confirmed, separate the food story from eating arrangements and defer the hosted meal.

**A worthwhile result:** the visitor remembers a dish name, the person’s own explanation and an acknowledged variation. This is cultural learning, not a nutrition or food-safety certification.

## Check the return deadline before adding a stop

Complete one sheet for the specific conference venue, group, date and candidate. Keep personal contacts and participant needs in an agreed restricted home; public Git should hold only non-sensitive summaries and evidence references. No restricted storage is created by this document.

| Input | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Earliest departure | When the group can actually leave the venue, after its session and assembly needs. |
| Required return | Arrival location and time needed before the next commitment, including venue re-entry. |
| Each transfer | Start, destination, drop-off and pickup, date/time basis, provider or observed evidence, and a realistic allowance. A map estimate alone is not a transport arrangement. |
| Each activity | Host-agreed duration, entry or queue allowance, group capacity and appointment window. |
| Breaks and meal | Rest, toilets, water and meal time appropriate to this group. A food route still needs these planned explicitly. |
| Return contingency | A separately visible allowance and the conditions that require an early return. Do not hide it inside an optimistic transfer estimate. |
| Decision owner | Appointed person who can shorten or stop the actual visit; their scope and emergency arrangements must be agreed before travel. |

Calculate **available time = required return − earliest departure**. Compare it with **assembly + all transfers + admission/queue allowances + activities + breaks/meal + return contingency**. Use a dated basis for every input. An unknown is not zero. If the inputs cross midnight, include the dates rather than subtracting clock times alone.

If any necessary input is unknown, the result is **not assessable**. If required time exceeds available time, **revise** by removing an optional stop or changing the window. If it fits arithmetically, **send for human review** of appointments, access, funding and suitability; a sum does not establish a workable visit.

### A synthetic example of an overloaded plan

The following numbers demonstrate the method only. They describe no Odisha venue, road or host.

A fictional group can leave at 09:00 and must return by 13:00: 240 minutes. Assume 15 minutes assembly, 35 outward travel, 80 at the anchor, 45 for a meal and rest, 35 return travel and 30 contingency. The total is 240 minutes, leaving no additional unallocated time. Admission and queue time are assumed included in the fictional 80-minute anchor allowance; in a real sheet, establish that explicitly.

Adding a 35-minute second activity gives **275 minutes**, already **35 minutes over** the window, even before any extra transfer. Remove the extension or secure a different window. Do not erase the contingency to make the plan appear to fit. Neither version is a confirmed route.

## From these drafts to an actual itinerary

The coordinator’s handoff should contain one selected candidate, its exact start/return locations, one completed time sheet, confirmation records for every retained activity and transfer, a separately scoped quote, cancellation and weather terms, and an agreed reduce-or-return decision. Record how hosts are compensated and which purchases remain optional. Use the [cost tracker](cost-tracker.md); missing prices are unknown, not free.

Apply the [operator assessment and trial decision pack](operator-readiness.md) to the selected activity and group. The Founder decides whether to authorize a bounded trial and spending. An agreed appointment, a completed calculation and an approved trial are distinct states. Keep declined or deferred elements visible.

Only after the applicable local evidence exists should WORK171’s actual itinerary be prepared for review. Public visitor copy then needs its own supported scope and publication decision. Do not turn these candidates into a tour-book preset, online sale or public itinerary merely because the draft has merged into the repository.
