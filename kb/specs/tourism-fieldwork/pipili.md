# Pipili appliqué visit brief

**POD-EXP-P02 · Puri district · Desk lead.** Proposed research visit; the host, date, duration, capacity and price remain unconfirmed.

## Story to explore

**From a cut shape to a finished object.** Ask a maker to show how one shape or colour choice changes an object. Distinguish making from retail and identify whose work is being shown. This is a question-led editorial direction, not a promised visitor product.

## Who to approach

A willing maker or workshop owner, with any represented makers included. A named recipient and the exact message require Founder approval before contact. Verify their authority for the specific venue or activity; an introduction alone is not permission.

## Proposed visit

A small workshop conversation or demonstration if the working space permits. Start with a two-person research team, subject to the host’s agreement. Treat a 60–90 minute appointment as an internal scheduling allowance only; the host sets an appropriate duration. Agree the date and meeting point before routing the day.

## Questions for the host

Where is the item made and by whom? Can the person explaining it consent for the people and designs involved? Are demonstrations offered, and what can visitors try safely?

## Practical checks

Making-versus-retail location, fee and materials, group size, tool/production restrictions, language, seating, toilets and separate reproduction rights. Apply the [shared confirmation checklist](host-confirmation.md), recording exact conditions and the respondent’s role and date. Reconfirm before travel.

## What to bring back

A maker-attributed explanation, actual route and entry notes, quote and consent scope. Save a short account of local benefit, one practical barrier and what the host wants corrected or omitted. Obtain permission separately for quotes, recording, photographs and publication; payment is not a rights transfer.

## If the visit cannot proceed

Use a retail conversation only with clear description; do not call it a making demonstration or claim a workshop relationship.
