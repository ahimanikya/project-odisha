A Morning with Odia Bibhaba

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

15 November 2026

I attended the unveiling of Odia Bibhaba’s redesigned website at Lohia Academy in Bhubaneswar on 15 November. It was a good experience, and a welcome opportunity to spend a morning with people gathered around the preservation of Odia writing.

I had already written about Odia Bibhaba and the work of keeping literature available. This visit gave that article a personal connection: the archive I had been writing about was now the reason for being together in a room.

The banner called the occasion Odia Bibhaba’s Nabakalebara—a renewal, or new form. That is a fitting description. This was a new presentation of an existing collection, built through years of work by Srujanika. The handout made the distinction plainly: the information and material remained, while the website had been rebuilt with readers in mind.1

The changes described were practical ones: easier use on a mobile phone, reading a book or magazine online without downloading it each time, and returning to the page where reading stopped.2 Such details matter when an archive becomes part of someone’s everyday reading. Finding a title is one step; being able to settle down with its pages is another.

The handout also brought attention back to the work that continues beyond a launch. Rare books and periodicals remain scattered across private collections and institutions. Bibliographical records need checking, and biographical entries need additions and corrections. Keeping this material available requires patient, continuing effort.2

For me, the morning was a chance to connect an interest in Odia literature with a gathering devoted to its care. I am glad I attended. My earlier article follows the archive’s history and its place alongside other reading and listening resources; this is a small personal note from being there for its next step.
