# From Bapa to Pratikshya

Research brief for a closer reading of literature and cinema

The connection between Gourahari Das’s *Bapa* and Anupam Patnaik’s *Pratikshya* is established in a published filmmaker interview. What remains to be explored is the passage between them: how a written story becomes dialogue, performance, sound and a shared experience in a cinema. That comparison needs the original story and the relevant film sequences.

## The journey we can trace

Asked about adapting *Bapa*, Patnaik told Ashok Palit that he had connected with the story when he read it. He explained *Pratikshya*, or waiting, through his film’s concerns with employment, marriage and a settled life. This establishes a creative choice about the film’s title; it does not establish what changed from the story.[^title]

Writing for the screen was collaborative. In a separate interview, Patnaik described seventeen drafts and three years of work, naming Roshan Bisoi as the screenwriter. The existing [Anupam Patnaik profile](https://projectodisha.org/people/anupam-patnaik/) follows that account.[^drafts] The IFFI account also connects the project’s development with Patnaik’s own bereavement and his family’s encouragement to continue.[^iffi]

These accounts give the case study three distinct strands: the literary source, the work of adaptation and the filmmaker’s personal circumstances. None should stand in for the others.

## A specific route to the text

A retailer’s indexed contents for Gourahari Das’s *The Little Monk and Other Stories* lists **“Father” starting on page 24**, with the next story starting on page 36 and a note on translators on page 198. The record identifies Rupa and **ISBN 9788129116994**.[^collection]

This is a promising lead, not a confirmed translation of *Bapa*. We have not read those story pages. The useful next material is the volume’s imprint, acknowledgements, original-title or translation credits, the complete “Father” text and the translator notes. If no explicit title mapping is supplied, the Odia text or an attributable author or translator account will still be needed to establish identity.

## What the comparison should notice

Once both works are available, follow one complete episode. Whose perspective guides it? What can the prose tell us directly, and what does the film convey through an actor, a pause, a room or a sound? What choices belong to the father, the son and other family members? Which details locate the experience in Odisha?

Keep the original title, edition, translator and page references beside the film timestamps in the research record. A short, concrete comparison can then strengthen the existing [Pratikshya chapter](https://projectodisha.org/cinema/pratikshya/) and its connection with [The page behind the screen](https://projectodisha.org/cinema/literature-and-screen/). There is no need to repeat the biographies or the film’s synopsis.

## Questions for Gourahari Das

Which collection or magazine contains *Bapa*, and which edition or issue should we read? Is “Father” in *The Little Monk and Other Stories* the same story, and who translated it? Is there a published reading link or a copy of the story and its publication details that you could point us towards?
