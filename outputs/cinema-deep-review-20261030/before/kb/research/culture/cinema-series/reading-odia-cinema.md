# Reading Odia Cinema: the books behind the screen

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

A song can remain with a family long after the film around it has become difficult to find. A photograph may outlive the studio where it was taken. Somewhere, a reader opens a book and encounters the name of the person who wrote the song.

The writing around Odia cinema belongs to this continuing exchange. It includes histories and criticism, but also collected lyrics, working lives and memories kept by families. Read together, these works can bring more people into the picture.

This is a growing reading guide, not a claim to have found every book or reviewed each one. We have inspected selected public-preview pages of the two studies by Ranjan Kumar Rath and Sanjoy Patnaik. The other entries draw on publication records, author bibliographies, library catalogues and documented launches. Unsettled edition details remain open.

## Where a longer account begins

**Kartik Kumar Ghosh — *Oriya Chalachitrara Itihas*.** A Fakir Mohan University accession list records a first edition dated **1984**. The bibliography of the *Encyclopedia of Indian Cinema* also acknowledges Ghosh’s work. This is an important earlier point of reference when tracing who has documented the field; later bibliographies give another date, so editions still need to be compared.[^ghosh]

**Bhim Singh — *Odia Chalachitrara Agyanta Adhyay*.** The filmmaker’s book is documented in biographical reporting and later scholarship. Roy’s bibliography gives 2008; the edition history still needs to be established. Its place here also reminds us that practitioners have written about the industry from within.[^bhim]

**Surya Deo — *Odia Cinema Rupa Rupantara*.** A history of changing forms of Odia cinema. Reports place its beginnings in 2010; a bookseller lists a 2016 second edition. His *Kali Paine Kalira Cinema*, published by Pakshighar Prakashanee, brings together accounts of 21 films. It received a **Special Mention for Best Writing on Cinema** in the 68th National Film Awards, announced in 2022 for the 2020 cycle.[^deo][^kali]

**Ranjan Kumar Rath — *Evolution of Odia Cinema: A Historical Study (1936–2010)*.** Published by B K Publications in 2024, this English-language study is listed at 324 pages. In the preview passages we examined, the cinema’s beginnings connect to theatre-trained actors, musicians, makeup workers and stage builders. That is a useful starting point for following skills across media. [Explore the available Google Books preview](https://www.google.co.in/books/edition/EVOLUTION_OF_ODIA_CINEMA_A_Historical_St/pKYDEgAAQBAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&printsec=frontcover).[^rath]

**Sanjoy Patnaik — *Of Reels, Romance and Retakes: Social Narratives of Cinema in Odisha*.** Released in 2023, with Routledge’s catalogue carrying a 2024 copyright date, this study places cinema in a social and cultural frame. Its contents and publisher description point towards literature, audiences, class, gender and the industry’s future. We have read selected preview passages, not the full arguments of those chapters. [Explore the Google Books preview](https://www.google.co.in/books/edition/Of_Reels_Romance_and_Retakes/NnrOEAAAQBAJ?hl=en&gbpv=1&printsec=frontcover).[^patnaik]

These books invite different kinds of reading. A chronology helps establish what followed what. A social study asks who participated, whose lives were represented and how audiences encountered them. Neither task is completed by a list of awards.

## Songs, literature and the people around a film

**Surya Deo — *Jhura Mana Jhara Giti*.** A study of Odia film music, documented in his published profiles. Alongside it are his two film-song compilations: *Odia Cinema Gita 1936–1969* and a second volume described in a festival biography as covering **1970–1978**. The first appears in a government book-selection list and a scholarly bibliography; the second volume’s exact title-page date range still needs inspection.[^deo][^lyrics]

**Devdas Chhotray — *Cinema Geeta*.** His author biography lists this collection of film lyrics. A lyricist’s words on the page offer a different object of attention from the finished recording; publication and edition details remain to be completed.[^devdas]

**Dillip Kumar Pani — *Odia Chalachitra Ra Sahitika O Sanskrutika Mulyayana*.** Its launch was reported alongside a 2019 Film Journalists’ Forum screening of *ରକ୍ତ ଗୋଲାପ (Rakta Golapa)*. The title concerns cinema’s literary and cultural evaluation. We still need the title page to settle its exact transliteration and publication details.[^pani]

**Iti Samanta, editor/compiler — *Odia Cinema: Expansion & Analysis (1936–2019)*.** Her author website lists this work with material spanning creative and technical roles. The English wording is how the website presents the title; it does not establish an English-language edition.[^iti]

**Sanjoy Patnaik, editor — *Odia Cinema@90: Rhythms, Renditions and Reflections*.** The Bhubaneswar Film Circle’s 2024 collection brings multiple contributors into the discussion. A contemporary review identifies writing on literature, folk music, film songs, production facilities and representation. The festival’s subsequent report describes the book as bilingual.[^ninety]

That range matters to the questions running through our series. Pragati Mohapatra’s chapter on literary classics and cinema is also recorded in her institution’s publication list. The collection’s review names Mahasweta Baxipatra on film songs and Debasis Mohapatra on folk music. These are promising routes for further reading, rather than arguments we can claim to have examined in full.[^ninety]

**Sandip Kumar Nayak — *Ollywood and Its Superstars: Its All About Odia Cinema*.** Catalogues list this short English-language book from Notion Press in 2021. We have not assessed its contents. Including it keeps the reading field broader than academic publishing, and cautions against calling a later book the first in English without a much fuller survey.[^nayak]

A lyric collection may help us find a writer’s contribution. An interview collection may reveal an editor, a technician or a producer whose decisions shaped a film. For the question that brought us here—how creative work reaches people and supports those who make it—both deserve attention.

## Lives that sit beside the histories

A memoir remembers from one position. A family account preserves access to another. Those qualities make such books valuable, while leaving room to compare recollections with other records.

- **Sarat Chandra Pujari — *Nijaswa Chitranatya*.** Autobiographical writing by the actor; a government selection list records a 2015 first edition from Sikshya Sandhan.[^pujari]
- **Ratri Ghose Barman — *Gour & Parbati*.** Launched in 2021, this family-authored account concerns Gour Prasad Ghose and Parbati Ghose. The family archive provides a route into their photographs and continuing remembrance.[^gour]
- **Akshaya Mohanty — *Aryyadasara Atmalipi* and *Aryyadasara Sheshalipi*.** The catalogue records these as separate works, dated 2002 and 2003. They belong to his autobiographical literary writing; individual episodes should not be treated as independently established biography merely because they appear in that form.[^akshaya]
- **Iti Samanta, compiler — *Amara Akshaya*.** Her bibliography lists this work on Akshaya Mohanty and his literary creations. Its wider literary scope makes it a companion to cinema reading rather than a general film history.[^iti]

## Read beyond the cinema shelf

Three broader works offer useful connections without being books exclusively about Odia cinema:

- **Piyush Roy’s *Appreciating Melodrama: Theory and Practice in Indian Cinema and Television*** includes a chapter on rasa, the *Natyashastra* and the birth of Odia cinema. The publisher’s contents identify the chapter; our earlier research used selected passages of Roy’s related doctoral thesis.[^roy]
- **Ashish Rajadhyaksha and Paul Willemen’s *Encyclopedia of Indian Cinema*** offers a wider reference setting. Its bibliographic trail also makes earlier documentation visible.[^ghosh]
- **Sharmila Chhotaray’s *Cultural Politics of Popular Performances: The Structure and Representation of the Odia Jatra*** concerns theatre rather than cinema. It belongs beside our exploration of storytelling, audiences and performance economies.[^jatra]

## A shelf that can keep growing

The present catalogue contains **14 cinema-focused volumes and five related volumes of life writing or tribute**, with the three broader works above kept separate. Volumes, editions and formats are not interchangeable: the two song compilations are two volumes, while a paperback and an ebook of the same text do not become two different works.

This is enough to begin a more generous conversation about the people who have already done the work. It also leaves concrete tasks: finding older editions, recording title pages accurately, reading the books in full, and bringing overlooked writing into view.

For Project Odisha, the next step is to let that reading sharpen our questions. What can an earlier production teach a new team? How did a song acquire a life beyond its film? Which experiences have still not reached a page?

A bibliography becomes useful when somebody follows it—and returns with something we had not yet seen.
