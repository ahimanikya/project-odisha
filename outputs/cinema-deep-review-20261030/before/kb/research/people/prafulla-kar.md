# Prafulla Kar: A Composer in the Company of Voices

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Pranab Patnaik remembered a birthday request. At a celebration for Prafulla Kar at Rabindra Mandap, Kar asked him to sing his compositions “Majhire” and “Re Atman.” Recalling their long friendship after Kar’s death, Patnaik kept that occasion among his memories.[^remembrance]

A composer asking a friend to sing gives this musical life an intimate point of entry. The songs had a place in a working relationship, and the relationship could return through a song. [Pranab’s story](pranab-patnaik.md) belongs beside Kar’s: one voice carrying another musician’s composition, with years of shared work behind it.

## A singer who made music for others

Kar entered film playback with *ଶ୍ରୀ ଶ୍ରୀ ପତିତପାବନ (Shri Shri Patita Pabana)* in 1962 and made his debut as a film music director with *ମମତା (Mamata)* in 1975.[^career] These were different responsibilities within the same musical world: singing a part and shaping music for a production.

His work also reached the microphone through All India Radio and Doordarshan. The Sangeet Natak Akademi’s 2015 citation describes him as a vocalist, composer and lyricist.[^akademi] Individual recordings bring those roles into focus.

## A soundtrack with several companions

In *Mamata*, “Re Mita Aa Sangata” names Pranab Kishore Patnaik and Sikandar Alam among its singers, with Kar as music director and Sibabrata Das as lyricist.[^mamata] “Dekhi Se Banamalli” brings Chitta Jena and P. Susheela together, again with Kar’s music and Das’s words.[^banamalli]

A third song, “Mu Ta Manisha Marini,” gives Sikandar Alam the singer’s credit, Kar the music-direction credit and Sibabrata Das the lyric credit.[^mu-ta] The repeated names hold the soundtrack together while the combinations change. A listener can follow the composer across the songs, or stay with a singer and hear how that voice belongs within the larger work.

The [shared cinema-music story](../culture/cinema-series/cinema-music.md) begins with this company. Kar’s profile adds a reason to linger: a film’s music can carry several distinct partnerships, even when we remember the soundtrack under one composer’s name.

## Hearing the composer sing

Kar’s own voice offers another route. The distributed soundtrack listing for *ଶେଷ ଶ୍ରାବଣ (Sesa Srabana)* credits him as composer and main artist for “Kali Kapali.”[^kali] Here the person shaping the music is also named as its performer.

That movement between roles invites a different kind of listening. Put a recording he sings beside one he composed for someone else. There is no need to decide which represents him best. Each offers a way to meet a musician whose work took more than one form.

His musical range also included compositions for Odissi dance dramas.[^career] Film, broadcast and dance place music in different relationships with words, performers and an audience. The [AIR Cuttack reading](../culture/air-cuttack.md) follows one of those wider settings, where a song could have a life beyond its place in a film.

## Recognition, and the recordings that remain

The Sangeet Natak Akademi honoured Kar for Sugam Sangeet in its awards for 2015, presented on 4 October 2016. He also received the Padma Shri in 2015.[^akademi][^career] Earlier, the Odisha Society of the Americas recorded its own 2012 lifetime achievement award for his contributions as singer, music director, composer and lyricist.[^osa]

Those occasions recognise a body of work. The recordings offer the closer encounter. “Re Mita Aa Sangata,” “Mu Ta Manisha Marini” and “Kali Kapali” each provide a different starting point, with contributors named beside the music. Returning to them can make an honoured career particular again: a song, the people who made it, and another listener finding time for it.
