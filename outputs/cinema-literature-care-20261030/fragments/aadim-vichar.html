At an IFFI press conference in November 2014, Atal Bihari Panda spoke about acting as a search for truth through illusion. The official account introduced him as an octogenarian with nearly seven decades in theatre. Beside the film’s director, Sabyasachi Mohapatra, was a performer carrying a long life on the stage into the conversation about cinema.1

ଆଦିମ ବିଚାର (Aadim Vichar) brings those forms of storytelling together. Its protagonist faces a court, but the film asks viewers to consider a question that extends beyond a verdict: whose understanding of justice is being heard?

An older man before the court

The official festival account describes a righteous tribal man implicated in a case, whose candid defence surprises the judge. Mohapatra presented the film as an argument for the value of traditional justice.1 This is the director’s artistic argument within a fictional story, rather than a description of all Kandha communities.

The character’s voice, and the authority listening to him, invite viewers into the question. How is a person heard when the language of an institution meets the values by which he has lived?

From a written story to a shared performance

The Indian Panorama catalogue credits Kapileswar Prasad Mohapatra with the story and Sabyasachi with screenplay and direction. Its cast includes Atal Bihari Panda, Tapaswini Guru and Sushree Smita Panda.2

Selection and award

The film appeared in Indian Panorama at IFFI 2014. It subsequently received Best Odiya Film for 2014 at the 62nd National Film Awards, announced on 24 March 2015.23

Return from the award to the older performer at the press conference: Panda brought decades of theatre to the role. The court in the story gives that voice an authority to confront; the performance gives us a person to listen to.
