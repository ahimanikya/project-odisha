A. K. Bir trained in cinematography at FTII, then went on to write, photograph and direct films, including ଆଦି ମୀମାଂସା (Adi Mimansa) and ଲାବଣ୍ୟ ପ୍ରୀତି (Lavanya Preeti).1 A story could stay with him through the writing, the camera and the direction of its performers.

Making a film for younger viewers

Bir was a director recipient of the Best Children’s Film of 1993 award for Lavanya Preeti.2

The film companion follows its connection between an inherited love story and the feelings of young people. NFDC’s children’s catalogue also lists it under Loving Hearts.3 Both names lead to the same film.

For his Hindi film Baaja,3 Bir recalled preparing the boy’s character before looking for a young performer. Two students from the National School of Drama helped him run a workshop with the child. During filming, he said, he offered only a few instructions.4 Preparation, in this account, made room for the child to perform.

Work beyond a single title

The 45th National Film Awards catalogue records Bir’s films associated with the National Literacy Mission and his involvement in organising international film festivals for children and young people.1

Looking at the work together

Our series follows directors through different routes: Nirad Mohapatra through film societies and teaching, Sabyasachi Mohapatra through regional stories and performance, and Bir through cinematography and children’s cinema. Their activities meet in a shared interest: making room for viewers to encounter cinema.
