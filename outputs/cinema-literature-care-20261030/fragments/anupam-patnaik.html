Seventeen drafts and three years of work: that is how Anupam Patnaik described making ପ୍ରତୀକ୍ଷା (Pratikshya). In an interview before its release, he recalled working with screenwriter Roshan Bisoi to find the shape of a story about a son, an ailing father and the longing for a secure job.1 The title means waiting. Behind the finished film was another kind of patience—the repeated work of returning to a story until it could be filmed.

Learning the work of production

Patnaik’s education took him through business administration and information technology at Symbiosis, then an MSc in International Marketing Management at Leeds University Business School. His production company’s biography dates his entry into television and film production to 2011, with work on Tarang’s Horror Zone and the shorts Ichha—A Silent Urge and Smruti—Memories.2

Feature production came before the recognition for his direction. The Bengaluru International Film Festival’s 2016 catalogue credits him as producer of ତୁଳସୀ ଅପା (Tulasi Apa), directed by his father, Amiya Patnaik. The film follows the life and educational work of Tulasi Munda.3

A family story carried through loss

Pratikshya drew inspiration from a short story by Gourahari Das. As Patnaik recounted at IFFI in 2022, his father died during the film’s development. He considered stopping; his family encouraged him to finish it. The same festival account records Dipanwit Dashmohapatra describing Sanjay as his first leading role.4

Pratikshya received Best Odia Film at the 69th National Film Awards, announced in August 2023 for films of 2021. The official result names Amiya Patnaik Productions as producer recipient and Anupam Patnaik as director recipient.5

Another story, another form

Patnaik’s later କର୍ମ (Karma, 2024) moved into a murder-investigation thriller, with Anubhav Mohanty playing investigator Amar Patnaik.6 A family drama and a thriller ask different things of a director. Together, they suggest a career still exploring what kinds of stories can find an audience in the language.

Seventeen drafts are easy to compress into a sentence. Patnaik’s recollection asks us to leave room for the time behind them: writing with Bisoi, losing his father, and accepting his family’s encouragement to continue.
