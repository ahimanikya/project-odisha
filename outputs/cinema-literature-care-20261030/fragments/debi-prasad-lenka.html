Before ଦମନ (DAMaN) was filmed, its actors spent fifteen days in a workshop. Debi Prasad Lenka recalled detailed scripting and preparation that helped the team revise dialogue and translate passages spoken by tribal characters.1 The script was being tested through voices.

Lenka also edited DAMaN.2 Rehearsal and editing stand at different moments in a production, but both ask what a story needs when it is heard and seen.

Lenka is from Cuttack. His public professional biography describes a three-year filmmaking course followed by work as an assistant director and editor in Odisha and Mumbai.3 A 2022 Sunday Post profile identifies him as an alumnus of the Biju Patnaik Film and Television Institute of Odisha.4 His route into feature films therefore connects local training with the wider working world of television and production.

A career beyond the feature-film poster

His earlier credits include corporate films, documentaries, government productions and a commercial for the 2018 Men’s Hockey World Cup.2

Mentis Films identifies him as a filmmaker, director, editor and founder. Its profile connects him with B for Bundelkhand, ବିଜୁ ବାବୁ (Biju Babu) and DAMaN.5

A partnership formed at work

Vishal Mourya recalls meeting Lenka, then an editor, on a Zoom television project in 2010.6 They later made films together. NFDC’s 2017 Film Bazaar catalogue lists both as writers and directors of B for Bundelkhand, a Hindi film about a farming family under pressure.7

The partnership carried into Odia cinema through Biju Babu and then DAMaN.5

Recognition, and work still to explore

DAMaN received Best Odia Film for 2022 at the 70th National Film Awards, announced in 2024.8

Lenka’s recollection returns to dialogue revised in preparation, before filming began. His work on the film continued in the edit. Between those moments, a story moved from the script through performances into its finished form.
