A story heard in childhood can become a way of understanding feelings that arrive later. In ଲାବଣ୍ୟ ପ୍ରୀତି (Lavanya Preeti), A. K. Bir places adolescent experience beside the story of Kedar and Gouri. NFDC’s catalogue describes a story within a story, in which elders misunderstand the young lovers and tragedy follows.1

The film, also catalogued as Loving Hearts, is a 1993 Odia live-action feature.1 Young performers bring its friendships and misunderstandings to the screen.

An inherited story and a present feeling

The National Film Awards catalogue connects the Kedar–Gouri story with jatra and describes the contemporary classmates Gopal, Vidyulata and Mayadhar living under its imaginative influence.2 An older story can become part of how a young person understands a new friendship or separation.

The inherited tale and the classmates’ experience give the film two connected levels of storytelling. What do young characters recognise in it, and where might their own lives depart from its ending?

The people making the film

The catalogue credits Bir with script, direction and cinematography; Dilip Panda with editing; Manoj Sikka with sound; and Bhavdeep Jaipurwalle with music. It lists Tara Shankar Misra as a child artist.2

Recognition for children’s cinema

Lavanya Preeti received Best Children’s Film of 1993 at the 41st National Film Awards.2

The classmates encounter Kedar and Gouri through an inherited tale; young viewers encounter the classmates through a film. Between those two acts of watching, a story acquires another life. What will the next listener recognise in it?
