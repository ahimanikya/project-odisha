In କ୍ଳାନ୍ତ ଅପରାହ୍ନ (Klanta Aparanha), retired Adikanda lives with his mother and daughter Niru. Niru teaches alongside Sandhya and Veena, with whom she exchanges news and hopes. An anticipated marriage proposal draws the family into financial pressure: Adikanda mortgages land to raise money for the dowry.1

The premise brings a social expectation into one household. Marriage becomes a question of property and security as well as affection. In his account of Odisha’s new-wave cinema, critic Ashok Palit discusses Mahapatra’s films through changing rural society and tensions within families.2

Working with the filmmaker

Colleagues’ recollections add another view of his practice. In Bijay Mandal’s 2020 retrospective, actor Jayaswami described his help in developing her work while keeping actors within their characters. Susant Mani recalled subtitling and mentorship; Pradeep Mohanty remembered borrowing Mahapatra’s 35mm camera rather than hiring one from Madras.3

The journey from training to recognition

The 1985 Indian Panorama catalogue identifies Mahapatra as a graduate of Utkal University who completed film-direction studies at FTII in 1975. It dates the making of ଶୀତରାତି (Seeta Raati) to 1976 and its national recognition to 1982.1

The 29th National Film Awards catalogue lists Seeta Raati as Best Oriya Film in the awards for 1981.4

The people making Klanta Aparanha

For Klanta Aparanha, the Panorama catalogue names Nandalal Mahapatra for the story, Ranajit Ray for cinematography, Satyendra Mohanty for editing and Shantanu Mahapatra for music.1

A practice carried by people

Jayaswami remembered help with her acting; Pradeep Mohanty remembered a camera he could borrow. Alongside the finished films, such recollections preserve the help one filmmaker could give another person’s work. Maya Miriga takes us into another household story and the people who made it.
