Nirad Mohapatra left postgraduate political-science study for film training at FTII. By 1972 he was teaching there; from 1974 to 1983 he ran Cinexstasy, a Bhubaneswar film society pairing world-cinema screenings with analysis.1

A film could be something to make, but also something to gather around and discuss. Those encounters belong to Mohapatra’s working life alongside his own production.

A place to encounter cinema

The same account connects his film-appreciation courses at Utkal University with his work on the film section of the literary journal Mana Phasal.1 Cinema entered a literary journal as well as a classroom. Screening and discussion gave it a shared place in the city.

A household at the centre

The extended family at the centre of ମାୟା ମିରିଗ (Maya Miriga) provides the subject of our companion film article, which follows its collaborators and later restoration. The filmmaker who made that household drama also made time for other people’s films.

Recognition, and the work after recognition

The film received Second Best Feature Film at the 31st National Film Awards, covering films of 1983 and presented in 1984. Cannes Critics’ Week records its 1984 selection. The award and the festival selection mark distinct forms of recognition.23

What can be carried forward?

The film society, the classroom and Mana Phasal leave us with a different picture of a filmmaker’s work. The screen was also a place around which he brought other people together.

Cinexstasy lasted nearly a decade. Making those screenings and discussions possible was itself sustained work, worth remembering beside the film that travelled abroad.
