Pranab Patnaik remembered a birthday request. At a celebration for Prafulla Kar at Rabindra Mandap, Kar asked him to sing his compositions “Majhire” and “Re Atman.” Recalling their long friendship after Kar’s death, Patnaik kept that occasion among his memories.1

In Pranab’s story, a song could recall years of working with its composer. Here it was also a request from a friend.

A singer who made music for others

Kar entered film playback with ଶ୍ରୀ ଶ୍ରୀ ପତିତପାବନ (Shri Shri Patita Pabana) in 1962 and made his debut as a film music director with ମମତା (Mamata) in 1975.2 These were different responsibilities within the same musical world: singing a part and shaping music for a production.

His work also reached the microphone through All India Radio and Doordarshan. The Sangeet Natak Akademi’s 2015 citation describes him as a vocalist, composer and lyricist.3 Individual recordings bring those roles into focus.

A soundtrack with several companions

In Mamata, “Re Mita Aa Sangata” names Pranab Kishore Patnaik and Sikandar Alam among its singers, with Kar as music director and Sibabrata Das as lyricist.4 “Dekhi Se Banamalli” brings Chitta Jena and P. Susheela together, again with Kar’s music and Das’s words.5

A third song, “Mu Ta Manisha Marini,” gives Sikandar Alam the singer’s credit, Kar the music-direction credit and Sibabrata Das the lyric credit.6 Kar and Das worked across these changing combinations of voices.

Hearing the composer sing

The distributed soundtrack listing for ଶେଷ ଶ୍ରାବଣ (Sesa Srabana) credits him as composer and main artist for “Kali Kapali.”7

His musical range also included compositions for Odissi dance dramas.2 Film, broadcast and dance place music in different relationships with words, performers and an audience. The AIR Cuttack reading follows one of those wider settings, where a song could have a life beyond its place in a film.

Recognition, and the recordings that remain

The Sangeet Natak Akademi honoured Kar for Sugam Sangeet in its awards for 2015, presented on 4 October 2016. He also received the Padma Shri in 2015.32 Earlier, the Odisha Society of the Americas recorded its own 2012 lifetime achievement award for his contributions as singer, music director, composer and lyricist.8

The honours describe a career; the recordings let us spend time with it. Return to one song, and to the particular company of voices Kar brought together.
