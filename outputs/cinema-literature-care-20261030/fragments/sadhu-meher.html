Kishtaya comes back to the village with money he has earned. Seeking work from Surya, the landlord’s son, he is beaten; his wife Lakshmi intervenes. The National Film Awards catalogue describes this painful return in ଅଙ୍କୁର (Ankur), the Hindi film for which Sadhu Meher received Best Actor for 1974.1

The same catalogue records Meher’s Odisha background and training at the Film and Television Institute of India.1 His career would carry him from this performance into choosing and directing actors in Odia films.

Kishtaya: the man who comes back

Meher plays Kishtaya, a poor potter and husband of Lakshmi, played by Shabana Azmi. Anant Nag plays Surya.2 Kishtaya had left following public humiliation. On returning, he accepts his wife’s approaching motherhood, before the encounter with Surya turns violent.1

The conflict puts employment, dependence and the landlord’s power inside a marriage. Kishtaya’s return is therefore more than a change in the household: it brings a vulnerable worker back within reach of the person who can decide whether he earns.

The actor was also a director

Kapilas Bhuyan’s 2010 retrospective credits Meher’s ଅଭିମାନ (Abhiman) (1977) with helping revive cinema attendance.3

Uttam Mohanty recalled meeting Meher, who photographed him and cast him in Abhiman. In the 2018 interview, Mohanty described obtaining his family’s permission and completing the shoot in 35 days.4

The official 60th National Film Awards catalogue later listed him as chair of its South I feature-film jury panel. Its biography recorded six directed feature films by that point, alongside work in acting and television.5

In Ankur, Meher was the actor returning as Kishtaya. In Abhiman, he was the director giving Uttam Mohanty a role. Both belong to the career.
