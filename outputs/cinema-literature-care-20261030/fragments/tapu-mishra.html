In 2018, Tapu Mishra was thinking about which work to accept. She said she had become selective about stage appearances and film playback, and wanted to create new songs through her own production house.1

From Sambalpur to film songs

Mishra grew up in Sambalpur and began singing young. She attended Sambalpur University and obtained a music degree from Gandharva Mahavidyalaya. Her film career began with Himanshu Parija’s କୁଳନନ୍ଦନ (Kulanandana) in 1995; “Na Re Na Bajana Bansi” from ଆଇ ଲଭ୍ ୟୁ (I Love You) brought her wider recognition.2

Her career continued across film songs, bhajans and recordings in languages including Bengali and Hindi.2

A duet, and a song outside cinema

Tapu and Humane Sagar shared the title song of ଇସ୍କ ତୁ ହି ତୁ (Ishq Tu Hi Tu). Abhijit Majumdar composed the music and Nizam wrote the words.3 The recording places her within a newer chapter of the Odia cinema-music story, alongside a fellow singer whose voice also became closely associated with contemporary film songs.

Another project took a different form. Her Hindi single “Bali Umariya,” with music by Sushil, was released by Venus on 14 February 2018. It was a music video outside a feature film, with Poonam Mishra appearing on screen.1

The work she left, and the choices she voiced

Mishra won four Odisha State Film Awards and also released albums through her own YouTube channel. She died on 19 June 2021.2 Alongside that loss, her recordings preserve the work itself, while her interview preserves something more personal: her wish to choose carefully and make new music.

For readers interested in how such ambitions become possible, the cinema-economics reading opens questions about time, resources and sustaining creative work. Tapu’s story gives those questions a person’s scale.
