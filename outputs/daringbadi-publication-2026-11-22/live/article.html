Daringbadi: Coffee, Shade and Hill Views

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Daringbadi is a hill town in Kandhamal, in Odisha’s Eastern Ghats. Its coffee gardens and views across the surrounding hills invite both a broad look at the landscape and closer attention to a particular place.1 Leave time for both.

Coffee beneath the trees

The Balliguda Forest Division’s working plan describes coffee and pepper growing beneath larger shade trees near Kutubadi. It identifies the garden with the Soil Conservation Department.2 This is a cultivated landscape, where the arrangement of plants matters as much as the view. Look for the relationship between the taller trees and the crops below them; a garden need not be in flower or ready for harvest to reward attention.

A visit can be simple. Allow time for looking and photographs, and ask before entering working areas or handling plants. If buying coffee is part of your plan, asking where it was grown and processed can make the purchase part of learning about the crop.

Choose a view, then a walk

The same working plan places Hill View Park opposite Nature Park, describing viewing towers at one and themed gardens at the other. It also identifies the pine area along the Brahmunigaon road as a plantation.2 These places offer different subjects for attention: a wider outlook, a garden, planted trees.

Midubanda, called Midhubandha by Odisha Tourism, is a forest waterfall near Daringbadi.3 Give this outward excursion its own place in your transport plans, with time to return comfortably.

Choose stops to suit the people travelling together. Someone who wants time with a camera may prefer a longer pause at one place; someone travelling with small children may need a shorter outing. Before settling on a walking stop, ask about the approach, changes in level and toilets if those details affect your party.

Choose the season

Odisha Tourism recommends October to February for Daringbadi.1 Check the forecast for your dates, pack layers for the colder parts of the day and leave room to rearrange outdoor stops if the weather changes.

Getting there, and getting back

The Kandhamal district administration names Brahmapur, also written Berhampur, as a rail gateway and Bhubaneswar as the air gateway, with a road approach through Phulbani.4 Choose the connection that fits your journey, then arrange the onward leg to Daringbadi and the return. Confirm transport against your actual arrival time.

Avoid making the last outdoor stop depend on a hurried departure. Agree the return pickup, carry drinking water and bring back packaging. If time is short, give fewer places a proper visit. A hill journey can leave space for conversation, a meal and an unplanned pause without needing another destination added to the day.
