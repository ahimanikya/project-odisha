# Dhumpa Music and Its Players

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Dhumpa names an instrument and the music performed with it. An account from Khandapada in Odisha’s Nayagarh district places it among singers, stick players and a dancer.[^1]

## An instrument within a group

The Indira Gandhi National Centre for the Arts’ cultural-mapping report describes a hollow bark cylinder, six to seven feet long. In this Khandapada account, the bark comes from trees named Bhendiamardan or Mayi and is removed during the rainy season. A principal singer performs with a chorus, Kathias or stick drummers, and a Gotipua dancer. The Kathias strike the cylinder to accompany the singing. The report also names harmonium, tabla, flute, vina and mardal among the accompanying instruments.[^1]

The long cylinder belongs within an ensemble. Lead singing, the chorus, stick playing and dance each contribute to the performance; describing the object alone leaves much of the music out.

## A named ensemble on stage

A report dated 17 September 2018 gives that collective practice a public setting. *OrissaPOST* names Gangadhar Dhumpa Sangeet Parishad as the group that opened a programme at Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan in Bhubaneswar, organised in collaboration with Infosys Foundation. It also associates Khandapada’s Dhumpa songs with the rainy season and Radha–Krishna love themes.[^2]

The rainy season and the Radha–Krishna theme give the songs a setting beyond the concert venue. In the reported Bhubaneswar programme, that repertoire also had a public audience and an identified ensemble behind it.

## A player in the programme

Sangeet Natak Akademi’s programme for the 21–25 June 2022 Jyotirgamaya festival in New Delhi lists Alok Bishoyi with “Dhumpa Vadya.”[^3] The listing gives us a named practitioner within a festival devoted to performers and musical instruments. It is a programme credit, rather than a description of what was played on the day.

Keeping these names with the music gives readers something concrete to follow: an ensemble associated with a reported performance, and a musician named in an organiser’s programme.
