Lipika Singh Darai: Listening Beyond the Performance

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

In this article
Finding the people behind the puppetsSound as a way of workingA conversation held in a filmFollow the listeningFootnotes & Sources

A filmmaker’s listening connects puppeteers, collaborators, remembered relationships and the patient work of making a film.

After seeing a curated puppetry programme in 2013, Lipika Singh Darai wondered how much a city audience understood about the practitioners beyond their performance. In her account to journalist Nandini Ramnath, that question became a reason to seek them in their own surroundings.1

Finding the people behind the puppets

Darai recalled asking rickshaw pullers at a station how to find the puppeteer Maguni Charan Kuanr, then meeting him at his home in Keonjhar. She worked with cinematographer Indraneel Lahiri2 and a crew over several years, with other filmmaking between periods of production.1

At the Mumbai International Film Festival in 2022, she spoke of spending a year and a half on research. The official account identifies her as Backstage’s director and editor.3

Ramnath’s report places the puppeteers’ livelihoods and changing forms alongside their art.1 An audience’s enjoyment and a performer’s ability to continue meet within the same story. The Bhukha reading approaches that relationship through a fictional screen world; Backstage brings documentary attention to particular practitioners.

Sound as a way of working

Darai received the non-feature Best Audiography award for Gaarud at the 57th National Film Awards, for films of 2009. Her credited role was re-recordist of the final mixed track.4

A final sound mix is part of how a viewer encounters a film: voices, spaces and other sounds must be heard in relation to one another. Recognising that work widens our attention beyond the visible frame.

She records location sound and designs sound for her own films, and edits for other filmmakers. Her editing credits include The Sound Man Mangesh Desai and In the Shadow of Time.5 These collaborations sit alongside her directing, bringing another person’s material into the work of shaping a film.

A conversation held in a film

For her first film, Eka Gachha, Eka Manisa, Eka Samudra (A Tree, A Man, A Sea), Darai turned towards her late Hindustani music teacher, Prafulla Kumar Das. An old surviving cassette held his voice. In 2013, she recalled retracing his teaching journey from Balasore to Baripada and meeting his family before filming. Mani Kaul encouraged her to begin with her own feelings of the teacher’s presence.6

Her filmography also describes an imagined conversation with her late grandaunt, developed while revisiting image and sound recordings from Mumbai in 2013. Her film letters include କଙ୍କି ଓ ସାପ (Kankee O Saapo), ରାତି ଓ ଭୟ (Raati O Bhaya) and ବି ଓ ଏସ (B O S).5

An imagined conversation leaves room for a present voice and for what remains beyond reply.

Night and Fear appeared in Rotterdam’s Tiger Short Competition in 2023; B and S was programmed in its Short & Mid-length section in 2025.7 These festival encounters offer further routes into the work.

Follow the listening

In the search for Sita Bibaha, she turns to surviving records and remembered voices. That search follows another kind of listening.

Listening for what remains.
