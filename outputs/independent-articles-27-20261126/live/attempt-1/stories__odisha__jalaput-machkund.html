Following Jalaput’s water towards electricity

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Jalaput stores the water; another dam downstream directs it towards generation. That separation is the key to understanding the Machkund hydroelectric scheme, whose works occupy different points in the Eastern Ghats landscape near Duduma Falls.

APGENCO identifies the diversion dam as approximately 17 kilometres below Jalaput. Water is directed into a power canal, while the reservoir provides storage upstream. The scheme has six generating units with a combined installed capacity of 120 megawatts: three of 17 MW and three of 23 MW. Its commissioning record runs from August 1955 to August 1959. 1

The machinery uses both the flow of water and its descent. In a conventional hydropower system, water turns a turbine linked to a generator. The available flow and the difference in elevation—the head—help determine the power that can be produced. Storage, diversion and generation therefore perform distinct tasks within the same system. 2

The river continues under another name. APGENCO describes the Machkund as the Sileru in its lower reaches. Odisha Hydro Power Corporation identifies Balimela as the second stage of development of the Machkund–Sileru river, after Machkund. Jalaput thus belongs to a sequence of river works, rather than an isolated reservoir. 3, 4

Interstate cooperation is another part of that continuity. APGENCO records an agreement of 23 October 2020 for Andhra Pradesh and Odisha to share costs and benefits equally in connection with renovation, modernisation and upgrading. Maintaining an older generating system requires institutional arrangements as well as replacement equipment. 5

For someone looking across Jalaput, the visible expanse of water is consequently only the beginning of the explanation. Installed megawatts describe the generating equipment’s capacity; they do not say how much electricity is being produced at that moment. Production depends on the water available and the operation of the plant.

Following the water supplies a more useful picture than a single dam photograph: an upstream store, a downstream diversion, a controlled descent through machinery and a river continuing beyond it. The scheme’s history stretches across those structures and across the decades since its first unit entered service.
