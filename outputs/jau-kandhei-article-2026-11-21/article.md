# Jau Kandhei and the Doll Makers of Balasore

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Jau Kandhei from Balasore bring two kinds of work into one object: shaping clay and decorating its surface with lac.[^1] Lac is a resin secreted by insects living on trees.[^2]

## Form and surface

The South Asia Collection’s account describes fine clay shaped by hand, dried and fired in a kiln. A lac coating follows, with contrasting threadlike decoration added to the surface.[^1] The modelling and decoration are distinct stages of work.

## Makers and groups

In a report published on 8 September 2022, Ashis Senapati describes Kanakalata Das and other women applying coloured lac to figures in Balasore. Das told him that she had formed the Binapani self-help group with fifteen women in 2005. The report places the group in Koshamba Nagar, on the outskirts of the town.[^2]

Senapati also records Kesu Das’s account of ceremonial doll weddings and offerings during Sabitri Brata.[^2]

A separate organisation appears in Mission Shakti’s 2022 publication *Scaling Up*. It records the Bampada Jau Kandhei Producer Group in Remuna, Balasore, as formed in 2020 with thirty women from six self-help groups. The account describes a move from tailoring into making lac toys and bangles, with support from Mission Shakti and ORMAS and training from the District Industries Centre, Balasore.[^3]


The South Asia Collection records a different destination for finished figures. It acquired four Jau Kandhei figures and a Ganesha in 2017 at the Crafts Council in New Delhi.[^1]

When looking at a doll, its surface can be a starting point for questions: who shaped it, who decorated it, and what occasion was it made for?
