Taptapani: A Spring in the Wooded Hills

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Taptapani’s perennial hot spring lies among hills and forest in Ganjam.1 Pause beside the water and consider the landscape beneath your feet.

A spring and a shrine

Maa Kandhuni Devi is worshipped at Taptapani’s temple.2 Give people space to worship and ask before taking photographs around the shrine.

Beneath the wooded hills

The spring also poses a geological question: how does heated water reach the surface here?

In a 2026 study, Ananya Parthapradip Mukherjee and Animesh Mandal examined gravity and magnetic measurements collected around Taptapani in December 2022 and December 2023–January 2024. Such measurements help researchers investigate rocks below ground. Their model suggests that boundaries and breaks in the rock help thermal water move towards the spring. The authors present this as a preliminary interpretation that needs further investigation.3

That work adds another way of looking at the hills. The forested surface is the part a visitor sees; the spring offers a glimpse of processes beneath it.

Planning a visit

Berhampur is the rail gateway. Ganjam district places Taptapani about 50 kilometres away by road, making the city a useful starting point for the journey.1

Odisha Tourism recommends October to March for a Brahmapur-based trip and includes Taptapani among the region’s excursions.4 Check the forecast and confirm the final approach and return transport before travelling. If bathing is part of your plan, ask directly about the arrangements available at the time. Carry water and take litter back with you.
