Margasira · osha / household worship

Manabasa Gurubara

A measure of grain becomes a way to think about sustenance, dignity and who may share a meal.

A narrative research checkpoint

The story as told

Lakshmi and the rejected devotee

Balabhadra Ghadai retells the Lakshmi Purana associated with Balaram Das: Lakshmi honours Sriya, a devotee identified in the narrative as Chandala. Balabhadra objects to the visit, and Jagannath excludes Lakshmi. The conflict centres on caste and the goddess’s freedom to recognise devotion. This is the text’s story, not evidence that discrimination subsequently disappeared.1

A reading of its meaning

Hunger changes the argument

The Mohantys’ 2021 essay describes the brothers’ hardship after Lakshmi leaves and reads the work as a challenge to caste barriers. Food becomes central to the dispute over authority. Our interpretation is that a household’s feeding labour is made visible through its withdrawal: those who dismiss its source discover their dependence on it.2

The story as told

The meal and the recognition

In Ghadai’s retelling, the hungry brothers reach Lakshmi’s new household. Told that its mistress is Chandala, they first attempt to cook for themselves and fail. They finally accept her cooked food, recognise its familiar preparation and reunite with her. The reversal makes exclusion answerable to the experience of hunger. Ghadai himself says the story’s time of formation is not definitely known.1

A source-attributed account

The household observance

The same essay places the worship on Margasira Thursdays and identifies the mana or grain measure among its objects. Dash records khiri, khechudi, kakara and chakuli as associated foods. These are attributed examples, not a compulsory menu for every Thursday or home.23

The story as told

The measure, grain and drawn threshold

The Odia transcription describes the first Margasira Thursday through a prepared grain measure, new paddy and rice-paste designs. Areca nuts, sugarcane, radish and banana belong to its ritual arrangement. This passage records objects and preparation, not a verified household menu.4

The story as told

A separate Sudasa passage

The text then explicitly introduces Sudasa Brata: a Thursday coinciding with the bright tenth. Its ten-knotted thread and ten manda with banana, coconut and chhena follow that transition. They cannot automatically be assigned to every Manabasa Thursday; quantities and practice need edition and household checks.4

The story as told

Return on the condition of shared food

At the close, Lakshmi confronts the brothers for accepting food after condemning her as Chandala. Before returning, she demands mutual feeding across the caste hierarchy; Jagannath agrees. The passage makes shared food a condition of reconciliation within the story. It does not prove that historical discrimination ended.4

What remains to be established

What this witness cannot date

The digital page labels the work1868 without an identified imprint or scan. That label remains quarantined; the composition date and household version are unknown.4

What remains to be established

A history of text, voice and work

The next historical step is to identify dated editions and manuscripts of Lakshmi Purana and compare how they are read aloud. The author attribution alone does not date today’s menu or decorations. The collection should credit the reader, cook and person who draws the household designs, including work that a dish photograph would leave invisible.

Reading before a visit

A visitor’s starting point

Learn the meaning of the grain measure and ask a willing cook about one preparation. A shop selling chakuli does not provide access to a household’s Manabasa worship. A future tasting record needs its own date, provider and consent.
