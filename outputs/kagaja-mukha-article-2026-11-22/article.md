# Kagaja Mukha and the Paper Masks of Raghurajpur

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

A paper mask need not be made to wear. In Sakshi Gambhir’s documentation of Raghurajpur masks, Srikhetra Maharana and Kumudini Maharana make non-theatrical objects without openings for the eyes or nose.[^1] The face is meant to be looked at; it does not need a wearer looking out through it.

## Giving paper a shape

Gambhir describes soaked paper, waste cloth and natural fibres beaten into pulp and bound with tamarind-seed gum. The mixture is shaped over a die or sample mask and dried in the sun. Her account also describes cow-dung reinforcement over a paper body, with features shaped while wet.[^1] Paper and this added layer can belong to the same object.

A separate NID account by Bibhudutta Baral, Divyadarshan C. S. and Aakash Sharma describes newspaper strips adhered around a mould. Drying, a white coating and smoothing prepare the mask for colour.[^2] Here paper remains in layers as it takes the mould’s form. The painted surface comes after the work that gives it a body.

## Makers who demonstrate the work

Lalit Kala Akademi’s 2016–17 annual report records Sulachana Maharana and Pratima Maharana, both from Raghurajpur, demonstrating papier-mâché techniques to village women and schoolchildren on 25–26 July 2016. Its Bhubaneswar Regional Centre organised the workshop at Pratisruti in Padanpur, via Jatni, Khordha.[^3]

A finished mask invites attention to its painted face. Looking at its shape and how its body was made brings the work before the brush into view.
