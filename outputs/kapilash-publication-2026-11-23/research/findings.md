## A1 — original inscription study: an older temple remembered in stone

Proposed source ID: `kapilas-sircar-inscriptions-ei33`.

D. C. Sircar, **“Kapilas Inscriptions of Narasimhadeva”**, *Epigraphia Indica*, vol. XXXIII, article 5, printed pp. 41–45. [Full facsimile PDF](https://library.bjp.org/jspui/bitstream/123456789/1910/24/epigraphia-indica-vol-33.pdf).

Root downloaded the 28,010,561-byte original facsimile to `/private/tmp/kapilash-epigraphia-indica-vol-33.pdf`. The web tool rejected the large PDF; its scan has no useful extractable text. I rendered and visually read **printed pp. 41–43**, corresponding to **PDF pages 79, 80 and 83** (one-based). Intervening pages 81–82 are plates. The remaining pages were located but are not claimed as fully read. An earlier JainQQ OCR copy helped locate the article, but final claims below were checked against the original page images.

At printed p.41 Sircar describes examining three inscriptions on a detached stone *kalasa*, a vase-shaped architectural piece, during his November 1957 visit. He inferred that it had crowned an older temple, subsequently replaced on the same site. At p.42 he discusses the first inscription's construction record and calendar details: they fit 30 April 1246, making Narasimhadeva I a possible identification, though he expressly retains uncertainty. His comparison of the second inscription at p.43 supports that identification.

**Drafting implication:** attribute this reading to Sircar. Distinguish an earlier building and its inscribed remnant from the standing temple. Do not write that every surviving structure dates to 1246, or that the king's identity is unqualified. Do not promise the detached object is still displayed or publicly reachable. No independent inscription decipherment is claimed. Use 1246 if useful; the exact day adds little for a visitor.

A compact narrative can explain the object first, then what it records and why the historian's qualified dating matters. This supplies substantive history without turning the article into a catalogue.
