Mahavinayak: A Hill Shrine and Five Forms of Worship

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Mahavinayak is a place of pilgrimage in the hills near Chandikhole, in Jajpur district. Its temple belongs to a landscape of wooded slopes and neighbouring shrines, with a religious identity that brings several forms of worship together.12

The hill setting

Mahavinayaka is also a name in the region’s geography. The Archaeological Survey of India identifies it alongside Alti, or Nalti, and Assia as three hill ranges around Jajpur. The name therefore refers to more than the temple alone: it belongs to the surrounding hill country.3

Five deities, one stone

The distinctive object of worship is a single sacred stone associated with Shiva, Durga, Vishnu, Ganesha and Surya. In an essay first published in 1969, Gouri Kumar Brahma described portions of the monolith as assigned to each deity. He presented this shared worship as an example of different religious traditions finding expression together. His account gives the temple a significance beyond its association with any one deity.4

The pilgrim guide compiled by R. K. Periwal also describes the stone through five divine forms. Its 1995 edition distinguishes Mahavinayak from the nearby Chandi temple, treating them as separate places around Chandikhole. The guide records a stream flowing from the hill beside Mahavinayak, linking the shrine’s setting with water as well as stone.2

Planning around Chandikhole

The two temple names are worth keeping distinct when arranging a visit. Mahavinayak and Chandi are neighbouring shrines, rather than interchangeable names for the same stop.2

For an overnight stay, Wildlife Odisha lists the Parbat Sova eco complex at Mahavinayak. Its official EcoTour destination page is a starting point for checking accommodation. Check availability directly; the listing alone does not confirm a room for a particular date.5
