Mahendragiri: Mountain Shrines and Forest Life

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Mahendragiri rises to 1,501 metres in the Eastern Ghats, in Rayagada block of Gajapati district.1 Its mountain shrines and varied forests offer two ways into the same landscape: through places of pilgrimage, and through the habitats that give the hills their richness.

The mountain’s shrines

Bhima, Kunti and Yudhisthira temples at Mahendragiri appear in the Archaeological Survey of India’s list of centrally protected monuments.2 These names give a visitor a starting point for exploring the mountain’s heritage. Allow time to look closely at the structures, while respecting worship and any instructions at the site.

Odisha Tourism also associates the mountain with Parashurama.3 These are places of religious memory as well as archaeological interest.

Forests worth looking at closely

On 25 November 2022, Odisha declared 4,250 hectares across the Idongiri and Mahendra Reserve Forests a Biodiversity Heritage Site, the state’s second.4 The designation draws attention to a landscape with several kinds of habitat. The National Biodiversity Authority describes semi-evergreen forest alongside moist and dry deciduous forest.5

One small orchid makes that botanical interest tangible. In a 2015 paper, P. K. Dash and colleagues documented Cheirostylis parvifolia from Mahendragiri, extending its recorded range into the Eastern Ghats. Their specimen had been collected in December 2013. The paper describes shaded, humus-rich places, wet rocks and fallen moist trunks as its habitat in evergreen and semi-evergreen forest.6

It is a reason to value the smaller, shaded parts of the forest as well as the broad mountain views. Leave plants where they grow; the published record is no promise that a visitor will find this orchid.

Planning a visit

Odisha Tourism recommends October to February and describes the road approach through Paralakhemundi.3 Its April 2026 festival guide identifies Koinpur as the base village, with Palasa and Paralakhemundi as rail options.7

Confirm the final approach, any permissions and return transport shortly before travelling. Agree which places you intend to visit and how much walking is involved. Carry water, take litter back and leave time for the landscape.
