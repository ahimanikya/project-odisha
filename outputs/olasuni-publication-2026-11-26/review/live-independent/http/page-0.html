Olasuni: A Hill, a Saint and a Living Fair

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Olasuni belongs to the hill landscape around Lalitagiri, where separate ridges rise above low-lying, fertile plains. The Archaeological Survey of India describes these hills as outlying spurs of the Assia range, itself connected to the Eastern Ghats.1 Odisha’s Tourism Department includes Olasuni–Gangi Hill among Jajpur’s religious and scenic destinations.2

A hill remembered through a saint

Olasuni is associated with Arakshita Das, the nineteenth-century saint whose memorial is noted in the ASI report.1 Worship at his samadhi gives the hill its continuing religious significance. The Olasuni Gumpha Prachara Prasara Committee identifies Gumpha Ekadasi, also called Olasuni Yatra, as the observance commemorating him.3

The committee describes people sharing prasada across caste divisions and presents this gathering as an expression of human fellowship. Podapitha, khechudi and dahipakhal are among the offerings it records.3

Worship and working hands

The fair also brings the surrounding craft communities into the hill’s story. Reporting in February 2025, Ashis Senapati described stone-carvers selling grinders, household objects and carved figures at Olasuni mela. Brass craftspersons from nearby villages also attended. His account brings together visitors worshipping at the samadhi and people buying objects for everyday use: the gathering has a place in local working life as well as devotion.4

Planning a visit

EcoTour Odisha lists a nature camp on Olasuni Hill near the ashram. Its official destination page is a starting point for checking accommodation; availability needs a separate enquiry.5
