Saptasajya: A Shrine Among Wooded Hills

By Ahimanikya Satapathy

Saptasajya rises south of Dhenkanal town, where wooded hills surround a place of worship. The Raghunath shrine honours Rama, Lakshmana and Sita, with a spring nearby.1

Pilgrimage and legend

The district account attributes the shrine to Rani Ratnaprava Devi and describes Rama Navami as a time of pilgrimage. It also records the legend that the Pandavas stayed here during their incognito exile.1

Trees across the slopes

In a study published in 2019, Sudam Charan Sahu and colleagues examined Saptasajya’s tropical moist deciduous forest. Their fieldwork, conducted from December 2016 to March 2017, recorded 48 tree species in plots covering a total of 0.3 hectares across three elevation bands. Sal and mango were among the trees recorded. Sal was especially prominent in the upper sample, while mango was prominent in the lower two.2

Planning a visit

Dhenkanal is the road and rail approach; Bhubaneswar is the airport gateway. The district’s Odia visitor guidance recommends October–March. Check current shrine access and onward transport before setting out.1
