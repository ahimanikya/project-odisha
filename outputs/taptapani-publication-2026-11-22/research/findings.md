### G — `taptapani-geology-mukherjee-mandal-2026`

https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40517-026-00385-6

https://doi.org/10.1186/s40517-026-00385-6

Ananya Parthapradip Mukherjee and Animesh Mandal; Geothermal Energy 14, article 18 (2026). Published 29 April; version of record 12 June 2026. Original full HTML read selectively: Abstract; Introduction’s location paragraphs; Geological setting; Data and methodology; concluding limitations and Conclusions (web lines 39–43, 75–107, 111–136, 250–259).

Supports Ganjam/Eastern Ghats identity, dense vegetation and metamorphic rock setting. Authors collected gravity/magnetic observations in December 2022 and December 2023–January 2024. Their model suggests underground rock boundaries/discontinuities help thermal water travel toward the spring. This is a preliminary geophysical interpretation requiring further investigation, not a directly traced water journey or settled complete explanation of the heat source.

Useful public angle: the spring draws attention to the ground beneath the wooded hills; describe what the two researchers investigated in plain language and retain “suggests” or equivalent. Omit numerical reservoir depths, temperatures, discharge rates and chemical classifications; several are citations to earlier studies rather than new measurements here. The 104–913-metre elevation range belongs to the broad survey area, not the spring elevation. Do not call this a volcanic spring. No clinical evidence in this paper.

Footnote can use a short descriptive linked label such as “Taptapani gravity and magnetic study”, author initials, journal and DOI. Avoid copying the very long full title, both for readability and the quotation ceiling.
