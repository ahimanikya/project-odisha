# Odia Dhaga · ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଢଗଢମାଳି

A source-led repository of Odia sayings: what the words picture, what people mean by them, and what the evidence actually establishes.

Open **[index.html](index.html)** in a browser. Search in Odia or English, filter by theme, read the original wording and explanations, and follow each witness to its source. The collection works offline; source links require internet. The portable research data is **[collection.json](collection.json)**.

The current collection contains **26 explained sayings, 1 heritage illustration, 1 explanatory diagram and 13 source records**. It is a bounded seed, not a complete anthology. Three sayings have located nineteenth-century versions; provisional historical readings are labelled. The remaining entries are grounded in identified contemporary digital witnesses while historical comparison remains open. Odia explanations are editorial drafts, not a substitute for an experienced human language editor.

## What we found

**An actual 1893 collection survives online.** The title page of [ଢଗମାଳିକା, ପ୍ରଥମଭାଗ](https://archive.org/details/dhagamalika-1893) identifies Gobinda Rath as publisher and Victoria Press, Cuttack,1893. Its 19-page scan is short enough for a complete future visual transcription. Entries are arranged under initial-letter headings. Printed page 2/PDF 3 contains a version of the lemon saying used in entry POD-DHAGA-001. That establishes an attestation by 1893; it does not identify the person who first said it. Old typography and poor OCR make unreviewed machine transcription unsuitable.

**The 1926 volume requires joint-credit care.** [ଓଡ଼ିଆ ଲୌକିକ ପଦ ବା ଢଗଢମାଳୀ ବଚନ,ଭାଗ୧](https://archive.org/details/dhagadhamali-bachana-v01-1926) has a 259-page scan. Both Gopal Chandra Praharaj and Pitambari Debi appear on the title page. Their respective roles and the relevant rights history need further investigation. A 1990 reprint is a distinct edition. This release has inspected opening pages, not read or transcribed the whole volume.

**A bibliography supplies a wider search map.** Raymond Doctor’s [2004 bibliography](https://naklada.ffos.hr/casopisi/index.php/proverbium/article/download/588/380/1682), Oriya section, printed 52–54, points to collections associated with Kapileshwar Vidyabhushana(1875), Bamana Dwibedi(1898), Apanna Panda(1923) and Mohan Chandra Das(1939). These are catalogue leads, not verified edition histories. The Panda date conflicts with a later account; retain the disagreement until title pages are compared.

**There is existing digital work to acknowledge.** [Prateek Pattanaik’s 2017 announcement](https://medium.com/prateek-pattanaik/crowdsourcing-odia-proverbs-dhagadhamali-1cac38c39dbc) describes a searchable, downloadable, contributable Airtable collection. Its current contents, size and reuse licence have not been verified here. This repository does not claim to be the first such effort or import that database without checking provenance.

**Openly licensed wording is available now.** The two Wikimedia Incubator pages linked in each entry supply identifiable contemporary witnesses. Their open licence makes an attributed seed possible, but their community-edited status does not establish antiquity, village distribution or exclusive Odia origin. We preserve their wording, including spelling and punctuation, and clearly mark our added interpretation.

## What belongs in the collection?

“Dhaga” is a useful starting term, but the repository should distinguish rhymed maxims, proverbial sentences, idiomatic phrases, riddles and attributed verses. The [Wiktionary entry](https://or.wiktionary.org/w/index.php?title=ଢଗ_ଢମାଳି&oldid=72721) offers a distinction involving rhyme and satire; that is one dictionary’s account, not a rule that erases regional usage. The collection labels three entries as idiomatic sayings and does not silently turn every short expression into the same genre.

The entries illustrate several ways sayings work: a useful act carried too far; a burden teaching skill; household advice contrasted with actual conduct; a crop’s lead time exposing late preparation; a small disagreement magnified into an epic conflict. Explaining the image is different from inventing an origin story. A wedding image does not prove a universal Odia wedding menu. A weather image does not become a meteorological law.

## Publicly available is not necessarily public domain

The source catalogue separates **access**, **reuse rights** and **evidential limits**:

- **Licensed contemporary text:** the selected Wikimedia material carries CC BY-SA 4.0. This permits reuse under conditions; it is not a public-domain declaration.
- **Historical scans:** the 1893 and 1926 files are accessible, but the inspected Internet Archive metadata did not provide a rights declaration. We link them and record limited findings rather than republishing their facsimiles. Author identity, joint credit, edition and jurisdiction need separate consideration before a public-domain certification.
- **Institutional reference:** the [DSAL Praharaj dictionary](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/praharaj/) links a CC BY-NC-ND 2.0 licence. Its digital edition is not treated as unrestricted data. Historical underlying text and the digital resource must not be casually conflated.
- **Other public access:** Odia Bibhaba’s indexed notice acknowledges that some books remain copyrighted. A modern anthology, blog, PDF or retailer listing is an access or discovery lead, not permission to copy it wholesale.

No scan has been bundled into this release. This is a research status report, not a blanket legal determination for every country.

## Entry method

Each entry retains the exact source wording, a literal English rendering, an Odia meaning, an extended explanation, a newly composed usage example, cultural context, source locators, origin limits and reuse attribution. Empty `variants` means no variant has been independently documented here, not that none exists. No invented transliteration or audio pronunciation has been added.

The source witness answers “where did we read this wording?” The explanation answers “how does this image convey its meaning?” Historical attestation, interpretation and regional usage must be assessed separately. Explanations and examples are editorial additions; they are not quotations from unnamed elders.

The lemon illustration is an original AI-generated, Pattachitra-inspired interpretation approved by the Founder. The eggplant diagram explains preparation and lead time. Neither is an archival image or a reconstruction of a particular ceremony. The earlier lemon diagram remains in the assets as a retained alternative. See artwork-provenance.json for the illustration record.

## Expansion work, in order

1. Transcribe the 19-page 1893 scan visually, keeping page/line locations and uncertain glyphs. Obtain a second human Odia proofreading pass before declaring a reliable diplomatic edition.
2. Investigate creator roles and rights for each historical edition; record the actual evidence rather than inferring public-domain status from an old publication date alone.
3. Read the 1926 preface and collection systematically. Link matching sayings by identity while preserving edition-specific forms. Do not merge duplicates solely because an English translation sounds similar.
4. Inspect the Abdul Aziz 1927, Jagannath Singh and 1949 catalogue leads in the source panel. Verify title pages, edition statements and rights before extraction.
5. Investigate the existing 2017 database’s present accessibility and licence; acknowledge its contributors and avoid duplicate transcription work where lawful reuse is possible.
6. Add region and dialect evidence from named publications or consented field records. Sambalpuri/Kosali and other regional forms need their own witnessed wording, not silent standard-Odia rewriting.
7. Record difficult historical material, including caste, gender or disability stereotypes, with context and careful access design. A prejudicial proverb is evidence of an expression, never evidence that its target group has the alleged trait.
8. Add pronunciation only from verified, licensed recordings or clearly labelled new readings. Add images only when they explain a specific metaphor.

No outreach or interviews have been conducted. Village, district, speaker, recording date and permission fields should remain absent or explicitly unknown until evidence exists.
