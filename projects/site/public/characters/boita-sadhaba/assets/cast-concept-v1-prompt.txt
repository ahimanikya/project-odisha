Use case: illustration-story.
Asset type: first cast concept for an original Odia children's heritage story, landscape 3:2.
Primary request: Make the setting and people unmistakably connected to Odisha's Boita and Sadhaba maritime heritage through their work, vessel, dress and drawing language. Show an original fictional Sadhaba merchant family and boatman preparing trade cloth at a preindustrial coastal Odisha river landing. It is a thoughtfully researched story interpretation, not a claimed reconstruction of one century.
Composition: a complete narrative scene, four clearly different full figures in foreground, large recognizable wooden BOITA just behind them, river and a low palm-thatched warehouse beyond. The boita has a substantial curved wooden plank hull, raised curving ends, a simple central woven shelter, a single wooden mast with a pale cloth sail loosely furled, rigging ropes and an oar for steering. Modest working merchant vessel, no European galleon, no Viking prow, no pirate imagery, no cannon. Include a subtle carved water-bird shape at the bow, not a giant swan or dragon.
Cast and acting:
1. MALATI, girl 12, a quick attentive face, single long black braid, rust-red calf-length wrap skirt with a cream triangular woven border, cream short-sleeve blouse and narrow ochre shoulder cloth. She is comparing the visible cloth bundles while pointing out one overlooked detail, not posing at the viewer.
2. HEMA, mother 40, confident textile trader, broad face and low bun, indigo cotton sari with restrained brick-red and cream geometric border, plain brick-red blouse, a small pair of earrings and two simple bangles. She kneels or sits on a low stool checking a folded cloth against a palm-leaf tally strip, listening to Malati.
3. RAGHU SADHABA, father 44, upright, long face, small black moustache, hair tied simply back, cream bordered dhoti and a deep red uttariya draped securely over one shoulder and across his chest; no crown or lavish jewellery. He holds a small tied trade bundle and turns to listen to them.
4. HARI NAURI, boatman 50, lean and wiry, short salt-and-pepper hair, distinct rounded nose, short indigo working dhoti and cream-red checked gamucha tied at the waist, plain cloth over a shoulder. He coils a rope at the gangplank. Do not turn him into a comic servant.
Physical objects: cloth bundles wrapped and tied with fibre cord, reed baskets, palm-leaf tally, plain brass water pot. At one side a small brass send-off tray holds betel leaves, areca nuts, rice and flowers. No English or Odia text painted into the artwork.
Art direction: warm flat 2D picture-book illustration shaped by Odisha narrative painting: firm ink contours, expressive almond-shaped eyes of believable size, elegant simple hands, side-facing gestures, ochre, brick red, indigo, leaf green and warm cream. Light visible paper texture, minimal flat shadows. Human proportions and varied ages. Not glossy animation, not Disney-style huge-eyed cute toddlers, not generic stock village family, not airbrushed, not 3D. Let the flattened river waves and a restrained floral border echo Odia pata painting without copying a particular artist or claiming traditional handmade provenance. No lettering, labels, title, signature or watermark.
