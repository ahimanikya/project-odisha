Use case: illustration-story.
Asset type: first pencil-style character development board for an original Odia children's story duo. This is exploratory character art, not a polished cover or a full model sheet.
Create one wide 3:2 graphite pencil-style illustration on plain warm ivory paper. Confident expressive cartoon construction lines, some light searching lines, simple dark eyes, clean gestures, sparse graphite shading, no photographic rendering. The feeling should be lively and affectionate, suited to a readable Indian children's magazine. All people and designs are new fictional creations.
Two main full-body figures occupy the upper two-thirds, with generous space around their silhouettes. Benu is an eleven-year-old Indian boy with a compact wiry build, a rounded triangular face, short side-swept black hair with a small tuft, expressive eyebrows and a simple small nose. He wears an everyday loose short-sleeved shirt, knee-length shorts and sandals. His pocket has a small notebook. Give him a curious questioning expression and one hand lifted naturally, with a single index finger raised. He is not smirking and has no spectacles.
Beside him is Madhab Kaka, his fifty-two-year-old paternal uncle: noticeably taller, long-limbed, upright, lively and approachable. Broad friendly nose, short salt-and-pepper hair, small neat moustache, no beard. He wears an ordinary rolled-sleeve cotton kurta, simply wrapped dhoti and practical sandals. He has a cloth book bag over one shoulder and holds a folded umbrella lightly at his side. He is listening to the boy, slightly surprised and amused. No ritual markings, priest costume, crown, turban or ornate jewellery. The boy's head should reach approximately the adult's upper chest; realistic child/adult scale within simple cartoon proportions.
Across the lower third, draw exactly three small interaction vignettes using the SAME two designs and clothes: first, Benu has another question while Kaka pauses in mid-story; second, Kaka scratches his head and admits uncertainty while Benu listens with interest; third, both sit comfortably on a low plain bench and look towards somebody outside the frame, listening together. Do not draw that third person. These are body-language studies with no written text and no speech balloons. Repeat the characters faithfully across the vignettes.
Keep each vignette separate with whitespace, not heavy comic borders. Full feet and hands visible where the pose allows. Natural hands, clear limb attachments and uncomplicated drapery. No scenery beyond the single bench in the final vignette. No colour, labels, title, lettering, watermark, fake artist signature, sketchbook spiral or photographic desk props. Do not imitate a particular existing character or named artist. Present the whole board with comfortable margins and no cropping.
