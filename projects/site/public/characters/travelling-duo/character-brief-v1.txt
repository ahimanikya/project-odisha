BATARE KATHA / ବାଟରେ କଥା
Stories Along the Way — working series title
Original travelling duo | Character brief v1 | 17 October 2026

THE PROMISE
A boy who always has another question travels with his storytelling uncle.
Every short journey supplies an encounter, a disagreement and a story that
may change how both of them see the situation. The pleasure is in their
conversation: neither person has the last word every time.

SETTING
A fictional contemporary Odisha town and the neighbouring villages visited
for weekend reading sessions, family errands and short holiday outings.
Courtyards, local buses, repair shops, markets and shaded resting places give
the pair ordinary reasons to meet people. The exact district is still open;
specific architecture or craft references will be sourced before detailed art.
The relationship is family companionship. Both have homes and daily routines.

1. BENU / ବେଣୁ — BOY, 11
Role: the questioner; Madhab's nephew.
Wants: to be included in the grown-ups' conversation and trusted to find an
answer himself.
Strength: notices contradictions between an explanation and what is actually
in front of him. Remembers a small detail that everyone else has skipped.
Flaw: wants the answer so quickly that he interrupts the person who could
help. Sometimes enjoys catching somebody out more than understanding them.
Vulnerability: hides embarrassment behind one more question.
Growth: learns to ask with care, listen to the whole answer and accept that
some questions need time. His curiosity stays intact.
Humour: takes a sweeping statement literally, then discovers that his own
neat explanation has an equally inconvenient exception.
Habit: starts a question with one raised finger, then raises another because
he has thought of a second question before the first has been answered.
Voice: quick, direct and specific. He can apologise without losing his spark.
Visual proposal: compact, wiry silhouette; rounded triangular face; short
side-swept hair with a small stubborn tuft; expressive eyebrows; simple loose
shirt, knee-length shorts and sandals. A small notebook fits in his pocket.
Future palette proposal: leaf green and warm brown, with a small ochre accent.
Avoid: making every expression a knowing smirk; glasses and red kurta would
also make him too close to the existing listener design.

2. MADHAB KAKA / ମାଧବ କକା — MAN, 52
Role: storyteller, former library assistant, and Benu's father's younger
brother. His occupation and family relationship are creative proposals.
Wants: to keep the pleasure of shared storytelling alive and make his nephew
feel welcome in it.
Strength: remembers people, listens kindly and can make a crowded courtyard
fall quiet at just the right moment in a story.
Flaw: reaches for a familiar story before checking whether it fits the real
situation. Occasionally pretends he remembers an ending better than he does.
Vulnerability: worries that an admission of uncertainty will disappoint his
listeners; Benu helps him discover that it can invite them in.
Growth: becomes comfortable saying, "I don't know yet. Let's find out."
Humour: theatrical pauses, a dry reply, and the moment his own grand gesture
is interrupted by an ordinary inconvenience.
Habit: taps a folded umbrella lightly against his sandal while considering
an answer; he never uses it to threaten or discipline the child.
Voice: warm, measured and playful, capable of a short unadorned admission.
Visual proposal: tall, long-limbed and upright; broad friendly nose; short
salt-and-pepper hair and a small moustache. Rolled-sleeve cotton kurta,
simply wrapped dhoti and practical sandals. Folded umbrella and cloth book bag.
Future palette proposal: undyed cream, dusty indigo and muted brick red.
Avoid: a guru costume, a ritual identity inferred from clothing, an all-knowing
expression or a frail stoop. His clothes are proposed everyday choices.

THE RELATIONSHIP
Benu loves the way Kaka can make people listen; Kaka enjoys being surprised
by Benu's eye for detail. Their friction is affectionate and specific.
Sometimes the child supplies evidence; sometimes the adult supplies context;
sometimes a person they meet knows more than either. Let women and other
children be sources of knowledge in the wider world as stories develop.

REPEATABLE STORY SHAPE
Ordinary encounter → Benu notices something odd → Kaka offers a story or
explanation → somebody tests or questions it → they revise their understanding
→ a small final joke returns us to the journey. Vary this shape when needed.
Do not end every episode with a moral spoken directly to the reader.

THREE NEW STORY POSSIBILITIES
A. The Two Endings / ଦୁଇଟି ଶେଷ — proposed first pilot.
At a courtyard reading, the final page of a borrowed storybook is missing.
Kaka begins to improvise until Benu asks whether he actually remembers it.
His honest answer invites the listeners to suggest different endings. They
record these as their own versions and plan to ask the lender about the book.
The pleasure is shared invention, without presenting a guess as a fact.

B. The Important Seat — title to be developed in Odia with the script.
An empty chair is reserved for an important visitor at a reading. Benu's
questions expose several different assumptions about who that visitor is.
The eventual arrangement gives the person who needs the seat a useful view,
while Kaka discovers that the audience has already started telling stories.
Develop the ending through character action, not a lecture about status.

C. The Question He Forgot — title to be developed in Odia with the script.
Benu asks a basket maker so many questions about the work that he never asks
her name. Later he cannot describe whom they should thank. Meeting her again
gives him a chance to listen to her story as well as inspect her baskets.
Research the actual craft before drawing a specific technique or material.

PILOT OUTLINE: THE TWO ENDINGS — SIX BEATS, NOT A FINAL SCRIPT
1. Kaka and Benu arrive for a small courtyard reading with a borrowed book.
2. The reader turns a page and discovers that the final leaf is missing.
3. Kaka offers a possible ending; Benu asks whether it is really in the book.
4. Kaka admits he cannot remember. Two listeners propose different endings.
5. Benu writes down the new versions; they will ask the lender about the
   original. The inner story will also be newly written for this project.
6. They leave with extra pages. Kaka says the story has grown; Benu points out
   that so has the question list. Keep the final line light when scripting.

FIRST VISUAL STUDY
Show the two full figures side by side, then small studies of Benu interrupting,
Kaka pausing to reconsider, and both listening to somebody outside the frame.
Focus on silhouette, age, hands and their eye line. Keep text outside the art.
This is a pencil-style digital concept stage, not a claim of a physical sketch.
