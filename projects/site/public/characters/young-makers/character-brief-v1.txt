CHHOTA KARIGARA / ଛୋଟ କାରିଗର
The Young Makers — working series title
Original workshop cast | Character brief v1 | 17 October 2026

THE PROMISE
Two children and an artisan uncle turn everyday problems into small making
adventures. A clever idea becomes useful through measurement, mistakes,
testing and another person's help. The child can see something the adult has
missed, and the adult can supply experience the child has not yet gained.

SETTING
A fictional contemporary settlement in the Konark area of coastal Odisha.
The home workshop faces a shared courtyard; the children also encounter
local repairs, school projects and model-making. Exact village, architecture,
craft practice and dress references will be researched for detailed artwork.
This is not a reconstruction of the temple's construction or a biography of
Dharmapada. Stone-carving references belong to the adult's work; the first
child-led projects use cardboard, clay and simple tabletop models.

1. TARA / ତାରା — GIRL, 12
Role: the young maker; Dinu's niece and Kanu's friend.
Wants: to make something useful enough for someone else to depend on.
Strength: thinks in shapes, notices alignment and can explain an idea with a
quick drawing or model.
Flaw: becomes attached to a beautiful first version and skips the dull test
that could reveal its weakness.
Vulnerability: mistakes a failed prototype for evidence that she has no talent.
Growth: learns that showing an unfinished attempt is part of working well.
Humour: has an exact drawing for everything except where she left the pencil.
Habit: turns an object slowly in her hands before deciding what is wrong.
Voice: precise when describing shapes, impatient when defending an idea,
warmly excited when somebody notices an improvement she has made.
Visual proposal: tall, angular child silhouette; short practical bob tucked
behind one ear; plain kurta, straight trousers and sandals. Soft cloth apron
at the worktable only. The apron and drawing board create a recognisable pose.
Future palette proposal: muted mustard, charcoal and clay red.
Avoid: magical genius who never practises, miniature royal costume, or a
costume presented as a verified historical artisan's garment.

2. DINU MAMU / ଦିନୁ ମାମୁ — MAN, 39
Role: Tara's mother's younger brother; a sculptor with a small workshop who
also enjoys making simple models. This occupation and family are invented.
Wants: to do dependable work and help the children enjoy the patience behind it.
Strength: understands materials, spots where a test went wrong and can turn
a complicated process into a manageable demonstration.
Flaw: sometimes solves the problem for the children before they can try.
Vulnerability: wants to protect them from disappointment because he remembers
his own difficult early attempts.
Growth: learns when to offer a question, when to wait and when to step in.
Humour: gives a long explanation and then finds that Kanu has demonstrated
the same point with a bottle cap and two scraps of paper.
Habit: checks a surface with his palm, then looks again from another angle.
Voice: practical and patient, with room for surprise and self-correction.
Visual proposal: sturdy, grounded silhouette; wavy short hair and a modest
moustache; cotton work shirt, loose trousers, sandals and a cloth at his waist.
Future palette proposal: faded blue, stone grey and warm cream.
Avoid: making him a sacred authority or only a dispenser of advice. His own
work, deadlines and mistakes remain visible.

3. KANU / କାନୁ — BOY, 10
Role: neighbour and friend; the tester who asks what happens in actual use.
Wants: a useful part in the project, even when drawing is not his strength.
Strength: notices wobble, weight, reach and other practical details. Can
repeat a test in the same way and remember what changed.
Flaw: calls an entire idea useless after its first spectacular failure.
Vulnerability: worries that the maker gets the credit while the tester is
only the person who is allowed to break things.
Growth: learns to describe a failure precisely and recognise what already works.
Humour: supplies an unexpected but practical test that punctures a grand claim.
Habit: crouches to bring his eyes level with a wheel or work surface.
Voice: short, concrete observations; pleasure in a well-timed sound effect.
Visual proposal: shorter, rounder silhouette than Tara; cropped hair; small
checked shirt, rolled trousers and sandals. A paper measurement strip can
serve the pilot; do not give him a permanent heap of unrelated gadgets.
Future palette proposal: rust red and cream, with muted blue.
Avoid: comic fool, destructive sidekick or a child who exists only to praise Tara.

THE RELATIONSHIPS
Tara and Kanu: she asks how it could work; he asks whether it actually does.
Both get credit for the solution and both can revise their opinion.
Tara and Dinu: affection and shared craft meet a tension over who gets to try.
Kanu and Dinu: the adult takes practical observations seriously and sometimes
needs the younger tester to point out what everyone has walked past.

THREE NEW STORY POSSIBILITIES
A. The Wobbly Wheel / ଟଳମଳ ଚକ — proposed first pilot.
A small model cart keeps bobbing even on a flat board. Tara polishes the
wheel's edge; Kanu's slow test reveals that its axle hole is off-centre. With
Dinu's help they mark the centre of a replacement cardboard wheel and compare
the two. Their second cart runs smoothly with a small load.

B. The Roof That Sang — title to be developed in Odia with the script.
A tabletop model rattles in the breeze from a hand fan. The children disagree
about whether the sound comes from the roof or the base. Changing one piece
at a time helps them discover a loose joint and give their model a quieter roof.
Keep this a tabletop problem; children do not climb onto a real roof.

C. A Handle for Everyone — title to be developed in Odia with the script.
Tara makes an elegant handle for a light display box, but Kanu cannot hold it
comfortably. Testing different sizes with several people changes her drawing.
The successful design grows from listening to users as well as measuring.

PILOT OUTLINE: THE WOBBLY WHEEL — SIX BEATS, NOT A FINAL SCRIPT
1. Tara unveils a small handmade cart; Kanu volunteers to test it.
2. The cart bobs on a flat board. Tara assumes that the rim needs smoothing.
3. Smoothing does not help. Dinu asks them to watch one slow wheel turn.
4. Kanu notices the rim rising and falling around the axle; Tara sees the
   off-centre hole. Both contributed to the diagnosis.
5. They mark the centre on a new cardboard circle; Dinu handles the hole-making
   step. Keep the old wheel beside the new one so the difference is visible.
6. The cart carries a small load smoothly. Kanu proposes one final reasonable
   test; Tara laughs and agrees. Work out the final joke in the Odia script.

VISUAL DIRECTION
The home workshop is a place of useful activity, not a decorative temple set.
Use broad flat shapes, confident outlines and clear hands. Keep the tabletop
cart large enough to read. An eventual distant Konark reference must be
recognisable and sourced; do not use a speculative temple reconstruction as
established history. This cast should feel more grounded and geometric than
the long-limbed, conversational travelling duo.

INSPIRATION AND ORIGINAL CONTRIBUTIONS
Dharmapada's legend supplies the theme of a young person's craft and insight.
Tara, Dinu and Kanu, their contemporary relationships, workshop and new plots
are this project's creative proposals. They do not represent Dharmapada,
Bisu Maharana or an altered ending to the traditional legend. A direct
retelling would remain a separate named heritage story.
