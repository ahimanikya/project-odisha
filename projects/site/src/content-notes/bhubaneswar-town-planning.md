# How Bhubaneswar makes room for streets as it grows

*Project Odisha · 27 November 2026*

A road that ends at a plot boundary is little use to the family on the other side. A drain also needs a continuous route, whoever owns the land it crosses. Bhubaneswar’s first town-planning scheme tackles that practical problem across **746.70 acres** in parts of four mouzas, or revenue villages: **Paikarapur, Sijuput, Naragoda and Sahajpur**. The Bhubaneswar Development Authority (BDA) gives that figure as the area covered by Scheme 01/2015. [BDA’s scheme record](https://bda.gov.in/project-details/17)

## What happens to the plots?

BDA describes a process called land pooling and readjustment. Planners consider neighbouring holdings together so they can lay out connected streets and reserve land for open spaces, public services and other shared uses. They then redraw plot boundaries and allot reconstituted plots back to landowners. A street can run through several former holdings while each owner receives a plot in the new layout. [BDA’s explanation of land pooling](https://www.bda.gov.in/bda/department/planning-department)

## What the records show on the ground

On 17 July 2026, BDA listed a possession notice for plot 205 in Sahajpur and another for plot 880 in Sijuput. On 22 July it listed reminders for two plots in Paikarapur. Those notices show the authority dealing with individual plots. To establish whether a particular handover took place, a reader would need its dated completion record. [BDA’s July notices](https://bda.gov.in/notice)

## How residents can judge progress

For a family moving into the scheme area, the useful questions are local. Can an ambulance reach the house? Where does rainwater go when the street floods? Can an owner locate the boundaries of the plot they have been allotted? A dated map, a handover record and a walk along the road would each answer a different part of that enquiry.

The 746.70 acres tell us the scale of the plan. A stronger measure of delivery would show which streets and drains are working, when they entered service, and which households can use them.
